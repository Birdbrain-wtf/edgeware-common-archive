**Background**

Before we go any further, it's worth outlining why linking blockchain addresses to legal entities is worth doing. From the full [LexBridge proposal](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1qQCumpLd0VcrKstgFto7qoRgpqLcsslATByUhpZmFcs/edit#heading=h.uwkcexiru28q):

> We believe in a future where DAOS regularly transact with corporations, smart contracts interbreed with legal agreements, and NFTs save us from having to go to the Department of Motor Vehicles. 
> Mass adoption may seem inevitable... but how do we get there?
> Connecting the “real world” of bank accounts and courtrooms to the “virtual world” of DAOs and smart contracts seems necessary if we are to ever see dreams of a peer-to-peer society manifest. 
> This proposal is a bridge between the world of law and the world of code. 
> This proposal’s execution would permit any individual Edgeware user address, multisig wallet, or DAO to cryptographically associate itself to a legal entity. 
> This would permit these distributed entities to act as either an order or an asset of a legal entity.
> Individual users could store their assets in a favorable jurisdiction that recognizes crypto property. 
> Multisigs and DAO proposals would be able to open bank accounts and pay subcontractors with fiat. 
> All users would be able to issue legally binding agreements and sign them with their Polkadot extension. 
> Building the bridge between the ‘real’ and ‘virtual’ worlds would further solidify Edgeware’s strategic position. 
> Just as the EthBridge will allow entities from Ethereum to interact with Edgeware, this LexBridge will allow legal and human entities to meaningful engage. 
> The nexus of legal and distributed forces is key to the manifestation of the potentials of Web3. 

Despite blockchains being seen as panacea for every problem under the sun, we still (mostly) live in the real world - though whose reality is an argument for another time. 

As a result when we want to get stuff done, more often than not there will be some requirement for a legal entity or connection, especially if we want the old world (where we still pay taxes, lease property, own things and sign contracts for services) then we need to think about how to glue stuff together.

**Reasons for Legal Entities**
 
- They provide legal protections and assurances groups:
- limited liability 
- dispute between parties
- insurances 
- contracts
- tax liabilities 
- rights management
- asset management
- fiat / banking


The promise of the Lexbridge proposal is that anyone can launch a a DAO/LLC for ~$2 a pop, potentially bringing substantial economic activity to Edgeware. 

**NFTs**
- NFTs are a good way to represent assets, which can be owned by an individual or a DAO - so think less about the 'art' attached to the NFT and more about the 'rights' that NFT grants the creator or owner. 


- Fractionalized ownership of assets should permit wealth redistribution through smart contracts. 


- Alignment between NFT and DAO working groups to permit easy creation could similarly bring economic activity to the chain.

With Swiss DAAs there are lots of precedents that have been set there and an advantage is being close to regulators.

With Commonwealth and [@Dialectic - Ryan](/edgeware/account/jXCCBdq4Nzbv313i9VRFzm8VP4mtcEBcUsqBenh2apCR4e1) low on time, [@dillchen](/edgeware/account/hwJpK8k6Q1KR8F87u5KHfBghRRit7p5UYWC1ubPDwsjdh6s) has signalled his interest in rethinking the project with a greater focus as a community run concept.

In practice this means that the 12.5% of shares held back for Commonwealth for summoning the DAO could be shared amongst those in the community who push it forward (with maybe 1% held back for CW Labs) with all of this having vesting periods.

Rough structure:

- Operating agreement in top DAO
- Have MolochDAO contracts ready to go
- Need to agree how new members are added
- Votes at this address correspond to changes in the registry
- Vote on members and vote on shares
- Off-chain polling in every single community


**Wyoming LLCs **

Both Tristan and Frode were of the opinion that Wyoming was a good place to push forward rather than Switzerland (Frode has setup / established businesses in Wyoming for 10 years).

**Wyoming benefits**

- Small organisation there
- Chances are you can deal with people in an easier way
- More accessible, walk into secretary of state
- Wyoming allows you to list members via email addresses


**Wyoming structure**

- Wyoming, report all daughter LLCs to the state before launching.
- WyDAO has 1000 child LLCs, each named WyDao Series XXX. 


**Delaware**
- Does not require pre-registration of Series LLCs with state; making naming more flexible. 


**Use cases**

Beyond the functional aspects, we also discussed some of the potential use cases for LexBridge as a way of helping people imagine how this interoperates with Edgeware.

- Imagine an anonymous artists collective develops a new graphic novel but with all aspects turned into NFTs representing contributions.
- Let's call this "Project X"
- A wider community forms around Project X and also adds value with those contributions also being managed via NFTs. 
- Collectively all these assets and contributions form the 'rights' of "Project X".
- Now imagine the ownership was linked to a Wyoming LLC, which means Project X can also interface with the 'real world' of existing legal entities. 
- Project X becomes famous... bigger than Beeple famous and Netflix (and others) come calling because they see the value in developing this project as a bigger world.
- Now Project X's collective and community have a way of interfacing with Netflix both in terms of negotiating the terms of the deal (should we do it, shouldn't we) and also in terms of how payments from Netflix for the 'licensing' of that IP are returned.


This isn't so far fetched... and is in fact how we believe Decent's Web3 media collective will interact with established organisations. **DAOHaus**

We didn't discuss this on the call, but it is worth checking out the DAOHaus proposal and thinking around '[Creative Contributions](https://daohaus.substack.com/p/fair-launch-transmutation-proof-of)'. DAOHaus uses the MolochV2 smart contracts with a few additions. 

> the idea of transmutation is to implement a continuous function within a DAO that provides “proof of contribution.” Our HAUS token is attainable only through transmutation. This means there are only two ways to get it: buy it on the open market (someday, maybe) or contribute to DAOhaus.
> When a traditional funding proposal passes a DAO vote, funds move from the DAO bank to the receiving address. When a transmutation proposal passes, the funds move to the receiving address and also a quantity of HAUS token is sent back to the DAO bank as well.

You can again see how all of this connects back to the conversations around Decent and Ecosystem DAOs born from Edgeware.
