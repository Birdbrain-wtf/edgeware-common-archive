This post is mostly to inspire and explore the near-medium term future of how chains are developing and how we may grow in response using a multi-chain ecosystem architecture.


Currently we have 1 chain, Edgeware, and a testnet.  Ethereum maintains a few testnets for different reasons, and a mature Edgeware would likely do the same, one being the codename Hedgeware chain that would help test parachain bonding in a value-bearing way; the 'canary net' model. 

We're also seeing multi-chain architectures e.g. Binance, with a split between Binance Chain and Binance Smart Chain, where each uses the other for specific purposes.

Binance:
- Focused on solely transactions, orderbook matching, asset exchange. 


Binance Smart Chain
- More EVM, user-uploaded logic execution and contracts broadly. 


Use cases will increasingly require unique technical operations - derivatives for instance require high compute and high TPS. 

Legal and jurisdictional requirements may also increasingly hold on how chains develop, for instance, whether KYC is required, or the reverse- where we want all chain interactions to be utterly ZK private.

Additionally, we've seen a set of chains devoted to other use cases like NFTs (Flow) and Gaming (Enjin.)

So with that said, there is a potential future where there is a 'family' of Edgeware chains each purposed for specific needs, but with a trusted relationship between one another (bridges,) or Edgeware Relay Chain uniting this set. They would all use the same Token, (EDG)

That vision is also compatible with the later roadmap of the Substrate ecosystem with nested relaychains. In this vision, Edgeware's upcoming parachain slot bid may be purposed to act as testing ground in the runup to deploying subrelay functionality, which would allow us to continue using the same token for all of these- EDG

Additionally, one twist is projects like Axelar, we see attempts to 'aggregate' bridging work into a single chain, (Axelar bridging DOT, BTC, ETH, for instance) instead of many teams incentivizing and maintaining sets of bridges.
