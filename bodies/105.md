Web3 Foundation awards grants to teams with amazing projects that fit within the Polkadot ecosystem. This past August, they awarded most Wave 3 grants to projects that focus on security and identity. These are some really exciting projects, and even more exciting is the fact that you can still apply for a grant (you can reach out at grants@web3.foundation)!

I had the chance to ask some of these grant winners about what they’re working on and why they’re excited about it. Read the insights from NGRAVE, Zondax, and Geefu team members on each of their Web3 Foundation Wave Three grant projects below.

**NGRAVE ZERO hardware wallet by NGRAVE**

*What is NGRAVE working on with their Wave 3 grant?*

NGRAVE makes a fully offline wallet with which private keys are never exposed to an online device. Their Web3 grant will allow them to make a Substrate integration that gives the Polkadot ecosystem a wallet based on their technology. Xavier Hendrickx, co-founder and CTO of NGRAVE, took the time to answer some questions about the project.

Hendrickx described the NGRAVE ZERO, the wallet they are working on now, as “a fully air-gapped hardware wallet with innovative key creation and anti-tamper protection.” The term air-gapped speaks to the fact that the wallet is never connected online, nor does it need a USB to work. Instead, it uses QR codes to interact with the outside world, which Hendrickx believes is “the safest way to operate if you want to sign transactions.”

*Why are they excited about introducing this to the Polkadot ecosystem?*

In Hendrickx words, “The Polkadot ecosystem needs a wallet management solution that can compete with the best industry standards currently available for other blockchains. Without good security there are hard limitations on adoption, especially in order to get institutional support.”

Not only does NGRAVE see the importance of introducing secure wallet technology to the Polkadot ecosystem as necessary for adoption, but they also note the exciting challenge of building this sort of technology based on Substrate. As Hendrickx told us, “Substrate also deviates sometimes quite significantly on a technical level from other networks” - so figuring out a security solution is an important task.

*What does their project roadmap look like? What are some milestones?*

NGRAVE’s project roadmap for the NGRAVE ZERO wallet includes the following steps:

- “Research and development, identification of libraries, definition of exact seed derivation scheme, integration of libraries into firmware for Ristretto-compressed Ed25519.
- Implementation of production firmware code into the NGRAVE ZERO.
- Finalization of documentation on NGRAVE’s integration, and publication under MIT license, showcasing the first hardware solution with a seed derivation scheme for a wallet.”

After these steps, NGRAVE plans to focus on “fully functional integration…[including] account creation, account management, transaction signing, message signing, contract calls, and various other functions as needed” (NGRAVE).

According to Hendrickx, “The grant is only about the first part of the work: providing the right key derivation scheme & add Ed25519 support to our secure firmware. Once this is done we shall work on the actual usability, testing and inclusion in the production ready ZERO.”

**Ledger app for Polkadot by Zondax GmbH**

*What is Zondax GmbH working on with their Wave 3 grant?*

Zondax GmbH offers consulting services focused on machine learning, embedded systems and blockchain. Managing Director at Zondax, Juan Leni, took some time to tell us about the work they are doing with the Polkadot ecosystem.

Zondax has worked with Tendermint, Cosmos, QRL, Binance Chain, EOS NY, and others on Ledger Nano S and X wallets. They’ve worked on integrations for users and validators in Rust, Go, Python, and JS, and now they are working on a Ledger app for Polkadot.

*Where are they in their roadmap?*

The team has already released an early version of the Ledger app for Polkadot to the public, v0.2.0.

Leni listed a few further ideas the team is thinking about pursuing, including:

- Trusted Execution Environment (TEE)-based validator software+hardware stack
- Automatic code generation for Substrate-based Ledger apps
- SR25519 support in Ledger devices
- And more!

*What are they excited about right now?*

According to Leni, the final iteration of the Ledger app is expected to be completed soon. Zondax has also been working directly with Jaco Greeff, the primary author of Polkadot-js, on an integration that Leni believes will mean that “any project that depends on polkadot-js will have Ledger Nano S/X integration with minimal effort.”

**Vue JS components for Polkadot-js apps by Geefu**

*What is Geefu working on with their Wave 3 grant?*

Geefu is building Vue.js components that will allow for easier use of the Polkadot-js infrastructure. Matej Nemček, who is building the components, was kind enough to offer his perspective on the project.

According to Nemček, they are creating the Vue.js components to give developers the opportunity to build within the “Vue ecosystem,” which is meant to facilitate the use of Polkadot-js inside of applications.

*What is does their roadmap look like? What are some of their milestones?*

The project has already passed its first milestone, which included creating vue-identicon, vue-keyring and vue-settings.

The next two milestones focus on building a “whole dashboard with basic components like Accounts, Transfer, Staking, Democracy, Treasury, Extrinsics,” Nemček told us. They also plan to build a Vue wallet for use by any chain with voting and staking features and a mobile application interface.

Nemček hinted at an idea for “crosschain trading of votes,” but as he said, “that's subject of further research.”

*Why are they excited about the Polkadot ecosystem?*

Nemček expressed excitement about the potential for Polkadot to facilitate “easy forkless upgrades and swappable consensus” for blockchain networks based on the opportunities provided by the Substrate framework. For these networks, Nemček imagines a simple, clear onboarding process for all participants, and believes this will become the standard. According to Nemček, “We see this as a great opportunity to rethink where we failed so far in ‘blockchain’ world,” and to see what can be done about it.

*Is there anything else they’re excited about?*

There are several specific technologies Nemček showed enthusiasm about, including the Aragon Court launch, and how Reputation Curation Registries could replace Token Curated Registries (he sees huge risk here, but also advantages for innovative governance).
