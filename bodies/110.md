## [How Can We Fix Tribalism in Cryptocurrency?](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/87)

At Berlin Blockchain Week '19, Commonwealth’s Chief-of-Staff Thom Ivy sat down with Gavin Wood for a chat on governance in the blockchain space. They started off talking about the importance of community response in handling toxic tribalism. In this piece, I take you into their conversation to show how a problem like tribalism could be solved with on-chain governance. 

“If community members are willing to publicly call out those who engage in personal attacks or aggressive tribalist behaviors, it can shift the culture and create new norms of conduct...By using on-chain governance to implement these kinds of changes, the community will be able to take full autonomy of their culture.”

## [Implementing Social Key Recovery](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/89)

Social Key Recovery lets your friends save a copy of your key, so that if you lose your key, your friends can help you out. But your friends don't just get a copy - they get a piece, or “shard,” of the key, and they have to work together to use it. 

If you shared your secret with three people, then you would need two of them to confirm your identity and deliver your secret back to you. The more shards you make and the more trusted contacts you introduce, the more secure your recovery key is. We explore the implementation options and consequences in this piece.

## [EOS Vote-Buying and Power Distribution](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/90)

Since its beginning, the EOS blockchain has been plagued by critiques of its governance structure. Many concerns have emerged due to the concentration of power among top BPs. 

There is a strong sentiment that the undue influence of “whales” - those with enormous coin holdings, sometimes exchanged for votes - is creating a pool of BPs who are only interested in maintaining their economic status at the top of the BP rank, usurping BPs with talent or driven towards innovation.

## [How to Fund Dev Tools for Blockchains](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/95)

Building good, useful development tools is necessary for blockchain success, but it also requires funding - and it can be hard to ensure developers get the resources they need without a clear plan for supporting long-term innovation and development in a network. 

In thinking about how we can fund initial development tools for new protocols, like Edgeware, it’s important to consider the way that the structure of blockchain networks can be used to leverage developer talent and community-driven fundraising. 

## [Semaphore and the Potential for a Private DAO](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/96)

Semaphore is a language that was built to create smart contracts that allow for anonymous signaling and solve the problem of DAO privacy. But is privacy such a good thing? True anonymity could catalyze vote-buying attacks and other forms of collusion. 

“Any development that could lead to vote-buying in a DAO should be carefully considered, given the untested potential for this sort of organization...Some might then see the choice of true anonymity in a DAO as a choice between the freedom of privacy, and being held to standards of acting for network utility.”

## [The Implications of Dark DAOs](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/98)

Dark DAOs are typically described as anonymous vote-buying cartels. It's been argued that self-evolving systems (ie, networks that rely on on-chain governance) are uniquely susceptible to Dark DAO attacks, because votes can alter system operations in ways that facilitate the will of the attacker. But is that such a bad thing? 

If blockchains are seen as private goods then vote-buying, even by a Dark DAO, and the anonymous plutocracy shouldn’t matter. But if we view the chain as a public good, then a clear argument against plutocracy emerges. 

“Whether or not we think of the network as public or private, would allowing a Dark DAO to shift the system in its favor ever allow for the sustainable development of a network?”

## [Crowdsourcing the Public Good: Dominance Assurance Contracts](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/101)

Dominance Assurance Contracts are a product of game theory meant to incentivize everybody in a community to contribute to funding a public good (avoiding the free rider problem). It basically offers a full refund, plus a bonus, if not enough money is raised to actually create the public good. The promise of either a delivered public good or a net profit is theoretically enough to make contribution the dominant strategy for any member of the community. 

With the advent of smart contracts, experiments in implementing DACs for blockchains are bound to increase given the ease of creating a DAC smart contract. We’ve also compared different tactics for designing a DAC, as well as some alternative ways of funding a public good, including with funds that use token bonding curves and the concept of Quadratic Funding.
