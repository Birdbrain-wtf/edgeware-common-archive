### Quote of the Day 
*"Right, and how do we un-fuck ourselves?”*  - Gian Volpicelli

### Day Two at Web3 Summit: The End of the Golden Age?

Day One of Web3 Summit 2019 wrapped up with Dr. Gavin Wood ruminating on the end of the Golden Age of Blockchain.

Dr. Wood believes that the openness and collaboration that has long been celebrated in the blockchain space is coming to an end, with competitive venture capitalism slowly invading the ecosystem. This certainly set the tone for Day Two of Web3 Summit, which focused much more on a need for privacy and independent software in the face of threatening state and corporate control.

### Snowden, Assange, and the Problem of Increasing Central Powers

Nearly every talk on the main stage during Day Two of the conference referenced oppressive central authority. Talks by Edward Snowden and from whistleblower advocate Naomi Colvin on the extradition of Julian Assange may have initially seemed slightly off-topic for the conference. However, Colvin was clear in her presentation defending WikiLeaks - the US government’s ongoing fight to extradite Assange symbolizes the power of the state to crush anyone who acts against the will of those people in power. Colvin emphasized that the language used in legal materials for the case would set a precedent for the US to have power over anyone they deem even tangentially related to facilitating those disapproved acts. 

Especially in a space where freedom is so highly valued, those creating technology for a decentralized Internet should not allow themselves to be threatened simply for producing a platform that gives people more autonomy. This was Colvin’s main, haunting message to the audience: there is a need to stand up against encroaching power now, before it gets any further and truly can smother the blockchain space. 

Colvin’s description of the actions of the US government resonated with a problem clearly identified earlier in the day by Vlad Zamfir of CasperLabs. Zamfir described the difference between a rule of law, in which legal order structures disputes, and a rule of legal entities, which describes a rule of law system in which legal order has been captured by the interests of legislatures. Zamfir explored the possibility that Web3 could implement a system in which code is law, describing the demand for a protocol-as-law system as stemming from this sort of anti-capture paranoia. 

### How Can We Escape Oppression? 

While Zamfir’s talk did not situate the creation of a Web3 system of law in any sort of real-world context, it did provide a useful framework for relating talks like those given by Colvin and Snowden about oppressive central powers to potential Web3 developments. 

In a discussion between Gavin Wood, Gian Volpicelli, Harry Halpin, Richard Stallman, and Naomi Colvin, there was widespread agreement that governments and corporations with too much control are largely threatened by the technology being developed in the blockchain space. Dr. Wood went so far as stating that as long as Web3 developers have to answer to a central state or corporation, we’re all fucked - cue Volpicelli asking, "Right, how do we unfuck ourselves?" 

Dr. Wood’s response focused on avoiding the competitive future he earlier described. With economic cooperation and better opportunities for communication among those fighting for a more free web and world, a brighter future is possible. However, both Wood and Halpin agreed that this process would take place entirely separately from traditional governance structures, while Richard Stallman fervently disagreed. Stallaman was perhaps the only loud voice of the day arguing for the need to actually rely on democracy to create the technological changes we want to see in the world - because there really isn’t a way to escape the state.  

### Digital Sovereignty: Privacy for Freedom 

Responding to these concrete calls for broad infrastructure changes that would lead to less centralized power were concrete privacy technologies. David Chaum opened the day by presenting Elixxr, a response to the current incredible ability for nation-states and corporations to take and leverage their citizens’ information. Elixxr mixes messaging and payments, clearly a killer app based on the success of WeChat and Alipay. However, the key innovation in Elixxr is the lack of metadata leakage and assurance of privacy. 

The peer to peer electronic cash payment and messaging system will be based on a novel consensus algorithm, using teams of nodes for batches of messages. For governance, Chaum introduced another innovation in order to stop vote-buying: decoy voting. For each proposal, decoy ballots would be available, with only those who take the ballot aware of its decoy status. This way, anyone attempting to buy a ballot would have no way to know if the ballot is a real or decoy ballot, de-incentivizing the vote-buying act altogether. Chaum also plans for the untraceable messages to allow for the development of an off-chain dApp marketplace. 

Harry Halpin’s project NYM Technologies has the same goal that Chaum iterated - creating applications that don’t leak metadata in order to give people control over their digital identity. NYM uses the mixnet idea invented by Chaum, which sends all traffic in and out of multiple nodes. Decoy traffic will also be created to make it even less possible to uncover identities and ensure digital sovereignty. 

## The Recap. 

Day Two of Web3 Summit was about freedom, from the necessity of privacy for freedom, to finding ways to escape over-authoritarian decisions of governments and states. How to work with traditional governments, corporations, and other sources of power is a clear point of controversy, but the need for a fair distribution of power isn’t. All in all, Day Two was a very idealistic day at Web3 Summit 2019, but based on ideals with incredible power behind them.
