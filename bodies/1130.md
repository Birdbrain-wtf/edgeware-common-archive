I've been approached by people who operate traditional businesses, but who would like better ways to integrate crypto into their models- and some also seek funding for public good offerings, but simply not in the digital but the physical world instead.

If we want to pursue a more fair, open, and opportunity-filled world,  our technology should begin to have real-world consequences and integrations. To explore what a localist Edgeware might look like, I'm sharing the EDGPort concept, a kind of embassy into the local.

An EDGPort is a publically-funded brick-and-mortar business in the physical world.  You can imagine them as a set of mixed purpose locations that integrate with Edgeware organizations, offer public goods to Edgeware citizens, and serve as laboratories for the business models of the future. 

Examples of EDGPorts we may explore over the next year:

1. Community-owned and crypto-integrated community theatre model in Berlin, with a warehouse that can serve other needs and community meeting space.
2. Biohacker / Maker space in Austin TX that works on publically funded and opensource medical solutions.
3. Maker and hacker spaces in Japan. 


The EDGPort concept helps facilitate awareness, investment into local communities, capacity building, and community meetups around the world.
