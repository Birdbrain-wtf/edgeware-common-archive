So this follows a few discussions over a long period about the Edgeware community treasury which currently holds c.$20m - a not insignificant amount of money.

It is available to anyone, for practically anything which offers an incredible new opportunity to fund public goods, infrastructure and as a way to accelerate innovation. 

There are a number of ways the treasury has been tapped so far and a few ways that it may be in the future.

One challenge we may increasingly see is that getting funding from a DAO will be easier but enforcement of those terms will be harder - and there will be many messy arguments as to who bears responsibility. 

As a result it's worth outlining and discussing these things as we experiment - refining processes and standards as we go, there's likely no perfect answer or silver bullet, but we should be open to discussing the ups and downs of each route.

## 1. Grant funding 
Commonwealth Labs were funded with a 50m grant for their 2020 roadmap - you can view the post [here](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/509-edgeware-2020-roadmap-discussion).

This is where things start to get a bit messy, as the 50m grant also included the launch of BuilDAO as part of the deliverables - for various reasons this hasn't happened. 

I really don't know how we should deal with this - 

- Is the 2020 roadmap complete without BuilDAO?
- Did the community get value for its funding with the 50m paid?
- What should we as a group expect from a reporting perspective?


Right now Commonwealth Labs is incubating projects such as Picoswap and Common protocol, but is this what was meant? 

We need a way to come to agreement on this or all the debates and hand-wringing over the Agency proposal, which also exists as a large treasury grant builds on a slightly wobbly foundation. 

What is also uncertain here is whether the funding is for open source work, or for profit - it's a little in between and hence where the waters muddy. 

## 2. Open source software and projects
This is one of the primary - if not the primary uses for a Layer1 protocol DAO's treasury. 

The aim is to expand the utility of the ecosystem and its awareness and ultimately to onboard new contributors, who then drive the cycle with ever increasing impact.

So the treasury can be simply to fund OS - this requires very clear up front sign-posting and clarity over the ways funds are used.

Separate thread on the way Decred frame this use case for a protocol DAO treasury [here](https://decred.org/sustainability/). 

## 3. Sub-DAOs / permissionless subsidiaries / delegated governance
One version of this was put forward by [@dillon](/edgeware/account/iCLdev9eJBSaUSD1qnbq1roqcV6jZmfLpbLJjTdmUocixcY) (see original post [here](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/958-frmr-subdaos-permissionless-subsidaries)) and is a way for wrapped EDG to be used in contracts / protocols - ensuring EDG has a voice in the voting and value capture of a project funded by the treasury.

This is something [@Ramsey](/edgeware/account/k44nJ5yAhFz3XHGeTPuKvegJ4GD1YCSVaPc7wj4miVL6epN) is considering for New-Futures and has been a discussion point around the Decent project, though I don't think its quite right.

This model shows a clearer alignment with the treasury and the community as a whole, but there it is still experimental as an idea. 

You could also see a version of this existing via the substrate [bounties pallet](https://substrate.dev/rustdocs/v3.0.0/pallet_bounties/index.html) and the role of curators in delegating the voice of the community through a waterfall of funding.

There are some Kusama bounties you can view here and development of subBounties, which delegate things further - enabling curators to pay out when deliverables are met.

## 3. Investment DAOs
As mentioned above the roadmap proposal included BuilDAO, which was due to be an incubator for the ecosystem, existing as an investment DAO that used the Moloch smart contracts with a legal wrapper.

This would enable the treasury to effectively hold real world legal contracts / rights / equity as well as tokens. 

In this treasury funding model there is an explicit expectation that the funds deployed will return a specified % of future tokens/rights. 

It's easy to understand model and in the new version the Agency is repositioning as Edgeware Alpha - with all the value returning to the community and its members - though the final terms are still tbd. 

_Side note: _

_It also bears some resemblance to the model of a record label who advance a musican funds in return for a share of future rights, though the structure of those deals has become increasingly onerous for talent as they give away the crown jewels - their recording rights, at the very earliest stages. _

_In most record deals, advanced funds are also recouped from the artist through commercial revenues - so much like a poor deal for a founder when taking money from aggressive private equity financiers who insist on preemption rights and near impossible targets that effectively dilute team members, you may have a successful career or startup, but never actually see any money. _

## 4. Commissioning / Patronage

This is closer to where Decent's model sits and is borrowed from the public service broadcasting model.

In this approach funds are advanced to people in return for some rights or licence. 

On the treasury side, there is a way to build up original and novel on-chain assets that relate to projects and resources. 

Note these are assigned by the proposer to the treasury and sit in a time-or-deliverable based contract / escrow. 

By locking up the rights the treasury has some assurance that the proposer will deliver, as they only unlock when final milestone hit. 

In the end the creator gets what they want - the funding of the project they propose, without any meddling - unless of course they want the benefit of community contribution, which will magnify over time. 

At the end of the project they can take their rights - and do whatever they want... however, the incentive will be to leave them bonded to the community, since they can propose new work and the community can continue to make that underlying work more famous... 

On the community side they have an incentive to keep the asset assigned… to grow value around it and keep it locked up as collateral, which in turn can drive increasing value to the bottom line of the protocol. 

This approach can be meshed with an EDGpass approach, since these rights that are assigned can also enable the community to 'do more things'. 

When rights are locked up - this is essentially a way for the community to have right to utilise those same assets - as defined in the terms of the intial proposal.

In this way, we can see a path to more and more value accruing to community in the form of assigned rights, with 'real world' value. 

---------

This is just a start - but there will be many other variations.

There will also be a mesh of all of the above - but if so, then the terms will be very clearly outlined to avoid confusion.

Final thought - just come to mind is that it is generally accepted that the most healthy economies are those of 'mixed economies' - with strong public and private sectors. I imagine something similar will come to be true in this new arena, but it will be important to guage the tension between each route and ensure some balance is maintained.

Again, would love to hear other's thoughts.
