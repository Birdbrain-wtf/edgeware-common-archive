This is a repost of a comment on the [Announcing Hedgeware ](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/1153-announcing-hedgeware-a-kusama-parachain-for-edgeware)post - I felt moved to actually post a longer piece here since I really believe the current distribution makes it a terrible idea. 

I realise most will care about an airdrop of new tokens - and see this as a 'win' but it really is worth considering the implications of the economic decisions taken in Hedgeware with regard to it's initial distribution and how this changes the fundamental nature of 'Edgeware' as a truly community owned & operated project. 

--------

Edgeware's defining feature is the lockdrop distribution - it is the project's one major success and something that confers unique advantages over other projects.

It was a complete one off - a success that surprised everyone - it is very difficult, if not impossible to replicate - many others copied the lockdrop, but none came close.

The distribution was the major attraction to me over any other Substrate project - and is at the core of the reasoning I give to those who I bring to the community. 

I'm going to drop the below here again as a reminder to how this distribution compares with other projects.
![](https://s3.cointelegraph.com/uploads/2020-10/9813bfb6-57d2-4de0-85bf-6f61a0fe7e2e.jpg)

This is the suggested Hedgeware distribtution:

> 50% to holders of Edgeware on the snapshot date of May 1st UTC 00:00
> 20% to core developers, including Commonwealth Labs and future developers

Now imagine 'Hedgeware' here and ask yourself if it is indeed a canary network, or a completely different entity. 

I noted in the [Edgeware USP's post](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/1133-defining-edgewares-usps-unique-selling-points) how the project's **unfair advantage** relies on this broad and fair distribution - without it, the project has no particular defining characteristic and the benefits that accrue to this and the community fund that is a product of this balanced design. 

Hedgeware is a new chain, that leverages the name of the existing project and community, but is in effect a fork that results in a distribution that actually **degrades the unique benefits and long term potential of the project**. 

This is the part I never quite get and has been my concern all along with where there is a pretty major misalignment of our views on the project. 

This looks more to me like a desire to control the governance of a new 'Edgeware' community and return to a position where there is no community vote per-se - just community theatre.

Perhaps there is two big a gap between the values of the agency and my thoughts around Decent and that of building a truly community chain that was driven by long term thinking up front.

Hedgeware is not Edgeware - **it is not Edgeware's canary network** - it is something entirely different.

So the major question is why not replicate the lockdrop distribution and actually create a canary network?

Or do we need a canary network at all?
