# So what makes Edgeware different? 

## 1. Upgradeable and interoperable blockchain 

First things first. Edgeware is built on [Substrate](https://substrate.dev), a SDK (software development kit) that makes developing, launching, maintaining and upgrading blockchains many orders of magnitude easier than previous generations.

As a result, all Substrate chains share the same basic building blocks meaning chains can adopt the innovations of others built on the same base and also interoperate and communicate via a [Polkadot relay chain structure](https://polkadot.network/technology/).

Substrate unlocks new capabilities for developers, community builders and creators and uses proof of stake to secure the network and also reduces gas fees that have made Ethereum an expensive place to experiment. Substrate chains enable fork-less upgrades that would previously have split communities into competing tribes and also offer governance building blocks to enable more direct decision making over the direction of a community. 

Differentiation is expected to emerge from the different technical implementations, use-case focus and team cultures that will become [parachains](https://medium.com/polkadot-network/the-launch-of-parachains-78188fcf024f) in an increasingly large 'network of networks'.

## 2. Wide and fair token distribution

Edgeware's native token EDG was distributed in 2018 as part of the lockdrop, with no funding event or ICO, instead anyone holding ETH could time-lock their tokens and receive EDG in return. 

The distribution event was a huge success, making the project far more widely distributed than the vast majority of crypto-networks - see [this analysis](https://main.edgeware.wiki/edgeware-runtime/lockdrop/gini-coefficient-of-edgeware) and the following graphic. 
![](https://s3.cointelegraph.com/uploads/2020-10/9813bfb6-57d2-4de0-85bf-6f61a0fe7e2e.jpg)

This means ownership is spread far wider than any other Substrate chain - including [Kusama](https://kusama.network) and [Polkadot](https://polkadot.network), [Moonbeam](https://moonbeam.network/), [Acala](https://acala.network/) or other high profile Substrate based projects. 

> So if it was so fair, then how come there are a few groups who dominate the governance of the chain? - anon community member  - community member

Right now there are a few large holders who are active in the network and in governance which can make things feel as if power is concentrated.

However over time, as we turn passive EDG holders into active ones and more ecosystem teams are funded by the treasury, we will end up with the chain being much more evenly balanced.

In this relatively near future, no one group dominates and the network is mediated by many on-chain collectives who may have different interests but share a common set of values and vision for the project.

This is genuinely a community led blockchain… there are few that can say as much. 

It is worth caveating that In the short term this wide distribution has downsides - with no one company directing things, it means we move a little slower than a centralised chain, decisions take longer and it can feel chaotic (because it is), but in the long run, the benefits are profound and near impossible to replicate. 

## 2. Ethereum compatibility

In the Edgeware's last upgrade the project became the first Substrate chain in production to offer Ethereum compatibility via an EVM bridge, meaning developers and creators can build anything on Edgeware that they could on Ethereum, but with lower fees and a path to unlocking additional capabilities.

With Edgeware you can write smart contracts in Solidity or WASM and deploy applications that leverage existing and new infrastructure. 

This is how defi projects like [Dtrade](https://dtrade.org/) (Polkadot's first options and derivatives exchange) and [Picoswap](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/1138-introducing-picoswap-and-pico) (DEX) can form defi foundations of the community.

However offering an EVM bridge is not in itself unique, [Moonbeam](https://moonbeam.network/) also offers this, as will many other Substrate chains in time. Outside of the Substrate ecosystem, many other chains also offer this.

## 3. Community treasury

Part of Substrate's core runtime functions is a treasury function, meaning EDG from validator slashing, transaction fees and inflation is directed into a multisig wallet that anyone can request funding from via an integrated off-chain and on-chain proposal system. 

Currently the treasury controls around $12m EDG at today’s prices - ready for proposals that can extend the awareness, utility and access of the project - the only limits are your imagination. 

Other than Polkadot or Kusama who each have large treasuries, no other project has this community treasury in production. 

This is rapidly becoming the standard design framework for every new blockchain launch, both from the Substrate SDK side and with the CosmosSDK.

Instead of funding from the _outside in_ - these projects can fund the development of their nascent communities from the _inside out_, involving them at every stage of the process and ensuring that incentives are aligned from the very start.

In simple terms, it has given people voice within the machine - this is a new and underappreciated aspect that will have profound consequences over the next few decades.

## 4. Governance systems and interfaces 

A generic smart contract blockchain such as a Edgeware or Moonbeam enables _permissionless innovation_ since anyone can build on the chain without need to ask anyone. 

This has been Ethereum's primary advantage when scaling its network of developers and projects - you don't need to ask anyone, just go ahead and do it.  

This has led to an insane amount of experimentation - good and bad, with loads of different things happening across the chain, but at the expense of a primary organising principle, vision or direction. 

For many in Ethereum (and definitely in Bitcoin) this lack of explicit community governance process is a feature, not a bug. With Edgeware we like to think that the option to govern the chain and direct its resources gives us another string to our bow... 

It is a way to direct a decentralised public fund in an explicit way - with Commonwealth's community interface integrated with the on-chain governance functions of Substrate. 

Commonwealth Labs launched both the chain and the interface and this has enabled Edgeware and it’s community to become the *power users* of the interface which is open to any blockchain or token. 

Layer 1 chainDAOs such as [Edgeware](https://edgewa.re), [Polkadot](https://polkadot.network), [Kusama](https://kusama.network) and [Decred](https://decred.org), alongside Ethereum based Layer 2 appDAOs like [Uniswap](https://uniswap.org), [API3](https://api3.org) and [PieDAO](https://www.piedao.org/) collectively control billions of dollars - put simply capital is no longer a scarce resource. 

The next great challenge for blockchain projects is to organise their internet communities and direct this spending. 

Edgeware's benefits directly from the experience of using Commonwealth for nearly 18 months, with community activity picking up and accelerating as more people discover this as a location to discuss ideas, develop proposals and vote on projects.

Other Substrate projects are launching from a standing start - do not underestimate how hard decentralised organisation is… there are no short-cuts, building a real community driven by participation is slow, methodical work, far away from the hype of most project's that are driven by speculative armies of token shillers, rather than direct contribution to the core project. 

Welcome to the future of work... pretty soon, we'll all be working for DAOs of one kind or another.

## 5. Belief in people

Shared stories are the ultimate civilisational primitive - tools to organise collective action that predate the rational logic of accounting systems by several thousand years. 

At their heart, blockchains are new coordination systems to empower collective action. People put the meaning into the machine - without them the software is _meaningless_.

This move away from ad-driven, attention based media will become even more pronounced as a new Web3 media environment develops, bringing with it new incentives that enable the funding of high quality information - in the process providing a desperately needed balance to profit driven, clickbait based newscycles that dominate digital media.

## 6. Network of DAOs

So we've brought a bunch of people together, given them a generic smart contract platform to build on, a public fund to kickstart their ideas... now we need to figure out how to organise them into collective action. 

Edgeware is itself a DAO - a layer one blockchain DAO for that matter with it's own community treasury, but there are limits to how socially scalable a single fund and proposal system can be. Pretty soon it becomes obvious that the major scaling issue for next generation DAOs with large treasuries is not one of transaction capacity, but of social scalability... 

> The challenge is no longer fundraising, these projects have enough capital to build for decades. The challenge is now sourcing, developing and delivering quality proposals and rewarding and retaining those that contribute over the long term.
> [- Edgeware Grant Programme Structure   ](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/1110-edgeware-grant-programme-structure-discussion)

DAOs have been around on ETH for a number of years, 

Substrate offers multi-sig wallets that can be managed by any number of participants, Commonwealth easily lets a new group spin up an instance for forum discussions, whilst [bounty systems](https://github.com/paritytech/substrate/issues/5713) pioneered in Kusama enable ways to delegate responsibility to creators who can pay out proposals based on the achievement of agreed project milestones. 

Pretty soon all of the building blocks for DAO business logic will be in place, setting the stage for an armada of agile sail-boats, able to forge ahead of the larger mother ship whose myriad capabilities they inherit and return value to. 

In this regard Edgeware is pushing forwards ahead of other chains, experimenting with a range of projects who will each manage their own multi-sig and funds - becoming _Ecosystem Partners_ in the process. 

One project is the [Edgeware Agency](https://edgeware.agency) - since the project has no foundation, the decision was made to develop a community foundation in the form of DAO, the agency would be tasked with the same role - solving issues on the chain, running maintenance, building key infrastructure, onboarding new teams, but be entirely community managed and funded.
