### Quote of the Day
"If we want real people building real stuff, we need to abstract this technology to a place where not just subject matter experts are building, but people are building this for the world, for themselves." *- Aidan Hyman*

### Day Three: For the People

One of the first talks on Day Three of Web3 Summit 2019 was given by Aidan Hyman on the evolution of ChainSafe Systems. The mission behind ChainSafe is to make it make easy for developers to build the things they dream about, making blockchain technology more inclusive. This mission was referenced throughout the talk, with Hyman pointed out that the technology driving Web3 development is meant to empower the community and be used by regular people - not just subject matter experts. 

Hyman emphasized the hard work of the people who have gotten the Web3 ecosystem to where it is today, describing them as people who love the tech for the sake of the tech, but further shared two calls to action for that group. The first was a hope to see acknowledgment across the space that decentralization, seen by many as an incontrovertible goal, is a process that is constantly shifting and evolving. Hyman also referenced the underlying current throughout the conference that there is growing competition in the space, calling for people to grow out of the idea that anything in the Web3 ecosystem is a zero-sum game and understand that it's all an experiment. 

This focus on openness to evolution and the strive to make blockchain technology more inclusive was continued in a talk given by Alexander Skidanov from NEAR Protocol. With NEAR, Skidanov said, the goal is that if a developer without blockchain experience wants to build decentralized tech, the tools offered should be as easy as to use as the building frameworks that the developer uses elsewhere. Skidanov emphasized that developers shouldn't need a foundational understanding of Solidity, or have to download a wallet or Metamask, or even understand gas fees. Instead, ecosystems should be developed that allow for genuinely easy building. 

## Web3 Summit 2019: The Review

Remaining talks at Web3 Summit circled back to common themes throughout the conference: security, privacy, interoperability, and scalability. These are becoming the standard goals for anyone building for a Web 3.0. Beyond these product-based principles, Web3 Summit also showcased shifting cultural values in the space of blockchain technology. 

[Web3 Summit began](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/109) with a call for manifesto-based organizations by Luis Cuende of Aragon One. Each speaker afterward continually emphasized the importance of building with truly forward-thinking utility in mind. There was widely a rejection of seeking short-term profit, despite the underlying thread of concern regarding invading capitalist competition. 

This foundational emphasis on building for the future was followed up by [strong ideas about what sort of future we want to see accompanied by Web 3.0](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/111). Talks by Edward Snowden and whistleblower advocate Naomi Colvin, as well as conversations with Gavin Wood and Richard Stallman, showcased the general feeling that current centralized authorities have been captured by individual interests, and power needs to be shifted into the hands of the people. Privacy technologies introduced by David Chaum and Harry Halpin were among the many innovative solutions for how to give people that freedom, with these focused on decentralized identities and payment systems. Other talks focused on how this could be accomplished with a code-based rule of law and the potential for DAOs to facilitate the more fair organization of human networks. 

This vision of reinventing our socio-political systems based on Web3 culture and technologies was rounded-out by the end of the conference with several calls to action for lowering the barriers to Web3. The final moments of Web3 Summit circled back to those opening calls for building for the future but stressed the importance of building a future that *all* of humanity wants to see, one based on consent, consensus, and inclusion.

There was a rallying cry to build for developers who are not experts, to create technology that can serve as infrastructural frameworks that expand human potential, not profits, and to build things that truly give people alternatives to the “traditional” world. No matter how much some of the ideologies of those individuals building in the Web3 space disagree with those of centralized power structures, the people currently relying on those centralized structures are the ones who need to benefit from this new technology.
