### Takeaways

- Poloniex, a major crypto exchange, announced that it would be leaving Circle and will instead become Polo Digital Assets, Ltd.
- As of November 1, 2019, customers from the United States will no longer be able to use the platform 
- The new company will be backed by Asian investors, who will spend $100M on development
- The move may have been due to increasing SEC scrutiny towards cryptocurrencies - or simply a lack of US interest in the exchange 

It was unexpected news this month when Poloniex, a major crypto exchange, announced that it would be leaving Circle, a cryptocurrency finance platform backed by Goldman Sachs. With the announcement came the news that as of November 1, 2019, customers from the United States would no longer be able to use the platform, and would have until December 15th, 2019 to withdraw their assets. 

Poloniex will reportedly become a whole new company, called Polo Digital Assets, Ltd. According to their blog post, “The spinout will free us to focus on the needs of global crypto traders with new features, assets, and services.” They stated that the new investment group offers $100M in spending on the development of the exchange (@Poloniex). This new group has yet to be announced, but is reportedly located in Asia (Hodl).

Poloniex was acquired by Circle as recently as February 2018 for $400 million (FreeGPU Bitcoin). Some have suggested that this spin-out is in response to an increasing lack of interest in crypto markets. Five years ago, Poloniex was at the top of trading volume in crypto markets, but recently it has fallen far behind other exchanges, like Binance. There has been some thought circulating that the spin-out is indicative of Poloneix’s declining future, though some still believe it is a response to increasing SEC action (Crypto News).

The latter may be more likely a cause, however, given the parallel scaling down of Binance and Bittrex action in the US. 

With increasing SEC action in the States, many are also wondering if Poloniex is setting the standard for how cryptocurrency markets will interact with the US government and their US-based customers. One important factor in setting those standards is the use of US customer data. One commenter to their Twitter announcement of the spin-out wondered if they will be sharing all the data collected from US customers with their new Asian investment group - to which Poloniex replied that they would, indeed, be keeping everyone’s data (@Poloniex).
