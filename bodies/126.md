## Takeaways

- MakerDAO’s Dai, which are over-collateralized stablecoins, will transition from Single-Collateral to Multi-Collateral
- A Dai Savings Rate smart contract will also be offered, mimicking traditional savings accounts
- Dai backed by specific types of collateral will become “Synthetic Assets”
- MCD is MakerDAOs attempt to allow for more wide-reaching DeFi opportunities - and it’s unclear how successful it will be, or what it will show us about the intersection of DAOs with more traditional structures 

### How should we react to MakerDAO’s transition to Multi-Collateral Dai?

For those who are new to this space, Dai is a non-inflationary, collateralized stablecoin offered by MakerDAO. The coin is meant to allow users to avoid the volatility associated with both fiat currency and crypto markets. For every Dai distributed, there is 150% collateral held (Sudarshan). 

Right now, all Dai are Single-Collateral Dai. On October 31, 2019, MakerDAO introduced a terminology change with a new innovation: Multi-Collateral Dai (MCD). **Now, Single-Collateral Dai will be Sai, and MCD, to take the official name Dai, will be released on November 18, 2019.** 

Along with this shift comes a terminology shift: what was previously called “Collateralized Debt Position (CDP)” will become a “Vault.” This was in response to confusion surrounding the term and it’s comparison to traditional financial structures, as well as confusion about whether the CDPs referred to all holdings in MakerDAO entirely, or individual types of assets with the introduction of MCD. The team clarified that the “Maker Vault” will be where a user deposits collateral and generates Dai, with each type of collateral in its own vault (Maker, October 2019). 

**Maker hopes that the introduction of MCD will serve to welcome a new group of users into the organization**, and highlighted the vocabulary changes as meant to welcome more newcomers to the space. 

Which types of collateral tokens will be accepted in the MCD system was decided by MKR voters. Maker Foundation chose some initial tokens for voters to test, including:

- Augur (REP)
- Basic Attention Token (BAT)
- DigixDAO (DGD)
- Ether (ETH)
- Golem (GNT)
- OmiseGo (OMG)
- 0x (ZRX)

According to their blog, these tokens were chosen for “diversity, an average daily volume of several million USD, and the relative stability of each token” (Maker, June 2019). 

There has been some hesitation around the possibility of including “real-world assets” as part of MCD. Community members have supported a forum proposal for PurityDai, in case this happens - a type of Dai coin that will be backed purely by ETH. This is an idea that is possible because of the Maker team’s intention for MCD to be used to spawn other “Synthetic Assets” - other Dai Stablecoins pegged to specific kinds of collateralized assets (rune). 

Another element introduced with MCD will be a Dai Savings Rate (DSR), which allows users to lock their Dai in a smart contract that will let them earn on those savings. **Longer-term, the team hopes the DSR will allow for broader decentralized finance integration and adoption** (Maker, October 2019). 

Christensen hinted at new governance processes for the MakerDAO system to further incentivize and streamline MakerDAO’s voting, which to this point has mostly revolved around ratifying changes to the MakerDAO Stability Fee. These fees play a dual role in the MakerDAO system, both stabilizing DAI to the $1 peg and providing interest on debt taken out against cryptocurrency collateral (Kim).

With the transition to MCD, the CEO of Maker Foundation, Rune Christensen, has hinted that **the MakerDAO voting system may see some governance changes implemented** (Kim). This is probably in reference to a change from the Maker Foundation Interim Risk Team introducing Executive Votes that change the Dai Stability Fee, to oracles and algorithms semi-automatically implementing the process (MakerDAO).  

## Outlooks 

The promised transition to Multi-Collateral Dai is MakerDAO’s attempt to allow for more wide-reaching DeFi opportunities. It’s unclear how successful it will be, or what unforeseen challenges will be faced, given how much new territory is being explored. At the same time, much of this transition is meant to let DeFi structures mimic traditional, centralized financial processes, which could mean the experiment will have curious takeaways regarding the intersection of DAOs and the traditional world. It will be especially interesting to see how new governance structures that use oracles and algorithms to moderate the stability fee will play out.
