**Summary: **This article discusses the challenges of Frontier, and asks where it fits in Edgeware technical priorities in light of ecosystem and strategic changes since network launch.

-- 

Commonwealth Labs has been working extremely hard on integrating , debugging, and improving our EVM compatibility layer 'Frontier'-  and one of our largest platforms, DTrade, will be leveraging this in a multichain fashion.

When we first began working with Frontier, XDai had just begun to see adoption as a L2 for ETH, and the EVM-specific platform, and within Polkadot, it wasn't yet clear how Moonbeam and other EVM platforms would evolve. 

Today, Polygon and other roll-up platforms have gained not-insignificant share, and we may in a good position to update our priorities.


1. EVM today faces usability issues and eats up a significant portion of our community engineering attention, from my perspective. (Take with a grain of salt, as I may not have the clearest or most up-to-date view.)
2. Dtrade is reliant on EVM working and is one of the larger and more anticipated projects on EDG.
3. ETH Bridges and EVM help facilitate asset transfer to the network, improving some traditional metrics for EDG. 


The existing strategy for EVM is, as I understand it, to enable a 'copy paste' level of deployment ease for teams in ETH who are looking to enter the DOT ecosystem. 

With unfinished research around the parabridge / parachain direction, and the possibility of swappable consensus, I am concerned that encouraging more teams to launch on EVM at this time may be premature - not only because we cannot provide determinative guidance on the direction (bridge/parachain) but because of the current status of EVM.

If we can resolve this functionality soon, and offer at minimum a 1x-- ideally a 10x experience over ETH deployment, I am comfortable continuing with the notion that we can rapidly onboard potentially tens of existing ETH Dapps. 

If we expect continued issues with Frontier, face EVM competition from specialized teams like Moonbeam, and growing L2s like Polygon, I think we should consider reprioritizing, a refactoring plan for EVM apps,  or even shifting away from EVM entirely (or at least until a later date,)

To flesh out that latter path, it requires significant compensation in enabling teams to build in Ink!.

- A better understanding of Ink!'s capabilities and roadmap.
- Greater capacity development for Rust/Ink! developers, stronger educational strategy - including improving our Builders program / Builders for Builders program.
- Stronger and more targeted strategy around the initial seeding projects that will launch on EDG. 
- Consider Hice Et Nunc, a Tezos dapp - NFT gallery and marketplace that is the top source of activity in Tezos, alongside their uniswap clone. A_ single_ STRONG dapp can lift an entire chain to relevance - reminding me of the parachain use case.



I think the best benefits for maintaining Frontier are in bridging assets, access to stablecoins, and supporting the teams and projects that have already announced and developed in reliance- and these are massive reasons. 

I think the best reasons to deprioritize are to better align and strengthen our relationship with Parity, encourage a targeted and synergistic early-growth ecosystem with a clear alignment with Edgeware's longer term growth strats (e.g. parachain factory,)  and to narrow the scope of our efforts on what our chain does best- Rust stuff. 

Regardless of how we priortize the 'sides' of our contracting, we should probably work to deploy the above Ink! strategy, just at lower bandwidth if EVM is a priority going forward. 

## Updates:
Jun 10 2021: After knowing that EVM issues are largely resolved, I feel confident that we should continue pursuing dual functionality.
