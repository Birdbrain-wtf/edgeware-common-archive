## Introduction

The following outlines the thinking for the community film which will be an exciting and experimental collective endeavour led by the [Edgeware Agency](https://edgeware.agency) and [Lemonade Money](https://lemonademoney.tv) a creative collective based in London of which I am a part. 

Our partners include Spotify, Nike, Red Bull, BBC and Channel 4 and we are the creators of [FTTF](https://fttf.tv).

Our aim is to further advance Edgeware's claim to be the most advanced DAO in existence, through the coordination of a large number of people from around the world around a single shared media project and experience.

This project will attempt to join the dots between the many elements of Edgeware - it's people, proposition and capabilities, in the process tightening the bonds between each and accelerating the operations and output of our emerging media publishing operations. 

## **Some history  **

9 months ago a few strangers who had taken part in the lockdrop joined a community call - trying to figure out whether Edgeware was a community worth contributing to.

In the beginning we were a little skeptical, in crypto, the word 'community' is thrown around so much that it has almost completely lost any real meaning, so it took a few more calls before we started to build initial trust. 

That trust was built further as we delivered a number of community led projects including community led [logo refresh](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/975-edgeware-brand-identity-process-concluded), [website](https://edgewa.re) and development around video ideas, strategic positioning of the network and a plan for the evolution of the community built around a network of DAOs that we call _ecosystem partners_. 

The culmination of this emergent process was the creation of the [Edgeware.Agency](https://edgeware.agency).


## **A sense of community **

The loss of meaning in the word _community_ isn't a new phenomenon, the shared definition has been slowly degrading for many years as marketers, brands, platforms, investors and entrepreneurs throw the label around - when really they mean customers, consumers, users... people to ultimately sell to. 

Defining c_ommunity_ is a bit like trying to define love, anxiety or calm, you kind of know it when you feel it, or recognise it in hindsight, but if you tried to stick it in a job description everything get blurry.

Perhaps that's because one of capitalism's defining features is a requirement to neatly order things in boxes - organise, define, label - after all if its _a thing_, we can point at it, package it, price it... sell it. 

Advertising acronyms such as CPA (Cost Per Acquisition) have reduced real people to products too - our attention is bought, our interests are mined, our friendships scraped, before geographic and demographics further define our worth in a giant evolving media spreadsheet. 

Crypto does this pricing thing very well - just look at CoinmarketCap and at Edgeware, down 200 places in a few months... well, that's it, game over... _the community is dead._

The issue with this thinking is that price is by definition aggregated throigh crowd consensus, namely the actions of a large number of unrelated people, cause the emergence of collective sentiment that is then translated into a number, which bobs around in the fish bowl of many other numbers, which you then stare at, and worry about and refresh and refresh and... FUCK! 

But does this number reflect the value or indeed the values of a community? 

No. Of course not. Price is a trailing indicator, it is just an echo of decisions made months or even years in the past. If it wasn't, investing wouldn't be a thing and the world would be static. 

A number is a noun - a definitive thing, however _community _is a verb - **a process, not a product**. 

In a 1986 study, McMillan and Chavis identified four elements of "sense of community":

1. _membership: feeling of belonging or of sharing a sense of personal relatedness,_
2. _influence: mattering, making a difference to a group and of the group mattering to its members_
3. _reinforcement: integration and fulfillment of needs,_
4. _shared emotional connection._


When you reorient your worldview, suddenly a whole load of other 'things' in crypto make a lot less sense. 

According to well known investors we are living through a 'CryptoArt' revolution - a veritable renaissance. Except art is not _a thing_, despite smart financial engineers telling you it is - it is a verb - something you do, not something you sell. 

Beeple's art was the 10 years of graft... the ability to sustain that process was where value was created. 

Money, currency, blockchains these words we use aren't _things_, they're processes. 

## Belief Systems

A blockchain without people is meaningless. It is just software running a program - its the decisions of the people in the project that decides what kind of belief system can be imagined into existence. You can shout DAO all day long, it's the people that matter. 

Luckily Edgeware has a pretty good belief system - it's definitely rough around the edges, but it has attracted a weird bunch from across the internet who agree on some stuff, disagree on many others, but kind of, well... just about get along.

When it comes to building real community, we have [a bumch of advantages](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/1176-out-on-the-edge-a-collective-vision-for-the-future-of-edgeware). 

Unlike the vast majority of projects there was no ICO, is no dominant corporate entity running the show and we have a community fund that we can deploy to expand our belief system - to conjure new ideas into existence, and in turn these ideas will attract new believers, who see themselves in the actions of others, suddenly realising... _hey, I can contribute here_.

Feeling useful is a deep human need, because the other side of the coin of usefulness, is being _needed_. And being needed is s precursor to a sense of community on which anything of real and lasting value is built. 

## The Community Film 

So we're going to make a _community film - _and by that I mean we're going to fund **a process, not a product**.

We're going to pull back the curtain and show you how the sausage gets made, and in this way, we're going to tell the stories of the people who are the foundations of Edgeware's emerging belief system: 

- Why are they in crypto?
- Why are they in Edgeware?
- What do they want to achieve individually and collectively?
- What are their dreams?
- What are their fears?
- How are they collaborating?
- What can they build together?


And we're going to wrap those personal stories into the context of the chaotic world we are all living in, the challenges, opportunities and the ways small actions by many add up to making a big difference. 

Of course we'll talk about Edgeware's technology, but this is not going to be another crypto film - we have made a solemn promise not to visualise transactions on a blockchain.

Of course, we need a 'thing' for the website, ultimately the trust to deliver will be delegated to those with experience to deliver, namely the team at [Lemonade Money](https://lemonademoney.tv) who some of you have already spoken to as part of an initial development funding which resulted in this teaser film which includes a few voices from the community.

There will be more detail over the days and weeks ahead as we move into full production and the aim is to share as much as we can as we go and to involve everyone in the process as much as possible.

For all those requesting "marketing" this is the beginnings of our own original approach to telling an interesting and novel story with the intention of recapturing and redefining that most misused of words - _community_. 

This process will illuminate Edgeware's unique position in the ecosystem as a truly community owned and operated entity that can achieve anything it's emerging collective intelligence sets its mind to and further reinforce its credentials as a home for Web3 media.
