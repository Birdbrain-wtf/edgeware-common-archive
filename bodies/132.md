Will we understand that code is law and leverage that knowledge to embed the values we want to see in the structure of the Internet? This is the question Lawrence Lessig asked in his essay from the year 2000, [*Code Is Law: On Liberty in Cyberspace*](https://harvardmagazine.com/2000/01/code-is-law-html). Lessig, the respected academic and political activist who once served as the director of the Edmond J. Safra Center for Ethics at Harvard University, actually coined the term “code is law.” At the turn of the century, Lessig elucidated the way code regulates how life on the Internet can be lived by limiting access, privacy, and individual control. And whether it is traditional governments and commerce systems or simply coders, someone has the power to determine how code is used to regulate freedom. In the author’s words, “Unless we understand how cyberspace can embed, or displace, values from our constitutional tradition, we will lose control over those values.”

Lessig first explained the regulatory nature of code in his 1999 book *Code and Other Laws of Cyberspace*, but if you don’t have time for that read, his essay linked above is certainly worth a read. In it, Lessig alludes to the foundational issues we are still struggling with today in the blockchain-based, Web 3.0 ecosystem: the challenges of designing liberty-preserving Internet architectures, avoiding system capture by partial interests, and implementing self-governance.
