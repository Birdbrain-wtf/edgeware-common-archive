Responding to: [Decentralization and Self-Sovereignty for Identity on the Blockchain](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/123-decentralization-and-selfsovereignty-for-identity-on-the-blockchain)

I think it’s important to emphasize self-sovereignty as a crucial part of making a digital identity. Has anyone seen the news about Australia’s digital identity? Check out this Tweet from Jenny Leung. It shows all the personal info the government will now have access to, including your calendar, your camera, GPS location, and even the ability to access, modify and delete USB storage: https://twitter.com/jnnylng/status/1189170416335622144

This is why Web3 spaces are important!

I bet we all agree in this space that our data is important and it needs to be protected. I am curious about what people think about the point that people should be able to monetize their own data, though. The first data union was just officially launched (it’s a browser plugin, https://swashapp.io). The goal of this kind of group seems to be to let people, not giant tech corporations, profit from personal data. But it still doesn’t sit well with me that data can be sold at all. Obviously, capitalism runs the world, but it seems like a decentralized, self-sovereign identity revolution could also mean the end of data harvesting entirely. Seems like that should be the goal, not just individuals getting the profit from data mining instead of giant companies. Maybe that’s just not realistic, though.

Anyone care to disagree, or have other thoughts on adding that sort of limit to self-sovereignty? I feel like this could get into a bigger discussion on the actual meaning of self-sovereignty and freedom of choice in today's world of psychological marketing tactics...

I also just want to emphasize that Catalonia’s push for a digital decentralized state is a huge step. This is not something that should be looked at as a traditional system moving to blockchain tech — it should be seen as revolutionaries declaring their freedom from a central state. They’re using decentralized identity tech to opt-in to a community they are building that will have a very different power source than the Spanish government. This Tweet for reference: https://twitter.com/jbaylina/status/1189896524483964928

It’s an incredibly important case to see how blockchain tech can actually be implemented to provide alternatives to traditional structures and highlights the fact that this doesn’t just mean an update to old infrastructure, but a complete overhaul of what nation-states are. Is it possible this tech will set off a reaction that resets our current global values?

I know for political systems, in particular, there’s been attempts at creating blockchain-based nation-states, but I’m curious if anyone has any more expertise or can point to resources for understanding what the future of these political efforts could mean.

For those unfamiliar with how serious the situation is, this is a great summary: https://www.bbc.com/news/world-europe-41584864

The problems in Catalonia show how much this could seriously be the first huge case of a “nation-state” transforming itself to a decentralized community, or at least attempting to - how successful or not this move is will have serious implications for how groups trying to empower themselves with decentralized building will be able to react when confronted with pushback from traditional power structures.
