Hello all, 

In the ongoing efforts of the agency to provide the best developer experience for developers looking to build on Edgeware, it's important that we're looking at some key areas that we should target that will help begin to really begin to get the ball rolling on creating a more engaged and robust developer ecosystem. The three points I'd like to cover are:

- **Overhauling the structure of the documentation**
- **Creating a UI pipeline on the Edgeware website for grants and bounties**
- **Creating stronger feedback loops and internal conversations surrounding developer tools and resources available on Edgeware**


Our documentation for Edgeware is an especially fundamental piece to the success of our network, as we are a community operated network, it's important that **knowledge sharing** is available to the fullest extent as to allow everyone to understand and be on the same page about how different processes work and play a role in the bigger picture. Successful documentation will make information easily accessible, provide a limited number of user entry points, help new users learn quickly, simplify the product and help cut support costs. The next step in refining our documentation will be restructure it from the ground up to take an approach that is going to allow clarity and comprehension for those not only within the Polkadot and Edgeware space, but for someone who may be entirely new to blockchain technology as a whole. All of our subsections should be carefully renamed, placed and organized so that we can provide the knowledge, resources, and guidance needed to allow for complete immersion into this space without new developers feeling overwhelmed or intimidated immediately. We should be viewed as the standard for aspiring developers who want to learn more about blockchain technology and includes all the best parts of a robust and developer friendly ecosystem. 

Another important blocker that we've seen is the confusion with what to do when new developers who enter the community. Often times, someone new will enter our developer channels with no real guidance on what to do next. Moderators will help guide individuals on where to go, but even still, many new developers may feel discouraged or potentially even intimidated with our current pipeline for tasks that are available to the community. We're currently working towards revising our grants and current objectives to best align with our short and long term goals and then we are planning to push these grants / bounties to the [website](https://edgewa.re/) with a UI that categorizes these different tasks respectively with relevant links. This will allow for an easy reference for anyone who wants to build on Edgeware but doesn't know where to get started, and with the overhaul on the documentation, it'll allow for a less convoluted approach when onboarding new developers into the community while also providing a structured model of progression. The tools and resources have been available, but they're very difficult to find, and when we are providing a very poor 'first-contact' experience for anyone looking to build on top of Edgeware, it quickly turns away any developers who may have been excited to learn more, prior to their poor encounter, as it suggests that we may have a poor structure throughout our network.

Lastly, we want to engage in more meaningful conversations with the community. We want the community to choose what they want, and to voice thoughts on what things are blocking important decisions or features from being executed on. Especially in relation to grants and bounties, what are some features that would be beneficial to see that were not yet covered above. What are some additional problems that are causing issues with some of our developer pipelines that need addressing so myself and other agency members can continue to work with the community in evolving the ecosystem. Creating stronger feedback loops and internal conversations surrounding developer tools and resources will help speed these developments up by allowing us to understand exactly where we're falling short. We want to encourage the community to strive towards engaging in weekly meetings such as the Friday Builders call that reoccurs every 2PM EST. and the weekly community calls that occur every Wednesday at 1PM EST., as well actively participating in channels and joining in on discussions! We are a community-owned chain and we have the flexibility and awesome capability to make decisions in ways that no other chain can.
