### Why is governance important for blockchains?

Governance is a hugely important topic in the crypto world. Because blockchains today represent the ultimate meeting of cryptography and decentralized human networks, how to best integrate management controls into those systems is a question full of exciting possibilities that are just beginning to be truly explored (de Filippi & McMullen). It’s too soon to know what governance structures are the most effective; even at a primitive level, it’s unclear what type of governance mechanisms work best for what cases. 

### What are some theoretical frameworks for governance?

There are some wider theoretical principles that can be applied to designing good blockchain governance. For instance, if we consider a blockchain-based community and its resources a shared commons, we might look to Elinor Ostrom, who won the 2009 Nobel Prize in Economics for her work on how communities Eight Principles Managing a Commons:

1. Define clear group boundaries.
2. Match rules governing use of common goods to local needs and conditions.
3. Ensure that those affected by the rules can participate in modifying the rules.
4. Make sure the rule-making rights of community members are respected by outside authorities.
5. Develop a system, carried out by community members, for monitoring members’ behavior.
6. Use graduated sanctions for rule violators.
7. Provide accessible, low-cost means for dispute resolution.
8. Build responsibility for governing the common resource in nested tiers from the lowest level up to the entire interconnected system (Walljasper). 

We might also think about the importance of emergent properties, which are unexpected properties that emerge simply from the act of a group functioning together. A city, for instance, is an emergent property of human social interaction. Nobody specifically planned the first city’s existence - a group of humans operating together naturally brought about this phenomenon (Mercer). For blockchains specifically, the materialization of Schelling points in a token bonding curve or a curation market can be understood as an example of emergence that can be leveraged in governance. As blockchains see more and more experimental applications, we might think about using governance to optimize our chances of discovering these natural meeting points of the human mind in designing pathways for collaboration and organization. 

### What does blockchain governance actually look like?

One thing is certain: governance mechanisms must look different for different types of blockchains. There can be variations in how voting happens, or how treasury funds are raised and used. Voting in a blockchain-based network might take the form of liquid democracy, an algorithmic voting system that implements a futarchy, or token-weighted voting that preferences either stake, community preference (via quadratic voting), or reputation. Funding choices for a network’s treasury might apply mechanisms based on game theory, like [Quadratic Funding or Dominant Assurance Contracts](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/101). These can all be part of governance, and with other governance mechanisms managing how these parts interact or evolve. 

Dispute resolution is another big part of governance, and one that hasn’t seen much wide attention. For blockchains that implement smart contracts, oracle type and maintenance is another governance consideration. 

There can also be broader cultural choices: how much decentralization is okay and with what parameters, or whether changes to the chain are decided on and implemented on-chain or off-chain (or somewhere in between). 

Especially in the context of DAOs and dApps, governance choices are made at different levels of chain infrastructure. The levels these mechanisms operate at include:

- The Internet protocol layer
- The blockchain network layer 
- The dApp framework 
- The dApp layer (de Filippi & McMullen)

## Application 

At every layer, concepts of game theory, carefully implemented crypto-economic primitives, and alignment on principals and incentives are key for designing good governance structures. But it’s a complex, experimental key, one we learn more about every day. Despite the unknowns surrounding blockchain governance, there is still a lot to be gained from testing, learning, and improving how different mechanisms can optimize social coordination and the largely untapped human potential in blockchain networks.
