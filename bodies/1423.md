I spent this morning reading the latest post "[Positive Sum Worlds: Remaking Public Goods](https://otherinter.net/research/positive-sum-worlds/)" from [Other Internet ](otherinter.net/)(authors of [Headless Brands)](https://otherinter.net/research/headless-brands/) and other great pieces of research. 

It's a long essay and is essential reading for anyone interested in discussions of what "blockchains are for" and how we can evolve past conversations around price to understand how these amazing new social coordination technologies and the treasuries they enable offer us a path to building a better world over long time horizons. 

> _Can crypto protocols be considered public goods if ownership is concentrated in the hands of a few whales?  Colloquially, these market primitives are sometimes described as "public infrastructure," but if blockchains serve a "public" today, it is primarily one of decentralized finance. _
> _**Fundamentally, these tokenholders share only one common object of concern: price.**_
> _As protocol treasuries transition to community stewardship of public works, it's time to imagine how crypto protocols might uniquely conceive of public goods._

And leading on into the cultural aspects - namely how new ideas are adopted: 

> _French revolutionaries orchestrated a new peoples' self-consciousness by organizing festivals, erecting monuments, and designating new communal spaces (Ozouf, 1975). _
> _In both instances, political bodies were raised to the status of institutions by establishing new venues for storytelling—venues in which people could begin to identify themselves as part of a collective whole, that is, a public._
> _This notion of the public as a self-conscious entity has also taken hold on the internet, where **adoption is driven by publication and circulation of media.** To publish a web document or software application is more than making something public: it's an act of making a public (Warner, 2002)._

This is very much at the heart of the [Decent vision](https://github.com/Decent-Partners/decent-overview/blob/main/README.md) around the research and development of networked public service media and was something Dillon articulated early on in his post on [Programmatic Public Goods](https://www.notion.so/Programmatic-Public-Goods-089048e41dbe429bab72b67b9dcffaaf#19f10d42dc5e401ab7d64854db28769d). 

I would argue that a Commonwealth.im (this interface) as an open source protocol for governing public blockchains and their treasuries falls very much into the thinking articulated through the piece. 

With governance frameworks and funding in place, it is really now just the recruitment and development of projects that fulfil the notion of public goods with positive externalities with the opportunity to make local impact that results in a great story that can be communicated well.  

I think we are slowly seeing the emergence of these as projects that are funded by the treasury - baby steps that open up conversations as to how we can play more than financial games. 

Of course we need some basic functions - but really, we have everything we need right now - a treasury, ability to exchange, funding and people willing to accept $EDG as a medium of exchange. Literally everyone taking funds from the treasury is exchanging their most precious resource - time - in return for EDG.

Of course this doesn't play well when someone wants ETH / USDC instead of EDG - but in my mind, we should just work with people and projects who will accept EDG. Of course this will limit our potential contributors, but it focuses attention on those who really do believe.

As long as we continue to attract interesting and innovative people and their projects to the chain and its children, we are making progress, price is a lagging indicator and a big distraction.

> The shape of society in the 20th century was determined by corporations explicitly driven by a profit-only value system. Do we want the next-generation of protocol-based institutions to result in same outcomes? 
> The type of social institution needed is not an on-chain corporation, but a container for a different idea of what capital and coordination is intended to do altogether. 
> We see a seed of this in today's protocol granting bodies, distributing responsibility for treasury funds to smaller groups with targeted public goals. Imagine the power of entire protocols operating according to this principle. 
> The scope of our ambition goes well beyond conscientious token voting. We want nothing less than entire communities aping into a redefinition of public concern. 
> The opportunity in front of us is bigger than any one protocol. In today's world, capital is not scarce; ambitious visions for the public's benefit are. 

**What would a fair launch chain factory look like? **

It also linked to a post on [Fair Launch Capital](https://www.fairlaunch.capital/) - which has an interesting framework that prompts interesting ideas that could serve as inspiration for Kabocha or any future chain factory models. 

> When we say Fair Launch, we mean:
> ➡️ A decentralized crypto network that is earned, owned and governed by the community from the outset.
> ➡️ Everyone can participate on equal footing.
> ➡️ There is no early access, pre-mine or allocation of tokens.

In essence there is a "pay it forward model" that drives a long term, community first mentality, putting collaboration over profit. 

> Here’s how it works:
> 1. We identify the most promising projects that want to do a Fair Launch.
> 2. We make a no-strings-attached grant to cover the costs of audit and launch.
> 3. We never take any allocation of tokens before launch.
> 4. After launch, we initiate a governance vote to let the community decide to pay the grant forward to the next project that wants to do a Fair Launch.
