MakerDAO’s [transition to Multi-Collateral Dai (MCD)](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/126-potential-lessons-from-multicollateral-dai) is accompanied by the establishment of “Synthetic Assets,” a Dai Savings Rate smart contract, and a potential new governance model that uses oracles and algorithms to manage the stability fee keeping Dai pegged to the dollar. These all sound exciting, and the MakerDAO team hopes the transition to MCD will open the space to a much broader user base. But it’s left many of us wondering: **will the new Dai fix any of the problems that Sai already had? What new challenges will arise?**  

A 2017 code review of MakerDAO’s Sai revealed opportunities for participants to game the system, including a way for whales to capture rewards meant for PETH holders (Baylina et al.). This was not the only serious issue the team of code reviewers found, and as they concluded: “We believe there are likely to be more issues in this code beyond what have been identified, and addressing the issues described in this document may introduce more bugs into the system.” 

While the MakerDAO team did offer a blog post responding to the major issues found, there are still questions about what new problems MCD may bring. This is important, because as MakerDAO has recently explained, “MCD is essentially a decentralized system without any operator. Once deployed onto the blockchain, it operates in an autonomous way.” In other words, it’s crucial that the team thinks through issues in the design of the system, given its autonomous execution.

The potential to use “real-world” collateral to back MCD has also triggered anxiety in the MakerDAO community. This collateral option is made possible via the Tinlake smart contract structure from Centrifuge, which has been successfully trialed in four pilot programs with New-Silver, Paperchain, and Future Finance (Schmitt). The real-world collateral infrastructure seems well-tested, and could potentially be a big step for DeFi adoption. But the inclusion of non-trustless assets begs the question: **Is MakerDAO trading a commitment to DeFi freedom and self-sovereignty for a broader audience?**
