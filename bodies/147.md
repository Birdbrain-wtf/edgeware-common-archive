## Exit Strategies for DAOs

An intentionally-built exit mechanism is an important DAO primitive, but it's another aspect of DAO governance that we haven’t yet seen enough experimentation with to develop industry best practices. To some degree, **the possibility of exit can incentivize the entire group to collaborate more efficiently and selflessly,** working toward group utility to keep the group together. At the same time, **the ability to leave freely and flow between groups allows for the organic formation of like-minded communities.**

An open-source DAO makes it technically possible for anybody to leave, at any time, because they can just copy and paste the code from the original DAO and start their own version (Grx). However, this could lead to a situation in which a small group of divisive stakeholders with loud voices drive away other organization participants who feel like their perspective is not well-represented or supported in the group (even though it very well may be). While these other organization participants could theoretically exit and start their own version of the DAO, they may not have the network or reputation to see the same amount of participation as in the original group. Therefore, their participation is simply lost, along with any other members who left the original DAO due to the loud voices of the divisive minority.

**Could a revised exit mechanism be implemented to help fight this potential challenge?** Perhaps a mechanism that says a participant can’t leave a DAO until a certain time period has passed, encouraging everyone to at least try participating in the organization and forcing slower community evolution. 

At the same time as it may be beneficial to support long-term participation for the success of a DAO, it’s also important to be able to leave a group you don’t agree with. This is especially in a DeFi DAO, and MolochDAO’s “ragequit” function has seen a lot of love in the DAO community for exactly this reason. MolochDAO’s entire goal is to provide funding for projects that the Moloch community agrees contribute to the public good. Ragequitting lets any MolochDAO member withdraw their funds from the organization within seven days of a proposal they disagree with (Concourse).

**But if MolochDAO’s exit mechanism allows for a very fast turnover rate for the organization population, can the group facilitate the creation of public goods that are truly valuable?** It seems as though a lack of a strong, long-term participant base would lead to the approval of projects that are, like the community, fleetingly useful and lacking sustainability.
