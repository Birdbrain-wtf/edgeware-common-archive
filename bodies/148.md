Decentralized autonomous organizations (DAOs) are exploding right now. We’re starting to see them take very unique shapes - from DAOs wrapped as legally compliant LLCs, to DAOs that hope to replace nation-states. This explosion has given us a lot of different concepts and terminology around DAOs, which begs the question: What really is the definition of a DAO? **What are some other words we could use for DAO?** 

This question gets a bit complicated thinking about the differences between types of decentralized organizations. For instance, in [a terminology guide](https://blog.ethereum.org/2014/05/06/daos-dacs-das-and-more-an-incomplete-terminology-guide/) by Vitalik Buterin, a Decentralized Autonomous Corporation/Company (DAC) is defined by the fact that “there is a concept of shares in a DAC which are purchaseable and tradeable in some fashion, and those shares potentially entitle their holders to continual receipts based on the DAC’s success.” So there should be a nominal distinction between a DAO that has a standard shareholder framework and offers financial returns on investments, and a DAO that doesn’t. 

Buterin also distinguishes between DAOs and DC’s: Decentralized Communities. This one is straightforward - there are not smart contracts defining any collective action by a DC, as there are in a DAO. However, the distinction drawn between a DAO and a DO (decentralized organization) is not so clear.  In Buterin's words, this is because “there is really no difference between a certain set of actors making decisions directly and that set of actors controlling all of the information through which decisions are made.” That is to say, even if the decentralized org is not technically autonomous, if a small group is controlling the people’s understanding of governance options, then the actions of the group could be as limited or defined as if some group was writing smart contracts to execute them. 

**This range of definitions shows that the term “DAO,” which is overused today, doesn’t apply to every situation equally, or appropriately.** There needs to be a clear distinction between decentralized autonomous organizations, decentralized organizations, and decentralized autonomous companies in their terminology - as well as better names altogether. **Better branding could help people understand that there is groundbreaking tech behind these types of groups, without obscuring the actual implications of the tech.**

Maybe a better term would be a “SmartOrg” or “Smart Company” for DAOs and DACs, following the significance of the smart contract for their creation. Smart could be over-played, though, and simply calling a DAO a SmartOrg fails to connect the name to the powerful opportunity that exists in on-chain governance for collaboration and cooperation. 

**So maybe we should consider something more radical.** Perhaps a phrase like “Independent Collective,” or a “Sovereign Collective?” These represent well the foundational ideals behind DAOs and DCs, though some phrasing changes might be needed to distinguish between the two. 

**Does anybody else have a better word for DAO?**
