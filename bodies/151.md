## Takeaways

- If you want to assess an ideal token distribution, first you need to decide what the goals of token distribution are.
- The most common idea behind an ideal token distribution is the maximization of decentralization.
- The opportunity to implement mechanisms of game theory and crypto-economics means that goals for token distribution could potentially go beyond simply having a low Gini coefficient.

### What does the ideal token distribution look like?

Token distribution is a key element to the success of a blockchain network. Concerns about [vote-buying for EOS](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/90-eos-votebuying-and-power-distribution) and potential [validator centralization in the Cosmos network](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/118-diversifying-delegations-stake-distribution-the-cosmos-example) show the importance of token distribution for protocol governance, security, and sustainability. But what does the ideal token distribution actually look like? 

The question breaks down to a few parts. **What are the goals of token distribution? How do we implement them, and how can we measure the results?**

A foundational principle behind ideal token distribution is maximal decentralization. The most common way to measure decentralization in token distribution is with a Gini coefficient calculation. For some context, Ethereum’s Gini coefficient is 0.798, and Bitcoin Cash is 0.754 (Parsiq). These aren’t necessarily bad, but we’ve set a new standard with the Edgeware lockdrop: recent [analysis of Edgeware’s token distribution gave a Gini-coefficient of 0.694](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/102-examining-the-edgeware-lockdrop-gini-coefficients-and-successes).

**The opportunity to implement mechanisms of game theory and crypto-economics means that goals for token distribution could potentially go beyond simply maximizing decentralization.** We could consider ways to spread distribution to individuals with diverse backgrounds, incentive alignment with the entire network, and potential to help develop the network (Laurent). Of course, even with these targets, it will always be important to consider an intentional amount of decentralization as a foundational goal for the overall health of a network.

Blockstack offers an example of a network that has targeted the goal of increasing token distribution to those who are actively developing and building on their network. They use “novel mining mechanisms that distribute tokens to app developers and users and miners who each strengthen the network” (Shea). 

But with these shifting goals, we have to get back to one of the first questions - how can we implement them?
