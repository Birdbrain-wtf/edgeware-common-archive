_**TL;DR**: Existing knowledge institutions and legitimation pipelines are in crisis. Pfeilstorch is a new, institutionally unaffiliated community for knowledge production, built on a Commonwealth fork and using Edgeware's treasury and governance structure to award microgrants and reputational credit. Our plan is to leverage the chaos of the current moment, and the emergence of new Web3 paradigms, to build cultural technology that supports decentralized research and philosophy. Our focus lies on those topics that existing institutions have shunned due to their messy unreadiness for formal verification and data collecting—topics in the “[inexact sciences](https://twitter.com/literalbanana/status/1247400947472404482?s=21),” such as human communication, social strategy, and representation in the wild._

## Intro

The last decade has seen major changes in research and publishing, as a wave of young thinkers forego traditional institutions for online scenes. And yet old-school forums remain the go-to platform where such writers and researchers can engage in long-form discussion. Substack isolates its writers in semi-paywalled digital newsletters. Twitter, an [“oral” culture](https://twitter.com/ctbeiser/status/1401775079139155970), is too noisy, fragmented, and disorganized for participants to build lasting frameworks together. The Commonwealth forum is a step in the right direction, but currently lacks tools specific to research and publishing. What is needed are discursive platforms which hybridize discussion and publishing, that generate new ideas while keeping cultural memory: a true Web3 knowledge production scene, a system of watering holes for decentralized thinkers. Yet the few present rallying points for “Game B” (i.e. institutionally unaffiliated) thought are [presently](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_dark_web) [consumed](https://twitter.com/peternlimberg/status/1289241648912728065) [by](https://letter.wiki/topics/65) [culture](https://www.reddit.com/r/TheMotte/) [war](https://art19.com/shows/the-portal)—a battle whose narrow set of issues has already tempted too many promising thinkers into thoughtless polemic. We need _cultural technology_ in order to populate the emerging Web3 infrastructure. Tech that can support a new research style: less professional pipelining, more superorganism. 

The Inexact Sciences research group—"TIS" for short—was founded last spring, and now counts over a dozen active members, each an expert in their own respective domain. Its output includes [podcasts](https://pfeilstorch.substack.com/), [long-form essays](https://theinexactsciences.github.io/#:~:text=some indicated whole.-,Papers,-REASON & RUTTEN 2018), [books](http://www.notnothing.ooo/), and [research decks](https://theinexactsciences.github.io/#:~:text=DISCUSSION FORUM-,Decks,-INTRO TO STRATEGIC). We aim to pioneer the literal and cultural technology necessary for para-academic work in [the inexact sciences](https://www.rand.org/pubs/reports/R353.html), building healthy pseudonymous discursive norms while [continuing to conduct](https://letter.wiki/conversation/1194) our own in-house research towards a richer understanding of human communication. Our macro-strategy looks something like this:

![](https://commonwealth-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/2ae0c5b7-ec32-4fa1-9fe9-23c3c165e83c.1628565159956)

The key to it all is the fact that we are populating the cultural space *and* experimenting with governance, credit assignment, and the ecology of Web3 capital in parallel. The majority of our group write code for some portion of their day jobs—including three members currently employed at Commonwealth Labs. We are in a unique position: a group of people who have spent the past year building up new theoretical frameworks for tackling messy problem domains, but who are also native to Web3 and hungry to build and experiment with how to make tokens a useful representation of intellectual value and potential. The fact that our research is also focused on [representation and misrepresentation in signaling](https://suspendedreason.com/2021/07/22/surrogation-overview/) means that the documentation we plan to publish for our own experiments with token credit systems can lay the groundwork for more rigorous analysis of the social dynamics imposed by such systems in general—with the eventual aim of cataloguing those design patterns that work. 

[The walled garden](https://www.ribbonfarm.com/2015/03/04/gardens-need-walls-on-boundaries-ritual-and-beauty/) of [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/) is perhaps one of the best existing models for such a platform: for 15 years it has maintained productive longform discourse, building an extensive knowledge graph and birthing multiple [charitable](https://www.lesswrong.com/tag/effective-altruism/history) and [research](https://intelligence.org/) organizations. Yet we believe the rationalist outlook—its [lack of interest](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/ASpGaS3HGEQCbJbjS/eliezer-s-sequences-and-mainstream-academia) in engaging with [the best of traditional academia](https://theinexactsciences.github.io/docs/Situating the Contributions of LessWrong to the Philosophy of Language.pdf); its devotion to materialism and reductionism which can border on scientism; and its lack of viewpoint diversity—has hindered its potential, and prevents it from functioning as a more universal rallying point.

Our proposition is simple: use the squad wealth we’ve accumulated over the last year to make a Web3 platform _and_ native culture, to catalog and coordinate decentralized ideas and thinkers in the inexact sciences. By pioneering the cultural approach, and building open-source research tools, we chart the path for others to follow. 

## Inexact Sciences vs. Indie Thinkers 

Because IndieThinkers recently received a grant from Edgeware, it’s important to clarify how our value proposition differs from theirs, since both are fundamentally content and culture focused. Like IndieThinkers, we are a (generically) “Game B” organization—a non-traditional, knowledge-work institution competing for market share with a decaying academia ("Game A"). However, per IndieThinkers’ own materials, it is a site geared toward things like:
* Improving your writing and PR skills
* Getting feedback on your work
* Building professional connections
* Growing creators’ audience and income
* Accountability coworking sessions 

The Inexact Sciences, meanwhile, is collaboratively working to build a new body of knowledge, create shared frames and language, and tackle a rapidly maturing research agenda. Think reading group meets Discord server meets think-tank. Our research interests are wide-ranging, but center around language, representation, psychology, and the [microeconomics](https://www.pfeilstor.ch/-97/selection-games) of human social life. They are further unified by a worldview + methodology combo:
1. [Stamp-collecting](https://www.are.na/the-inexact-sciences/): You need to collect and organize a _lot_ of examples before it becomes possible to synthesize them into an adequate theory like evolution. But this crucial cataloguing stage has been skipped over, impatiently, by the current sciences of human communication.
2. [The “rigorizing pipeline”](https://www.pfeilstor.ch/-66/about-pfeilstorch): There is a natural flow of knowledge that starts with art or literature and ends with the hard sciences. Fields like psychology have crippled themselves by cargo-culting the methodology of later stages of the pipeline, without first accomplishing the early stages. They’d be better served reporting detailed observations of real human interactions and developing models that can be falsified down-the-line, rather than focusing on p-values from survey experiments.
3. [Generalized compatibilism](https://spilledreality.tumblr.com/post/620839172988715009/generalized-compatibilism) & [Knowledge logistics](https://twitter.com/everytstudies/status/1309928300157046784): Current institutions suffer from a lack of interdisciplinary communication, combined with professional pressure on academics to differentiate themselves from peers. This incentivizes endless rebuttals rather than shared frameworks.
4. [Pragmatism](https://crispychicken.cc/2021/02/12/concepts-are-tools-not-artifacts/): An unfortunate amount of contemporary philosophy is bogged down in verbal disputes, while psychology is hung-up on the intentionalist, teleological biases of everyday language. Ideas must “pay rent” by being able to _do_ something.

Some of our goals include:
1. Mapping the [microeconomic dynamics](https://www.pfeilstor.ch/-97/selection-games) of social interaction.
2. Modeling the structural operation of institutions, as they connect to the dynamics of other superorganisms (animals, cells, etc).
3. Connecting the technical vocabularies and interpretive frames of currently isolated inexact science fields, e.g., organizational theory and dating app design. 
4. Pushing forward modern AI/ML language technologies with a [fresh perspective](https://crispy.substack.com/p/wireheading-is-a-teleological-misnomer) focused on how to imbue agents with a notion of [functional utility](https://crispychicken.cc/2020/11/21/functionally-pragmatic-salesmanship/) .

Finally, our community organizing and growth models differ meaningfully from Indie Thinkers. We have fashioned our community on the “[squad wealth](https://otherinter.net/research/squad-wealth/)” model—a close-knit group of researchers, growing slowly and deliberately, and attempting to maintain the personal relationships and intimacy of an early-stage startup—rather than a paywalled community model. We will have a public sandbox for open discussion, but there will be a “blood-brain barrier” of publication and cataloging to ensure even our decentralized commons can’t be co-opted early in its development. Instead, our community's “exports” will serve as its primary public value add, a combination of: new research findings, the development of open-source knowledge tools, and self-documentation of the governance and treasury process. These exports double as beacons that will eventually attract and funnel like-minded researchers into our community. 

## Funding request 

Our request is for $80k USD-equivalent. 

$40k would be used to build technical infrastructure: we believe we can build a powerful on-chain, decentralized discourse and review ecosystem—half-forum, half-document publisher—that could one day rival traditional academic mechanisms. Our team includes engineers from blockchain startups, academia, FAANG, and Edgeware itself. We hope to fork Commonwealth's forum, streamline it for our community goals, and begin populating the site—paving the way for on-chain decentralized research publication and organization. As we begin publishing, we plan to build on top of our fork, providing and open-sourcing necessary infrastructure for deep knowledge work, such as on- and off-chain citation, document, annotation, and reputation systems. These tools can be integrated into the architecture of the Commonwealth forum, or any other Edgeware project that might desire to.

$40k would be earmarked for a low-friction micro-grant system that would sponsor research, field work, and documentation of our community-building process. Edgeware’s governance modules would provide a mechanism for community members to democratically award research proposals, so that only projects which are truly valuable to other members' work will get funded. As high-contributing members accumulate tokens, they in turn gain a higher stake in decision-making—a system resembling aspects of [futarchy](https://mason.gmu.edu/~rhanson/futarchy.html). Such grants would make the community's intellectual output more sustainable, while helping tempt Game-A refugees onto the platform to participate in shared discourse and build new intellectual frameworks. This money would provide for ≈60x micro-grants, ranging from approximately $250-1500 per project. For a community we expect to grow to 30 active members, this would constitute around 18-24 months of research funding and community growth.

    * _Estimated completion_: Winter 2021

    * _Estimated completion_: Spring 2022
