“The question, then, is whether the dispute resolution problem can be more effectively solved through blockchain-based mechanisms.” Researchers Darcy W. E. Allen, Aaron Lane, and Marta Poblet from RMIT University explore some pioneering smart contract solution methods in their recently published paper [“The Governance of Blockchain Dispute Resolution”](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3334674). Smart contracts have created new types of disputes, usually related to their automatic execution and human error in design. These novel conflicts require re-designed resolution mechanisms. At the same time, the RMIT researchers make the compelling case that smart contracts could also serve as alternative dispute resolution tools for challenges outside of the crypto ecosystem. 

The paper offers both a framework for blockchain-based institutional dispute resolution primitives, which they term the Dispute Resolution Possibility Frontier (DRPF), as well as case studies of current blockchain-based solution mechanisms. One case study looks at Jury.Online, which offers a dispute resolution process that relies on the random selection of autonomous, anonymous jurors and judges from an expert pool. Kleros presents a similar system with its Crowdsourced Online Dispute Resolution (CODR) mechanism. 

Another case looks at the dispute avoidance system of Mattereum, which puts assets in control of an automated “custodian” to ensure contract execution and relies on a technically knowledgeable mediator should further issues arise. The researchers also describe the LTO platform’s Ricardian live contract, which differs from a smart contract in that the contract itself does not autonomously execute, but instead prepares the infrastructure for execution and allows for off-chain changes.

The potential in blockchain-based dispute resolution mechanisms for challenges internal and external to the crypto ecosystem is still relatively unexplored, which makes the DRPF framework and the concrete examples offered here incredibly valuable.
