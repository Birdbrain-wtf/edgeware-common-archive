I’ve talked about [DAO verticals](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/106-possibilities-for-potential-daos), [DAO governance primitives](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/140-organizational-governance-mechanisms-for-blockchains), and [DAO exit strategies](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/147-designing-dao-primitives-the-exit-mechanism). I’ve also referenced how [unique crowdfunding tools](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/101-crowdsourcing-the-public-good-dominant-assurance-contracts), [reputation mechanisms](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/129-leveraging-reputation-with-blockchain), and [curation markets or bonding curves](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/142-tests-and-opportunities-for-curation-markets)  could be applied to building a DAO. My enthusiasm and focus on seeding DAOs and experimenting with different models is in response to a lot of excitement in the blockchain ecosystem for this type of smart contract-enabled org. Despite all this excitement, it seems as though experimentation has been hamstrung a bit by a lack of coordination and contextualization with the current DAO space. In light of this, it could be hugely effective for the advancement of this type of organization to test scientifically what sorts of decision-making frameworks and governance tools work best for different types of DAOs. 

The way to do this? Start with testing governance primitives for DAOs with one common goal - and seed 100k DAOs to run the experiment. 

The most exciting part? We could do this with Edgeware. 

Maybe 100k DAOs is pushing it, but community members *could* work collectively to create many unique DAOs that experiment with: 

- Liquid democracy
- Holographic consensus/prediction markets
- Majority vote
- Plurality vote (40%)
- Plurality vote (30%)
- ...and any other consensus mechanism you can think of. 

For experiment's sake, we could decide that these DAOs are only allowed to make decisions on investing in DeFi. We could potentially make it a blind experiment by introducing a mechanism that lets people participate in a DAO without knowing what decision-making framework is supporting it. We could go even further and mirror one individual’s decisions in one DAO across all of the DAOs, and see what the different results are. An algorithm could be designed to identify which decision-making frameworks are the most successful, and merge or fork different DAOs until the subset of most successful DAOs is eventually whittled down to one (or more likely, a handful). 

One caveat might be that this type of experiment does not account for the fact that you need people to partake in the DAO for the decision-making framework to be successful, and if you mirror everybody interactions across all the DAOs in the experiment, you don’t get a chance to test which sort of decision-making frameworks drive organic engagement, or what the optimal decision-making framework is for differently sized participant groups (but of course, those could be different experiments). 

This is something that we have a unique opportunity to try. Is anybody interested in working together to craft a formal proposal for this experiment?
