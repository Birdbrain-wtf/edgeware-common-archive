Check out what caught our eye in the crypto ecosystem this past week!

- [What kind of assets are cryptocurrencies: an empirical evaluation](https://www.docdroid.net/Ge5FCUS/macro-wtf-slides-nc.pdf): Nic Carter of Castle Island Ventures explores the taxonomy of cryptocurrencies within the variety of payment systems, revealing interesting contrasting and relating abilities between them.

- [Global Protests Reveal Bitcoin’s Limitations](https://www.coindesk.com/global-protests-reveal-bitcoins-limitations): Leigh Cuen has called this article her most important piece of journalism this year. Get insight on how “protests in Hong Kong, Lebanon and Iran have forced cypherpunks to test censorship-resistant technologies in the wild” - and why it isn’t working. 

- [cTokens vs iTokens](https://medium.com/totle/ctokens-vs-itokens-d7d0186da3c0): A new category of tokens increase in value as demand for lending on another token increases. Explore two examples, iTokens and cTokens — For example, cTokens represent proof that one has contributed some value to a lending pool - and deserves some interest on that work.

- [The Future of Trust](https://a16z.com/2019/11/12/the-end-of-centralization-and-the-future-of-trust/): “In this talk from Summit 2018, Crypto Deal Partner Ali Yahya explains how cryptonetworks could lead to the end of centralization and the beginning of a new era of trust and human cooperation. You can watch a video of Ali’s talk or read the transcript with slides below.”

- ["The Sovereign Individual" Investment Thesis](https://philjbonello.substack.com/p/the-sovereign-individual-investment): New technologies and tools are supporting the existence of a ‘sovereign individual,’ but targeting these businesses and projects for investment remains challenging.  Phil Bonello offers his investment thesis on this vision of the future.

- [Hard Problems in Cryptocurrency: Five Years Later](https://vitalik.ca/general/2019/11/22/progress.html): In 2014, Vitalik Buterin identified 16 hard problems for the cryptocurrency space in the areas of cryptography, consensus theory, and economics. Today, he revisits those problems and sets out new hard problems for 2019.

- [The #NoFork Movement, OR The Real Reason Why We Need DAOs](https://hackernoon.com/the-nofork-movement-dab036v8) As Grace Rachmany writes, “DAO technology today is focused on choosing a course of action, rather than the process that leads to choosing that course of action.” Her call for a #nofork movement suggests DAO developers should be asking a whole different set of questions than they’re asking now.

- [Data Tokens 1: Data Custody](https://blog.oceanprotocol.com/data-tokens-1-data-custody-1d0d5ae66d0c): Privacy and control over personal data are big topics in this space. Explore Data Tokens with Ocean Protocol, and founder Trent McConaghy’s framework for understanding hardware wallets, multi-sig wallets, and data DAOs as “variants of token-based data custody.”  

- [Contract Law Around the World](https://medium.com/cryptolawreview/contract-law-around-the-world-2bfb47adfa2d): CleanApp and Crypto Law Review offer a comparative chart on contract formation law. 

- [Simulation study of FPC](https://blog.iota.org/simulation-study-of-fpc-5b9e8b5a6910): Iota Foundation has recently released a paper on their Fast Probabilistic Consensus Simulator, in which they “identify the optimal amount of randomness for various Byzantine attack strategies on different kinds of network topologies.”
