## Takeaways

- Evolving blockchain-based marketplaces offer a chance to escape the problems of centralized financial systems and create novel DeFi techniques - including new valuation models for cryptocurrencies
- Traditional models for valuing assets could potentially be effective for legally compliant, for-profit DAOs. But other models are needed for DAOs with different goals and cryptos that are not meant to be used in the same way traditional assets are. 
- While several models have been introduced in recent years, there is still a need for crypto-valuation models that weigh the utility, scarcity, and perceived value of coins with diverse functions.

### What are some valuation models for cryptocurrencies?

The advent of blockchain tech has led to unique markets for cryptocurrencies, crypto-games, digital collectibles, and [curation schemes](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/142-tests-and-opportunities-for-curation-markets), among others. **These evolving marketplaces offer a chance to escape the problems of centralized financial systems and require the creation of novel DeFi techniques - including new valuation models for cryptocurrencies.** 

Traditional models for valuing assets include a Price/Earnings ratio, a Discounted Cash Flow, and the Gordon Growth Model (Liquid). Given that these models are designed for companies with stock and cash flow, they probably would only be applicable in a for-profit DAO - which may be a type of organization we see more and more of with efforts to offer [legal frameworks for compliant DAOs](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/155-what-types-of-legally-compliant-daos-are-there). 

**But what about organizations that are not for-profit? Or cryptos that are not meant to be used as a security or in this traditional way, but instead as a utility token?** 

ARK Investments, one of the first groups to formally value cryptocurrencies, used a model describes by the equation MV = PQ, where M = Size of the asset base; V = Velocity of the asset; P = Price of the digital resource being provisioned; and Q = Quantity of the digital resource being provisioned. Bloomberg has previously tried an approach using Factor Analysis, where they valued coins based on Size, Quality, and Service (Liquid).

Clearblocks has suggested that the value of cryptos can be assessed using the Network Value-to-Transactions (NVT) ratio and the Price-to-Metcalfe Ratio (PMR), based on the premise that early adoption stage networks can be valued the same way digital communication networks are (Clearblocks). 

Another, more radical model that could be applied is [the Harberger Tax system](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/160-how-blockchains-could-change-the-tax-system-harberger-and-daos). This economic abstraction describes a system in which owners of assets self-assessing the value of their assets, which essentially lets them choose their tax payments. But at the same time, owners are subject to losing their assets should anybody pay them their specified price. 

## Application 

There is a need for valuation models that weigh utility, scarcity, and perceived value of cryptocurrencies, with an awareness that these coins may not have the same goals and functions of traditional currencies.
