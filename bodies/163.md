## Takeaways

- To see the sustainable development of blockchain ecosystems, we need to support ways for potential blockchain developers to learn about the space. 
- One way to support developers? Support blockchain-focused hackathons.
- Some key questions about how to do this include: What is the target demographic? Does the community agree that funding a hackathon is a worthwhile effort? How else can the blockchain space encourage exploration? 

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

As we think about how to [fund development tools for blockchains](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/95-how-to-fund-dev-tools-for-blockchains) and [sustaining open source development](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/116-sustaining-open-source-funding) in this space, there’s one key question that isn’t asked enough: who will be the future crypto software engineers? 

A big part of this question can be answered by asking, how do people learn to be blockchain developers? Many in the field now are autodidacts and have benefited hugely from community support. While this is something that makes engineers in the crypto ecosystem uniquely passionate, it may not be a sustainable way to see a long-term focus on blockchain development. 

That isn’t to say that the field isn’t growing. Looking at [Electric Capital](https://medium.com/@ElectricCapital/electric-capital-developer-report-h1-2019-7d836d68fecb)'s latest post on developer statistics across different networks in the blockchain ecosystem, it’s exciting to see that smart contracts, infrastructure, and DeFi ecosystems continued to gain full-time developers throughout 2018-2019. The executive summary for the report wraps up by stating that the “Overall crypto ecosystems are approaching the size of well known open source projects such as Apache, but still has plenty of space to grow.”

The conclusion is indicative of the most striking part of the stats. Yes, the ecosystem is growing, and away from just cryptocurrency applications of blockchain tech. But the amount of developers working in this space still pales in comparison to an area like Machine Learning. 

The reason for this probably harks back to the first question. Major universities and educational programs have jumped to add courses and degrees focused specifically on Machine Learning. Blockchain, on the other hand, has few course offerings, and only a handful of related degrees - in fact, I was only able to find one, a Masters of Digital Currency at a university in Cyprus. Moreover, it’s only “top-tier” schools like Harvard and Johns Hopkins that are offering courses, and institutions like Columbia and Stanford that have been able to open blockchain research centers. You probably won’t see a blockchain-related course at your local college, as the topic is still very far from mainstream.

So, what’s my point? Basically, I think it’s safe to assume that if college students had more course offerings and exposure to blockchain tech, we would see more developers entering the field, offering a sustainable path forward. One possible way to do that could be with an Edgeware-funded hackathon: started, managed, and supported by this community!  

Interested community members could share their ideas and reasons for wanting to help facilitate the event. We could have votes to designate which of these people will be in charge and to figure out how to remunerate them. Then, it would be as simple as choosing a college town and renting a conference center, or possibly partnering with a university. Community votes could also allocate treasury funding for scholarships, and help design the hackathon program. 

## Application

The Edgeware community has a chance to use our network and our treasury to make a difference in the future of the entire blockchain ecosystem. How can we best do that? 

Remaining questions:

- Does the community agree that funding a hackathon is a worthwhile effort?
- Alternatively, is our sentiment that hackathons are past their heyday and this would just be a waste of money? Community input on this would be helpful, especially if we have any university students in our mix. 
- Are university students the right demographic to target? Is there another type of developer and/or another way to target them all together that you think would be a better use of time and energy?
- How else can the blockchain space encourage exploration?
