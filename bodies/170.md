“Privacy is necessary for an open society in the electronic age.” So begins “[A Cypherpunk’s Manifesto](https://www.activism.net/cypherpunk/manifesto.html),” written by Eric Hughes in 1993. The manifesto set off a movement that began percolating following works by early cryptographers like David Chaum: a movement to give the people control over their own identities and actions. The Cypherpunks gave us some of the foundational principles behind cryptocurrency, and their spirit has pushed us to think of even more global use cases for blockchain technology, from reinventing democracy to decentralizing the internet. 

Hughes’ manifesto points to the importance of building anonymous transaction systems, building code that protects the people, and a collaborative effort to realize the future of self-sovereignty that the Cypherpunks imagined. Since its publication, we’ve seen a virtual Cambrian explosion in cryptographic technology. As we continue down this rabbit hole, it feels significant that the words of one of the original fighters for blockchain-based freedom still ring true in many contexts: 

“We cannot expect governments, corporations, or other large, faceless organizations to grant us privacy out of their beneficence...we must defend our own privacy if we expect to have any.”
