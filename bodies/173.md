**What is holographic consensus?**

Holographic consensus, introduced originally by Matan Field of DAOstack, is an innovative governance idea that applies prediction markets to the issue of voting. Each proposal in an organization has a prediction market that indicates the likelihood that the proposal will pass in a community-wide vote. This mechanism is used to highlight proposals that the prediction markets indicate are most relevant to the community, which are moved to a “boosted queue” (Field).

The need to sort proposals is a result of the limited attention and voting ability inherent to any organization that sees a large number of proposals. If a DAO has 1,000 active proposals, for instance, there is no way that the entire community will be able to view and vote on each one. Thus, the name holographic consensus speaks to the fact that an ideal governance system would allow for a small amount of voting power to result in alignment with the global opinion of the organization. This makes a hologram, in which each pixel actually contains information for the entire picture, a good metaphor for the governance structure (Field). 

**Application**

Holographic consensus has been tested on DAOstack’s Genesis Protocol. Aragon has also built [a test implementation](https://blog.aragon.one/my-first-aragon-app-voting-supercharged-with-daostacks-holographic-consensus-part-4/) for a holographic consensus voting mechanism that could replace the regular voting mechanism in an Aragon dApp. dxDAO [reportedly](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/(https://blog.gnosis.pm/a-brief-discussion-of-the-protocol-governing-the-dxdao-7331407a2555) also uses a version of holographic consensus. 

This is a governance mechanism that is meant to facilitate organization scaling. As an organization sees more and more proposals, the community will need a reliable way to sort through them and find the relevant ones. However, an important question to ask is just how well the prediction markets can keep up with increasingly high numbers of proposals. 

Remaining questions:
- Given that is it meant to facilitate scaling, at what point does holographic consensus become relevant?
- How many people do you need to participate in the prediction markets to keep an updated and relevant boosted queue of proposals? 
- Are there other challenges holographic consensus systems face? Other reasons they are worth implementing?
