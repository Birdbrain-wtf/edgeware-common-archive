**Summary**
Edgeware was the first Substrate-based mainnet to launch. It deployed not only a smart contract chain but a set of palettes from Substrate that instantiated a council, a referenda body, an on-chain treasury, and several other features.

Since our inception, we've seen Edgeware as one large chain-dao, with the elected council, referenda, and the agency working together to develop the network.

This post argues that we can more effectively describe and manage our ecosystem through a more clear division in our chain-dao concept by focusing on the two aspects individually, analyzing our efforts, governance, and labor separately. 

# Edgeware-as-Chain

Edgeware's smart contract platform and blockchain is a highly technical, cutting-EDG product with evolving Ink!/Rust smart contracting and nascent but highly functional EVM support. With the upcoming Wako upgrade, we'll see a broad ability for Solidity contracts and projects to launch, extend and migrate to EDG.
The process of upgrading to Wako however, has revealed a deep need for technical ownership and leadership beyond what the Agency is currently able to provide. Previously, I'd opened a discussion on an offering of percentage EDG to teams that would effectively develop and sustain EDG's broad functionality with a focus on platform growth and use. Instead, I am proposing that we activate a third governing body: the Technical Committee/Council, which we see active in KSM and DOT governance.

"The Technical Committee can, along with the Council, produce emergency referenda, which are fast-tracked for voting and implementation. These are used for emergency bug fixes or rapid implementation of new but battle-tested features into the runtime.
Fast-tracked referenda are the only type of referenda that can be active alongside another active referendum. Thus, with fast-tracked referenda, it is possible to have two active referendums at the same time. Voting on one does not prevent a user from voting on the other.

I propose that Technical Council(TC) members in EDG are voted via Referendum, instead of Council. Additionally, I believe that we should have clear commitment and requirements from candidates for Technical Committee, namely that the account belongs represents a team with significant stake-in-the-game on EDG's future. This might be a technical team with an undeployed product and EDG to bond instead as their stake, or a team with an active or deployed project. This will be a topic for later discussion.

The TC would focus and lead on the chain and technical functionalities of EDG. They would work together to make improvements, verify upgrades via SRTool, lead on infrastructure conversations and strategy, and help direct partnerships that enhance the technical offering of the EDG ecosystem. Today's council is currently underpowered for the kind of technical oversight we are going to require: a dedicated group will help solve this need.

# Edgeware-as-DAO

The DAO of Edgeware is instead focused on governance, participation, asset management, investment into teams building, partnerships with other communities, and an altogether different set of KPIs than the technical leadership. They are complimentary, absolutely- but the success of each side of the EDG coin is not wholly dependent on the other. As a result of this, I argue that the Council should instead focus on the leadership of the DAO.

It should target KPIs around governance, participation, and asset management. It should measure community, contributor retainment, and more. It should be responsible for ensuring that EDG holders are kept informed and have clear access to joining the EDG community through partnerships like exchanges and interfaces that improve our DAO operations. 

I believe there is significant talent and interest in DAO operations and participation, and we should not require a deep grasp of Substrate technical fundamentals to find talent for our DAO-side. 


## The Agency and our Marketing

The Agency's long-term goal was to 'decentralize itself' while improving awareness and growth of EDG.

As mentioned above, we approached this path previously through Ecosystem Partners and KPI-weighted % EDG goals, and now we are hoping that an additional council-body and helping to make clear expectations and contributions from both groups will help attain this goal. Simultaneously considering how we order our operations, and also this division in labor for the chain as a whole.

For the remainder of our first proposal, I am directing Agency staff to focus on one of these functions. For example, Depth Hoar will focus on chain and smart contract functionality, documentation, and support, and Cameron will focus on improving guidance and information on the technical operations of the DAO side, interfaces, and more.

We are also planning to revisit our marketing from the perspective of two separate stakeholder groups:

1. EDG Holders (who should become EDG DAO Members/Participants)
2. Builders, and users of the smart contract platform.


As these two groups are not fully overlapping, I am planning a division in content for our site to target and onboard these separately. This is not a major change, as our homepage already focuses heavily on non-technical interaction, we just want to make these two paths more clearly differentiated so we can welcome a wider range of participants to both.
