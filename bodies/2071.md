## **Edgeware’s focus on smart contracts**

Currently our smart contracts are able to be written and compiled through the use of both WASM and the EVM. However, as these technologies begin to mature, there seems to be a much greater shift towards Rust, and technologies that are built on top of Rust such as ink!. 

**Why Rust?**

We need a better virtual machine than the EVM (Ethereum Virtual Machine), and especially we need something that is more efficient. We chose [WebAssembly](https://webassembly.org/) (Wasm). This is a virtual machine specification that is intended to match the semantics of physical machines in the real world today, making it efficiently executable on modern hardware. It’s also built to be easily verifiable, not necessarily for logical correctness but for memory safety.

Majority of chains within the Polkadot / Substrate ecosystem are going to be using Rust for core development and all of them are going to be integrating upgrades with Substrate. I believe that in order to ensure optimal efficiency and congruence with the rest of the Polkadot ecosystem, it’s in our best interest to further pursue ink! And double down on our ink! smart contracts, heavily incentivizing developers that are building using ink! As opposed to Solidity for developing dApps specifically. Rust developers are significantly harder to come by, so if we foster a good environment for Rust developers to emerge, then it’ll be easier for Edgeware to be targeted for partnerships or collaborations due to our powerful presence of Rust developers that would be actively building on top of Edgeware. Due to the size and saturation of the Ethereum market, there are Solidity developers everywhere. Of course it’s going to be very beneficial to allow the Ethereum marketplace to pour into Edgeware, but we do not want to find ourselves lost in the sea of other ERC-20 chains that can do what we do even better because they’re native to Ethereum. In finding our stride, it’s important that we’re always standing out within the competition. Even if we put all of our time and effort into making the best EVM ecosystem possible, moonbeam will still be miles ahead of us. Once again, I am not trying to negate the importance of the EVM ecosystem on Edgeware, it adds versatility and additional reasons for anyone to adopt our platform, however, I do not think it should be the focal point of our efforts as it relates to the progression of our developer ecosystem.

**Why are rust developers so limited?**

Many developers today are using high-level programming languages like Python and JavaScript to do a lot of backend tasks due to innovative frameworks and infrastructure that takes the heavy lifting out of the equation. However, these tools are limited in their functionality because they are almost like pre-assembled functions or parts of a project that just need to be properly placed and adjusted in order to work properly which doesn’t require an immense amount of understanding. Rust is very similar to C++ and is designed in such a way that enables a very precise level of customization and structure to be built from scratch from the ground up. With that being said, there are not a lot of C++ developers readily available either, because majority are working in other fields like game development, or infrastructure building for large corporations.

**How do we attract rust developers?**

First and foremost, we need to have the tools necessary for Rust developers to be able to build on our platform, and build well. We need to have guides that can show perhaps a C++ developer how they can easily integrate into Rust step by step so it’s a very seamless process. This opens our doors to not only present Rust developers, but any programmers that are working with low level programming languages. We should begin producing visuals and videos which break down the relationship between Edgeware and Substrate as well videos covering ink! In depth so that developers that are not familiar with blockchain, or that are not familiar with Substrate can begin painting a mental picture of what to expect when building on top of this platform. 

## Growth Strategy

What we’re trying to best figure out is: “How do we incentivize people (developers) to build on our platform, and how do we get them to stay”? 

**Tools and support**

We want to build tools and utility on Edgeware that are 

1. So easy to use that a non-developer could learn how they work.
2. Extremely powerful and lightweight.
3. Very descriptive and elaborate, yet structured in a way that is able to foster a beginner and encourages progression through a clearly marked path.


Executing on those listed will be the foundation for what is necessary to attract the largest pool of developers, and provide the retention needed to see consistent activity in our builders and support channels. We want to make it very difficult for a developer to choose an alternative chain solely due to the level of complexity associated with getting something up and running on our platform.

From the developer experience perspective, we want to limit the number of roadblocks developers might run into. We do this by testing our documentation and tools to continue to make sure our walkthroughs are easy to follow, and bug-free. Whenever experiencing issues with our documentation, or something is unclear, make sure you make an issue in the GitHub repository, or add a pull request. Especially if it relates to setting up certain dependencies for tutorials so we can add it to the respective troubleshooting section.

**Parachain-like leasing model for dApps (Planning Phase)**

By exercising our DAO, and by integrating a new Edgeware-funded decentralized application (dApp) model, we can create a system similar to how parachains are chosen for a relay chain, and apply the same principles for funding decentralized applications built on top of Edgeware.

**Qualifications for dApp auctions:**

- Application needs to be built on top of Edgeware.


- Application needs to fit within one of the requested categories from the DAO. 


- Submissions from applicants need to be provided prior to voting period, and will be viewable on the commonwealth forums.


**At a glance**

Applicants will be competing for a ‘lease spot’ Where they will be voted in by the community. This lease spot is only available to decentralized applications that are built within the parameters of the listed qualifications. These qualified participants will then be picked out of a pool of other participants that are competing for the same lease spot. There will only be a limited, specified number of lease spots available that are going to be able to be community funded by the treasury.

**Voting period**

The voting session will take place and last for a predetermined amount of time. Once voting has concluded, the applicant with the most votes will then be chosen as the ‘king of the hill’ for the current cycle, and will be added to the list of Edgeware funded dApps chosen by the community.

**Chosen applicant**

The chosen applicant will immediately receive funding upon conclusion of the voting period. Once voted in, the chosen applicant can choose to remain in a funded lease spot by ‘maintaining support’, or they can choose to no longer receive funding and opt out of their lease spot. By maintaining support, a project can choose to apply for another funding cycle. This must be approved by the DAO. If the vote is rejected, the funded project will no longer be funded in the following cycle and will have to resubmit as a new applicant in a future cycle.

**Repeat**

After a cycle has concluded, a new cycle will start up and the admissions process will begin once again. This gives new applicants an opportunity to receive funding from the treasury.

**Lease limit reached (trial by fire)**

Once the specified lease slot amount has been filled, and a new project has successfully been admitted into a lease slot, the pre-existing lease participants must undergo a voting phase in which the participant with the least backed votes will be removed from the next cycle, and replaced with the recently chosen lease participant. This phase is called ‘trial by fire’.

**Recap**

This process is going to enable a highly aggressive and consistent growth structure for Edgeware’s development ecosystem. This could potentially be integrated into a bi-weekly or monthly hackathon where other exciting community events will also take place, and seed new ideas. All of this helps to drive Edgeware’s adoption by creating an environment that is receiving the proper input, and nutrients necessary for organic growth. These teams will be able to build meaningful projects that are driven by competition and scarcity. Due to the limited supply of lease spots, and the incentivization of funding through our agency, we will have teams of developers that are going to be interested in securing a spot on Edgeware especially as Edgeware comes into the Polkadot ecosystem as a Parachain. The consistent nature of these dApp auctions is going to generate a lot of involvement, engagement and anticipation from the community as new projects are being added to the lease pool.

**What's next**

Following our Agency meeting this upcoming Monday and our builders call this Tuesday at 2PM EST in our Discord, a final draft for the '**Parachain-like leasing model for dApps' **with updated specifications will be submitted to commonwealth as a proposal before Friday, October 1st, 2021. If approved, an updated post will be made in our dev-digest weekly article pointing to commonwealth explaining in-depth how this process will play out.
