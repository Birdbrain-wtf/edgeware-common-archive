### Summary

We have no issues with the WithdrawDAO signal as it was a case of open contract participation.

Since we can reasonably believe the original signaler intends for the allocated EDG to be disused, the community may decide to do something with the balance.

### **Background**

On July 2nd, 2019, Nick Johnson, the deployer of a contract called the WithdrawDAO signaled roughly ~123,000 ETH in the lockdrop.

Shortly afterward, he published the ETH private key for the contract deployer account, which allowed anyone to repeat his signal. He also announced he had destroyed his only copy of the EDG private key he had signaled:

> I used the account that deployed the DAO Withdrawal contract to signal on Edgeware. (...)

> I've deleted the only copy I had of the Edgeware private key. If Edgeware credits the account, those funds will now be locked permanently. As it's impossible to prove I really did delete the key, I encourage them not to credit it (or anyone else signaling for that matter)*

> I'm hereby publishing the private key for the account that deployed the DAO withdrawal contract: D6C8ACE470AB0CE03125CAC6ABF2779C199D21A47D3E75E93C212B1EC23CFE51.*

From https://twitter.com/nicksdjohnson/status/1146018815836164096

### Handling additional WithdrawDAO signals****

The lockdrop process took a snapshot of all signaled ETH balances at 00:00 UTC Sept 1. Signaled ETH accounts were checked *at that time only,* preventing users from shuffling ETH around, signaling, and shuffling again to a new address.

If multiple signals map the same ETH to different Edgeware addresses, the lockdrop process must decide which **EDG address should receive the allocation for that signal. This was not defined during the lockdrop process, because the code for creating a genesis spec had not been written.

Commonwealth decided to allow an ETH address to only be signaled **once. In this case, later signals would be ignored and only the first signal from the WithdrawDAO, from Nick Johnson, would be processed. The EDG that will be allocated to Johnson's EDG account would supposedly be lost because he destroyed his EDG private key (although this cannot be confirmed).

### No changes

A principle of the lockdrop has always been open participation—not blacklisting this or any addresses. As a result, Commonwealth has not blacklisted the address.

At the conclusion of the lockdrop, the address was assigned approximately 0.65% of genesis EDG (32 million EDG). Since it was a signaled address, 80% of that balance was also locked for one year.

### **Community Proposals**

That said, the EDG allocated through the WithdrawDAOs signal isn't to be ignored, as the tweet above asserts. Here are several some appropriate post-launch steps that it seems like the community may consider: 

**Draft a post-genesis referendum to allocate the WithdrawDAO EDG to a separate DAO, or to the Edgeware Treasury.**

The benefit of simply redirecting these to the Treasury is that no additional structures, coordination schemes, or membership classes are required to manage the seized EDG, and they leverage the existing more-fair token distribution instead of requiring analysis and execution on a new DAO power distribution.

**Pre-genesis, the genesis configuration hardcodes a freeze or otherwise makes illiquid the EDG at that address for 90 days.** 

Given that the community cannot prove whether or not the corresponding EDG private has been destroyed, this option is combined with others, simply providing time to make a more firm decision about the EDG in this sort of case.
