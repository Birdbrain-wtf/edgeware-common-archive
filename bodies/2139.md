## Preparing for takeoff -- Dev Experience (⅔)

What we’re trying to best figure out is: **“How do we incentivize people (developers) to build on our platform, and how do we get them to stay”?** The latter of that question is more important in Edgeware's current state. Developer retention is very important as we do not want our ability to bring in developers to be suppressed by a poor **‘Developer Experience’**. This post is to highlight the new initiatives that are currently in motion towards transforming the developer experience on Edgeware into something one-of-a-kind to empower **[Edgeware’s mission](https://edgewa.re/mission)** of serving as a platform for the developers, creators, and members of its community to build, fund and manage their projects effectively.

We want to **provide tools and services on Edgeware** so that:

- Someone **brand new to developing** or someone with **little working knowledge** can begin working with our tools and learn new concepts and patterns to then produce something useful. Edgeware can bridge the developer gap to other tech industries, and learning on Edgeware could be the equivalent of getting an education in blockchain technology from one of the few [elite universities](https://www.technologyhq.org/8-top-universities-that-offer-blockchain-education/) that currently offer any education on the matter. This is can be attributed to the rate at which we’re able to provide new information and support for the latest technologies as soon as they release. The weight of this could be comparable to that of a [CompTIA certification](https://www.comptia.org/certifications/comptia-advanced-security-practitioner) as our merit increases. This would simply be a by-product of the incredible focus necessary to provide an ample suite of tools and resources that will encompass even the most abstract areas of knowledge.


- They are **easy to work with**, **do not require the latest hardware to run**, and are **extremely good at completing tasks** needed to proceed in an efficient and timely manner. This would mean testing the software, tools, and environments involved in developing on Edgeware through regular use-case scenarios and strategic attempts at finding potential user errors to provide additional helping steps in our ‘troubleshooting’ sections. To recap, tools that are extremely powerful and lightweight through frequent testing and revisions.


- **Developers of any caliber can contribute** in ways that are most aligned with their current level of understanding. From the use of **clearly categorized guides** for different levels of understanding to **infographics that provide elaborate detail for more visual learning**. Edgeware has the capacity to become a hub in which developers are regularly referred to obtain a breadth of knowledge in a neatly packaged subsystem of learning.


Executing on the following points above will be the foundation for what is necessary to attract the **largest pool of developers**, and provide the retention needed to see consistent activity in our builders and support channels. Consistent discussion in these sub-channels will bring more attention from investors and general enthusiasts. discussion provokes ideas, ideas further generate interest and increase the depth of understanding among engaged participants of the discussions. 

This is how you create a network of **like-minded developers** from which specialized contributions as a community can enable a relatively similar **macro-goal** to be realized and in turn produce the anticipated reward for everyone.

We’re going to continue communicating with complete transparency and listen to the concerns and requests from the community at large. Evaluation of the latest software and consistent refinement in our existing software will continue in the background as an attempt to ‘widen the margin’ with competing chains.

We are keen on offering the support and resources necessary when roadblocks/questions arise. Whenever experiencing issues with our documentation, or something is unclear, make sure you make an issue/pull request in the respective [GitHub repository](https://github.com/edgeware-network) or voice your concerns in one of our many mediums of communication including [Discord](https://discord.gg/zdFJm4gA5M), [Commonwealth](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/discussions/wg-builders), and [Telegram](https://t.me/edg_developers).
