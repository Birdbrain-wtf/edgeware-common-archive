There was a discussion the #proposals channel a few days ago, after Thom suggested that we write a bounty to speed up the Wako upgrade, which was very contentious and resulted in the suggestion that I write up some of my responses and takeaways in a document here.

We have arrived at a point where governance discussions are often unproductive. Edgeware can continue functioning in spite of this disagreement (and we are on track to launch EVM within the next few weeks). But this is after many disagreements over the last 1+ year of the network's operations:

- How should we fund core development? (proposal by Dillon, passed, then loudly opposed by remz)
- Should we pursue a Polkadot bond?
- Should we establish an ecosystem partner grant structure?
- Should we establish a technical council?
- Should we focus on EVM or WASM development?


All of these questions had significant disagreements, and I think these disagreements have root causes:

**Lack of a clarity on what Edgeware is providing as a platform**

We've presented many different visions for what Edgeware is. An on-chain incubator, a venture community, a smart contract chain for funding media projects — all of these seem like they do not capture the entire picture.

It's okay that we don't have a single vision (Ethereum never has) but it is problematic that we do not have a way to measure success. We often guide teams in different directions, suggesting to them that WASM is the future, but the WASM platform is also immature and we do not at all have the technical staff to support applications building on it. There seems to be a growing understanding that EVM is a platform we will support going into the future — but Drew and I have made most of the contributions there, and we're increasingly less involved in governance. Can we assure that it will continue to be maintained?

RESOLUTION: We should agree upon a technical strategy, that major members of the network all sign off on, that articulates what we're providing to developers. Likely this is support for the EVM and WASM contract module, maybe bridges. Every part of this platform should be something that we have the resources to staff.

**Inconsistent communication & technical strategy led by non-technical members**

It seems like communication between EDG councillors and core maintainers is inconsistent, and we have often pushed messaging around the future of the network without deep knowledge of the technical implications. When we say that WASM is the future, have we deployed WASM contracts and looked deeply into its feasibility as a platform? Similarly, do we understand the economics of parachain bonding?

In many cases, it's unclear what terms builders are working on when they propose to the chain. I know Drew proposed the Webb EVM upgrade somewhat reluctantly and spent a lot of time on it, and I've had to follow through on it because I consider it important that we ship this, but there still hasn't been a consistent agreement going forward for how all this tech will be maintained. I'm open to continuing to play a role, but the communications infrastructure and internal coordination needs to improve.

RESOLUTION: I recommend making a private, gated builders chat inside the Edgeware Discord. This would be open to councillors, core developers, and other long-term contributors on the network (not app devs, not stakers) and I don't think it would hurt the existing builders channel, since that will remain a critical touchpoint for validators and app devs.

**Crypto-NIMBYism**

Some members have spoken poorly of other projects, including Zora or Astar, touting that Edgeware "has more decentralized governance experience" or suggesting that Zora is going about media wrong. In the same way, Commonwealth was attacked for its motives and ethics after being the largest contributor to the network. Sometimes we've implied that EDG is somehow purer or better than other projects because of our decentralized distribution. Figuring out decentralized governance is pretty important but we're far from having done that. Let's please avoid these kinds of attacks, internal or external, in the future. It is a big part of why I've been less involved in governance (and same for others).

By all means, we haven't delivered that much over the last year — and some the projects that were supposed to build on EDG now have larger market caps than EDG since they've moved to other chains.

**Unclear shared values**

Councillor mrbulb said in a tweet that he was looking for crypto networks where users aren't motivated by incentives (money). Those networks exist today; they're projects like FWB that rely on a core of values that are shared among members. We do not have that here. Finding those shared values will be important to Edgeware working as a network.

"We gave away 90% of the network to lockdrop participants so now we have a decentralized community" may be fertile ground for other people to propose their values, but it's not a shared vision. It seems clear that none of the proposals for more specific visions have stuck — Edgeware as incubator, Edgeware as crypto social chain, and Edgeware as media chain all don't seem to be our focus.

It seems like where we are headed is "Edgeware is an EVM chain with active governance, and a proposal system that will fund public goods/proposals up to ~$50k". That's a promising starting point, and it needs more articulation before it can be considered the core of our values. For example:

- Do we care a lot about "community consensus" / "community approval" for projects?
- What do we think about Ethereum?
- What do we think about Polkadot?
- We can also ask questions of individual members:
- Why does Edgeware appeal to you / why did you join the community?
- What kinds of projects would you like to see built on Edgeware?
- What kinds of funding decisions would you like to see made?


**Forming a 1p1v governance committee**

Sometimes we'll disagree on values in a way that can't be easily reconciled. I think the idea of a solochain with public goods funding without a focus on value-maximizing is inherently Etherean, which means have to decide to align ourselves with the Ethereum community or be the Etherean chain on Polkadot.

There are difficult decisions to make, and there's no way we can resolve them using our current structure, which is coin voting + the council + community calls. We'll need a different political structure to be able to make this decisions, and it seems pretty clear to me that this should be a 1-person-1-vote entity open to anyone who has contributed to the Edgeware network since its inception. **I consider this the single highest priority/most key thing that we could do next; however, if we try to do it, we must do it well.**

(I am working on a tool to do this on other networks, however, the focus is other EVM DAOs and there wouldn't even be one for Edgeware to plug in right now. We also don't need the tool to be able to poll the DAO; just Snapshot is enough.)

There will likely be some disagreement about who should be let in the committee. That's the main issue; if we can resolve it, then it should be a valuable resource for everyone going forward.

It's possible that whales oppose this. If that's really the case, I think it should continue anyway. If the vision is something other than what whales believe, then the community's decision should win and we can figure out how to handle that later.

Whatever the vision is, if we can agree on it, we should make it so that what each chain contractor / community member is doing fits into that vision, and delivers something which is clearly responsive to it. "Delivering value" should be about advancing what the Edgeware community believes in, rather than increasing the value of EDG.
