Two big announcements:

- We are planning for Edgeware 1.0 mainnet to launch on February 12, 2020.
- We'll also release a major update to Commonwealth at that time — including new functionality around governance, funding, and community organization.


From the beginning, Edgeware has been about building a [crypto network with better governance](https://medium.com/commonwealth-labs/meet-edgeware-25e881e4a37e), and we've always believed that open governance can make a blockchain successful. By our definition, that means building a system that allows all kinds of stakeholders to participate, whether they're large or small, technical or non-technical.
Doing this well will let us build a new kind of decentralized network, which has a much larger audience of developers and users. We think this change will be transformative, the same way Git and Github were transformative for the world of open source.

To illustrate, here are a few goals we want to achieve:
### One system to capture, discuss, and fund new projects & improvements
Blockchain projects have been experimenting with community-funded treasury systems for many years (Decred and Dash were some of the first). But we think such a system needs to be as friendly and rich as a social network to reach its full potential.
Here's how we imagine it working:

- A project might begin its life as a single tweet, which gets saved in a thread for further discussion. Different stakeholders can contribute to the thread by asking questions or providing feedback; this process will identify potential points of collaboration, and help work through tradeoffs.
- As the discussion matures, councillors and stakeholders can signal their support for funding the idea through a treasury proposal, which can then be voted upon and disbursed within the UI.
- While this happens, we'll let developers build their profiles, establish their areas of expertise, and indicate their level of interest in different projects. Sometimes, this will involve deeply integrating with Twitter and other existing social platforms (e.g. embedding past tweets about projects and ideas). At other times, it will mean designing our own mechanisms (e.g. anonymous chats for feedback on proposals, or surveys that help identify where a contributor might be most effective).

Having all this in one place will help Edgeware identify opportunities where the combination of a simple initial idea, a motivated developer, and a small amount of capital can turn into a sustainable project — the recipe for successes like Ethereum's Uniswap.
### A **microfunding mechanism**
We‘re working on a Quora/Twitter-like interface for deploying small amounts of EDG towards individual contributors’ ideas. It should be super easy (and fun!) for a contributor to jump into a tool like Ink Playground to prototype a quick smart contract or interface; we think it's a good idea to encourage that using a funding mechanism as well. Got an idea? We'll back it with some tokens, so you don't need to go through an exchange.
We'd love to deploy a meaningful amount of EDG through this mechanism to get developers and contributors started, whether it's to build a smart contract, provide feedback on how an existing system works, or just expand on a promising idea.
### Building upon existing ideas
The community has already proposed many interesting ideas and collaborations: bridges to other ecosystems, DeFi infrastructure for Polkadot, and funding DAOs that stretch across crypto and into the real world. We’re excited to use Edgeware to seed some of these projects which already have backers and potential contributors.
### **Edgeware launch**
Last but certainly not least, here are our launch plans for Edgeware:

- We will finalize a genesis file and merge it to master sometime next week. We'll post a short technical update around then.
- We'll launch with open validator slots, which means block production will be slow for the first day or two while the network comes online. After several days, most slots should be filled and the chain should have stabilized.
- We are upgrading Testnet v0.99 to include the new Kusama identity and nicknames module, and upgrading Commonwealth to support them too. This will make establishing persistent names on the network much easier.
- We are working hard on documentation, and will update the Edgeware website with new guides shortly.


In the short term, we think there will be solid momentum from announcements like Bison Trails' recent integration of Edgeware, and upcoming guides to deploying contracts and using the chain, and launches of new governance features on Commonwealth. Additionally, we're keeping a close eye on other networks' governance, and will have more to share on how Edgeware can learn from them.

Once launch happens, we'll start using Commonwealth's governance and funding mechanisms in a real environment. The first council will be elected over approximately a month, and the on-chain treasury will begin accumulating funds, which can be used to support technical projects as well as BD deals and partnerships.
Finally, many projects have been interested in connecting to Edgeware, so we're looking forward to more developments there.

That's all we have for today. Look forward to our next few technical updates, and see you all on February 12!
