Hi folks, I am new here - but have been lurking in your Discord and perusing this discussion forum for some weeks now.

Firstly, a little about me. I run the final year IT product development program at a major university in Melbourne Australia that works with about 2000 students a year, working on 50+ products. In addition to this I am also deep down the rabbit hole of DeFi, blockchain and was an early participant in the Polkadot ICO (which makes up my largest holding). So I have some interest in Substrate based projects (disclaimer).

Being down in little old Australia, we are quite a long way away from the blockchain epicentres of Berlin/Europe, SV and places like Singapore etc. In my opinion we have been frustratingly slow to adopt and understand the importance of blockchain technologies. Over the past couple of years I have been working with academic colleagues to introduce some blockchain units to our curriculum, which is great, and course directors are starting to realise how big this space is and so there is now talk of adding more units for students to take up. We have obviously started with Solidity basics like everyone else, however Substrate has piqued the interest of some of my colleagues as we would like to get more RUST based programming in our courses.

One of the most difficult challenges with building curriculum in this space is that most academics are not up to date with the content, and most of their research and teaching has been in Web 1/2 technologies. There is a quite a steep learning curve for them - and quite often they get some of the basics wrong in the materials they develop, which is obviously not good when we are teaching! 

I'll be frank, some of the discussions here suggest to me that Edgeware is at a bit of an inflection point. It seems the protocol is unsure about its core purpose and how it fits into the larger Substrate eco-system. Its clear to me though that there are some talented developers and blockchain brains here, it just seems like there is a difference in opinion on a few things, and not a unifying long term mission that allows all the other things to branch off of. 

I am curious, why not focus on education? There are young brains in universities around the world studying IT, Comp Sci, Software Eng etc that are completely unaware of Web3 and all its glory. I know this because I mentor a number of students in this space, and when they start to "get it" you can see the excitement as they start to understand what they can do and build in this space. 

Why couldn't Edgeware have teams of students around the world working on decentralised capstone projects, and participating in workshops and hackathons using Substrate? Edgeware could be an experimental melting pot, and proving ground for up and coming Substrate developers testing their skills in a supportive environment, mentored by experienced Substrate developers. 

If Kusama, Polkadot and their smart contract parachains like Acala/Karura, Moonbeam/Moonriver, Astar/Shiden and Major/Minor League for Dapps and web3 initiatives, why can't Edgeware be Secondary School/High School for learning and deploying projects, breaking stuff, and learning. All the while Edgeware would also be able to identify up and coming talent that could also contribute to core development on Edgeware. 

I could envisage a future also, where IT Schools are also running their own Edgeware validators and contributing to network security. We could be teaching students how to manage these complex and unique networks. 

All of this would require Edgeware to support learning and education, so that would require time and effort. But I think it would be beneficial for Edgeware because it would be funnelling students into your ecosystem, and by extension the entire Dotsama ecosystem - but in a more tailored environment for learning. Web3 development is unforgiving. Buggy code and getting incentives wrong can cause failscades in projects, and often unlike Web2 development you can't release alpha, beta, then final version. It has to be perfect before mainnet deployment. I think this is where Edgeware can really help.
