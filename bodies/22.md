1. **What is your experience as a validator or node operator? What other protocols are you validating on right now, if any?**

The team at Commonwealth Labs has been setting up and debugging all Edgeware testnets as well as issues related to the `edgeware-node`. We have been participating as validators on Edgeware testnets for every testnet that has existed. ;)

2. **What's your technical setup? (cloud vs. on-premise; software stack; key management)**

The team at Commonwealth labs has maintained at least 10 validating nodes at any given point configured with either 2GB/1CPU or 4GB/2CPU nodes. We have also started using 8GB swap files for each of these nodes to augment the memory capabilities, given the software can still be made more efficient. We are using the cloud provider Vultr to run all these nodes. Each node is configured with the Ubuntu 18.04 operating system.

3. **How did you learn about Edgeware? Why are you interested in validating?**

Commonwealth Labs helped develop the software to perform the lockdrop for Edgeware; we also developed the first `edgeware-node` implementation in Rust. We largely became interested in the network to further [experimentation in blockchain governance](https://medium.com/commonwealth-labs/meet-edgeware-25e881e4a37e). Once the network goes live, we'd love to continue supporting the network by validating and continuing to evolve the protocol. We want to validate for a few reasons:
- We want to understand the incentives for validators at a deeper level than we can get just by acting as developers. We think this will help us build better governance software + systems for network funding (e.g. DAOs where validators donate rewards in return for other governance tokens)
- As we are responsible for the first `edgeware-node` implementation, we want to stand behind our software.
- We believe in the Edgeware Network  and it's place within the Polkadot Ecosystem!

4. **What are your positions on issues that governance will be expected to handle? For example:**

Further lockdrop or airdrop to holders of other assets:

- We think that any further lockdrop to holders of other assets (like DOTs) should be processed through a `democracy` vote or `treasury` spend proposal. The proposal will need to include either a roadmap of how payouts will be handled or an implementation of a DAO/module that can sustain a distribution of tokens to participants with those specfic assets.

Genesis distribution (contract signalling, generalized lock)

 - It is interesting to see how our decisions made originally have been treated in the past year or so. We started with locking, and to garner a larger community, we added different options for participating, including signaling by users, and then for contract signaling, and finally the generalized lock. We've always done this with an aim towards experimentation. Now that the network is extremely near launch, we're excited to see the governance decide whether the correct decisions were made.

In general, how should Edgeware handle forks of the network?

- Edgeware should take a non-adversarial view of forks. Forks have been a feature of governance since day 1. Having forks of the network opens up a possibility of a merger between chains, something that could be voted on by on-chain governance.

Are there other issues you believe EDG holders should be aware of, or which you believe governance should handle?

- The decision to become a parachain. This will be an extremely large action for the governance for Edgeware and Polkadot. It will be interesting to see how validators and network actors treat this decision. Will Edgeware provide enough value to the Polkadot ecosystem that it will be a "subsidized" entrance into the ecosystem? Will the treasury have to acquire DOTs? Will there be a network fork resulting from validators whom would like to remain a "solochain"? This is a process that will take several months to coordinate and plan. It is potentially as contentious as Eth 1.0 to 2.0, but hopefully on-chain governance can provide a clear answer along the way.

5. What proposals on the network are you most interested in seeing? (e.g. initial parachain offerings, DAOs, DeFi protocols, stablecoins, prediction markets, insurance)
    - DAOs and prediction markets! We think this design space is largely underexplored.
    - While parachain offerings are useful, it seems like an area where the Edgeware competitive advantage will potentially fade.

6.  What role do you think Edgeware should play within the larger cryptocurrency and Web3 space? Is there an overall long-term vision for the network which you are most excited about?

Initially, we think the Edgeware chain will serve as the playground for those interested in launching a Polkadot parachain. Developers will start by creating a smart contract, perhaps funded by the treasury, and then using a crowd parachain bond hosted on Edgeware. It remains to early to stick our fingers in the wind and discuss a specific direction, but we are excited by governance use-cases. (of course)
