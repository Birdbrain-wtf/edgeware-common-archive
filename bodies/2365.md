**Technology & Education**
The Technology that Substrate provides is an industry-leading blockchain framework that is able to bridge the gap for businesses and developers alike. Traditionally needing large-scale teams, an educated developer can now produce something that can be replicated on Substrate in under an hour which is an absolutely tremendous difference in time and resources spent. Being able to properly leverage the tools and infrastructure that Substrate offers enables even small teams to build extremely powerful networks and applications. 
If we are able to provide a consistent stream of new ways to build decentralized applications using Substrate, we would be recognized as the premier choice for easily building decentralized applications enabled by Substrate. 

This will be made possible by our abundance in the number of high-quality resources and toolkits available to us right now. Even within the Substrate ecosystem, a prime example of this would be Patract Labs, which is accelerating the smart contract industry’s transition to the Wasm technology stack. One of the products they offer: Ask!, is a smart contract language design based on AssemblyScript and running on [Substrate FRAME Contracts](https://substrate.dev/docs/en/knowledgebase/smart-contracts/contracts-pallet). Ask! uses a similar way to [ink!](https://github.com/paritytech/ink) of designing the procedural macro in the form of an embedded domain-specific language (eDSL), to write contracts by providing annotation type in [AssemblyScript](https://github.com/AssemblyScript/assemblyscript) (aka AS). This way can hide the implementation details of the contract and therefore reduce the difficulty of writing a contract. 

This is important because there is now an extremely high demand for developers that are capable of writing smart contracts in either solidity or Rust. However, there is a very small amount of developers that are actually available and capable. 

**[AssemblyScript](https://www.assemblyscript.org/)** could be the solution we need: AssemblyScript targets WebAssembly's feature set specifically, giving developers **low-level control** over their code. Being a **variant of TypeScript** makes it easy to compile to **[WebAssembly](https://webassembly.org/)** without learning a new language. It Integrates with the existing **Web ecosystem** - no heavy toolchains to set up. Simply npm install it! 
Gearing our education around understanding how Substrate enables functionality, high-level options for writing smart contracts that compile to WebAssembly, and ways you can leverage the EVM pallet from Substrate to capitalize on the increased efficiency of Ethereum smart contracts on Edgeware would be some suggestions that I think would greatly benefit our developer ecosystem.

Edgeware will provide all the tools and resources necessary to efficiently work within and navigate smart contract environments, and the barrier to entry will be diminished to almost nothing. What we’ve found is that there is a very limited number of developers that are currently in the Substrate ecosystem. However, the tech industry is bigger than it’s ever been, and there are thousands of students studying in fields such as Computer Science. Reducing the barrier to entry for crypto means making a developer that might not be familiar with blockchain, comfortable enough to work through our documentation, and step-by-step guides to then become self-sustainable and able to understand the more abstract, functionality of blockchain technology. This way this could be implemented would be through guides similar to [What is Avalanche?](https://academy.binance.com/en/articles/what-is-avalanche-avax)

**Parachain Dapp model**
Developers will submit an application to the DAO for a decentralized application (dApp) which has to be built on top of Edgeware. This application should be written and structured similar to a whitepaper, to highlight the features of their decentralized application. This will be necessary to help educate, and influence community members of the DAO to ‘bond’ their EDG, or vote in favor of one decentralized application over another. 
Once a voting period has ended, the project with the highest votes (EDG bonded) will be chosen for the current cycle. In theory, the applicant that has the most compelling and captivating submission will carry more votes and receive the round of funding. This funding will provide the team with the resources necessary to begin building a project over an 8 week period which will be then be integrated into the Edgeware ecosystem. 
This process will repeat until 5 ‘lease slots’ are filled, and 5 teams are actively building on Edgeware. Once all slots are filled, and 8 week periods for projects have been elapsed, new votes are set into motion through the DAO which will be called ‘**trial by fire**’ and ‘**lease renewal**’. 

Trial by fire will be a voting period in which new projects will have the opportunity to obtain a leased spot. Projects that have elapsed 8 weeks are then required to submit an updated summary describing the deliverables they managed to achieve over their funding period if they wish to receive additional funding. The new projects will submit their applications, and if a new project gets more EDG bonded than existing projects, it will then replace the lease spot of the project which it was able to surpass. Lease spot holders who were able to hold their position are then eligible to receive additional funding from the treasury. Additional funding is very flexible and helps to incentivize teams to create long-term projects from inception.
