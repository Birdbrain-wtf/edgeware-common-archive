## Next steps

I’ve decided that I don’t think I’d like to take any more money from the Edgeware community.

The main reason is simply that I don’t feel I’m very good at this kind of relationship and I don’t want to ever disappoint, or take more than I give. I find it very difficult to maintain many social relationships and this kind of brand alliance, in the way that I probably should for this kind of thing, e.g. always tweeting about it and really repping it and stuff like that, this is just very unnatural to me. I’m an idiosyncratic person and I get obsessed with certain things unpredictably and so I’m not good at being a public ambassador for anything other than whatever I’m obsessed with at the moment. It was great to try this kind of thing, I have no regrets, I’m just realizing I’m a bad fit for it. So while I feel confident I’ve delivered enough on the first leg to bow out honorably, it just doesn’t feel right to reapply for the next legs of the grant.

But as a gesture of good will—and in gratitude for the first leg of the grant—I’ll continue to honor many deliverables initially proposed in the grant.

I’m very grateful, and I have nothing but love for everyone I’ve gotten to know in the Edgeware community, so you’ll always have a friend in me and I want to make extra-sure you get more value than you gave me.

- I will, of course, complete the book; and I will always happily and gratefully mention the grant I received from Edgeware—in the Acknowledgments and elsewhere.
- I will personally beta-test, dog-food, or otherwise play with and use any new creator-facing functionality shipped over the next year, whether its on the Edgeware chain or for a strategic partner of Edgeware’s. Whether that's NFTs, or something else, just let me know (I can’t keep up with all the chats but email me and I am there for you). I can let you know how things work, and give any feedback I might have.
- Edgeware community members can continue to enjoy complimentary memberships in IndieThinkers.org. Again, you’ll just have to let me know. But that’s totally cool, no problem.

## Feedback

I know that feedback is something the Edgeware community really wants to take from their collaborations with creators. So I’d also like to leave you with a few thoughts on this front.

- Everyone I've met in the Edgeware community has been cool, so that's good. Solid people, nothing critical to say on that front.
- Applying for the grant was relatively straightforward, so that was good.
- The process for voting and then distributing was somewhat confusing but it certainly wasn’t too bad, and wasn’t a problem for me in any way.
   - If you want to continue with creator grants, perhaps you may consider offering a grants program with pre-defined grant amounts, and a standardized application, etc. This could maybe make things easier for you all, and the creators; and attract more creators by simplifying what they will be expected to deliver and what they can expect to receive.
- In retrospect, I think splitting the grant into 3-4 milestones was somewhat counterproductive to the purpose of the grant—at least in my case. I completely understand the importance of de-risking by splitting it up, but in my case I think I failed to really access the "deep work" this grant was intended to buy me—just because I couldn't really bank on the future allotments, which meant I had to keep maintaining my other revenue sources (in case future allotments were denied me for any reason). This inhibited me from making the serious reallocations of effort and manpower that would have been necessary to really show up for the larger vision.
   - I think this point would also be ameliorated by creating and offering your own specified and standardized grants program. In my case the problem was that my vision and the amounts required came into conflict with the de-risking needs of the community—but if you had your own pre-defined terms and amounts and such, you could prevent this mismatch from happening in the first place.
- This is not unique to Edgeware, but personally I find the quantity and variety of communication channels and meetings to be completely overwhelming. Admittedly I think I have a uniquely low tolerance for this kind of thing, and like I said many web3 communities have equally overwhelming channels, but perhaps it’s useful to hear. Between Telegram, Discord, Commonwealth.im, and the video calls—it would make my brain hurt to even start thinking “should I check in with the community this week?“ So I just found myself psychologically averse to everything altogether.
- Again not unique to Edgeware, but the price volatility was way harder to manage than I prepared for. I’m actually still hodling a lot of EDG; thankfully I was able to trade enough at opportune times that I I think I will still be in good shape after taxes, but this required a lot of active attention and I didn’t really appreciate the risk of a grant like this potentially even being a net-cost for me after taxes.

That's all I have for now. Once again, thanks for the grant. The book will come and I'll make you proud to have played a role in helping it along. I'm only an email away, if I can be of any service. Thank you folks, and best of luck on everything underway in the community.
