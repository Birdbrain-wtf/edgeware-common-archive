### 
**1. What is your experience as a validator or node operator?**

We started our journey at Staking Facilities in 2017 when we witnessed the emergence of numerous Proof-of-Stake protocols and a lack of professional validators to operate those networks. With the launch of the Tezos network almost two years ago, we opened our services to the public. Ever since our goal was to make staking as easy and accessible as possible for everyone. Over the past year we added other PoS blockchains to our services and built products such as a multi-asset staking-dashboard and an in-browser delegation tool in order to improve the staking experience. In addition, we engage in community work by hosting meetups and creating educational content. We do so in order to make the onboarding as smooth as possible and grow the overall staking economy. We were able to stand our ground and stress-test our set-up successfully under real-market conditions during Cosmos' Game of Stakes. Staking Facilities is self-financed and self-sustaining in order to provide independent, holistic staking services. Additionally, for every project, we have a substantial amount of "skin in the game" as to best align our interests with those of our customers.

**2. What's your technical setup? (cloud vs. on-premise; software stack; key management)**

Most of our infrastructure is running on our own bare metal hardware which is spread out across multiple private racks in top-notch datacenters. We also make use could services to improve peering and latencies, but also to shield our validators from the public internet. Whenever possible our validators make use of enterprise-grade HSMs, such as the YubiHSM, to sign blocks. Additionally, our advanced monitoring system detects network anomalies quickly, allowing us to respond to incidents immediately.

**3. How did you learn about Edgeware? Why are you interested in validating?**

Initially we learned about Edgeware from the lockdrop. This form of a token distribution model seemed very appealing and innovative to us. Ultimately, this let to the decision to participate in the lockdrop itself as well as to run a validator for Edgeware. In addition, we are convinced that it takes projects such as Edgeware to grow and reinforce the Polkadot ecosystem. While Edgeware is still in its infancy, it promises to offer real added value to the overall ecosystem.
Generally speaking, we are convinced that it takes a strong and professional validator set to leverage the full potential of distributed ledger technologies and ultimately enable a truly secure and open economy. Hence, we want to offer our professional services to support this vision.

**4. What are your positions on issues that governance will be expected to handle? **

As active validators for other PoS projects, we can draw on a wealth of experience when it comes to governance related issues. We constantly coordinate with the community and exchange views with our delegators in order to make the best decision and, above all, to vote in the interests of the community. We take our delegator's nomination very serious.

**5. What role do you think Edgeware should play within the larger cryptocurrency and Web3 space? Is there an overall long-term vision for the network which you are most excited about?**

Since Edgeware is one of the first Polkadot parachains with a strong governance model it can help shape the Polkadot ecosystem and consequently the larger Web3 space from an early stage. It can be seen as a sandbox for early experiments and functions as a stepping stone for developers looking forward to start developing dApps for the Polkadot ecosystem. We think Edgeware should evolve into the go-to smart-contract platform of the Polkadot ecosystem, piggybacking on the rapid technical evolution of Substrate.
