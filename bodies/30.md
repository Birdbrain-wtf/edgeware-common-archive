2. *How do you propose the treasury budget should be spent?*

Because the uses of the treasury are dependent on the price of the token itself, for simplicity we assume here that the network has a network valuation from $75M. At a rate of 10% inflation going to the treasury, this gives us $7.5M to spend on activities. To be clear; as token price varies, we wouldn’t scale down all initiatives, but cut initiatives with less amounts of funding instead. There are a finite set of dimensions that we can explore for uses of the treasury; we’ve explored each below and presented how we would vote on a given proposal. 

**Ecosystem Upgrades:**

**Perspective:** Edgeware is built on top of Substrate and the protocol will likely be upgraded as Substrate itself is. It is unlikely that the Edgeware dev community will come up with novel protocol upgrades, and funding exploration of such would be a superfluous endeavor. We should instead fund dev projects that make developer access easier.

**Treasury Compensation:** Compensate independent devs to build additional devs tools & services that benefit the community at a market rate ($150K)

**Bridges:**

**Perspective:** If Edgeware joins the Polkadot ecosystem as a parachain, it is highly likely that there will be bridges to both ETH and BTC. That said, it is unclear when Polkadot will launch, whether a parachain spot will be available, or whether Edgeware will ultimately want to become a parachain. Developing a bridge to Ethereum will give Edgeware access to the most robust set of users, apps, and tokens in the space immediately, while giving Edgeware leverage via interoperability if/when parachain negotiations commence with Polkadot. 

**Treasury Compensation:** We’d propose funding an independent developer team with the treasury to build a bridge to Ethereum ($750K)

**Wallets, Custodians, Exchanges**

**Perspective:** For Edgeware to be a success, we need to get hundreds of wallets, custodians and exchanges to support Edgeware. Edgeware’s most probable path towards achieving this has been, and will continue to be, airdropping tokens via initiatives like lockdrops and initial parachain offerings. We’d like to see airdrops increase in their community-specificity, by offering Edgeware to certain token communities we aspire to become closer with. Polkadot may be an example of this.

**Treasury Compensation:** We’d proposing funding community-specific lockdrops with $2M-3M out of the treasury, similar to the EDG lockdrop where longer periods of time receive larger bonuses, all of this in partnership with wallets, custodians and exchanges.

**Application Category Focus:**

Games, DeFi and DAOs are the three emergent use cases for cryptocurrency. Games aren’t purpose-built for exploration, DeFi’s networks effects on Ethereum are likely too strong to outright compete with (i.e. a MakerDAO on Edgeware), but DAOs present a greenfield opportunity for Edgeware to carve out its own niche. Our rationale here is that, unlike DeFi, there are no natural network effects inherent to DAOs built on top of Ethereum, and the DAOs that exist on Ethereum are currently focused on a niche set of initiatives that we could expand upon. Current Ethereum-based DAOs (in terms of ETH locked) are also small. If we were to fund DAOs directly through the treasury, they would immediately be some of the largest DAOs in the space. This would give Edgeware the chance to bootstrap a meaningful developer community by focusing on a niche and to bootstrap future growth. 
Treasury Compensation: We’d propose funding DAOs directly through the treasury in an ongoing matter with $2-3M of funding. We also propose paying the Aragon team, or another independent developer, to deploy DAO frameworks/infrastructure on the network itself.

### Questions for Both

1. *What are your positions on issues that governance will be expected to handle?*

We believe that the generalized lock and the application process to the Polkadot ecosystem to become a parachain are likely the most contentious issues that Edgeware will have to deal with in the near term. In regards to the generalized lock, it remains to be seen if this issue becomes a reality, as there are currently no applications for a generalized lock review on the commonwealth website. If it does become contentious due to a large generalized locker, there are natural checks to the generalized lock; no participant can garner more than 20% of the total supply and most organizations that hold this significant of a share of Ethereum are generally well-intentioned actors like Parity that we could benefit aligning with long-term. In the unlikely event that a malicious actor (like a ponzi scheme) applied and received a generalized lock, we would advocate for a fork away from this version of the network. 

The second issue is Edgeware’s potential application to become a parachain. For context, Polkadot, for the most part, is the European Union of Blockchains. Member states apply to become a part of the European Union, and give up some of the monetary and fiscal policy they control in exchange for a shared security model. In the EU application process, the larger the member state (UK, Germany), the more leverage they have to dictate terms on their incorporation to the union. The analogy here is that Polkadot is the EU and Edgeware will likely be one of the largest blockchains/member states to consider applying in the near term. Edgeware gives up inflation (or the importance of its inflation) in order to get shared security and network effects via the Polkadot ecosystem. We don’t yet know if this is a net positive value proposition to the network. 

We have several concerns about Edgeware potentially becoming a parachain and we haven’t yet decided if we would support or oppose Edgeware becoming a parachain. Our decision is based entirely on whether it would be help us achieve the goals we laid out earlier. Questions that we are actively thinking about in regards to becoming a parachain (+ our current thinking) that will help us decide are: 

How will the market assess Edgeware’s inflation in a world where Polkadot is securing the chain? How will this impact the viability of the ongoing inflation-funded treasury?

If Edgeware becomes a parachain and is effectively being secured by validators receiving DOT-denominated inflation, it remains to be seen what monetary premium will accrue to Edgeware if EDG inflation continues. There are a few problems with this. As a nascent network, there is a chance that Edgeware needs the inflation and inflation-funded treasury to bootstrap the network. Furthermore, without a monetary premium, there could be limited value associated with Edgeware at all - the economic value proposition for Polkadot remains unclear to us. We’d love to hear a more cogent economic value proposition from the Web3/Polkadot teams as they solidify things and get closer to launch.

What happens to Edgeware’s validators in a world where Edgeware is a parachain? 

As of now, it appears some validators will become collators on the Polkadot network if Edgeware joins as a parachain. It remains to be seen how lucrative this will be, whether they will be paid in DOTs or Edgeware, or how many will be feasible to transition to the role of collators. Validators will likely be some of the most engaged ecosystem actors, and losing them (or their interest) in a transition to Polkadot would be an outcome we would not be happy with. We would need to understand if these validators would become collators on the Polkadot network and what economic value proposition being a collator would come with. 

Will Edgeware be required to subsidize the cost of being a parachain? At what cost? 

The value proposition of Polkadot is still unclear to us, even moreso as Edgeware becomes a valuable standalone smart contract platform. Unless there are apparent significant network effects to becoming a parachain, this could reduce our opinion of becoming one. 

How can Edgeware realistically exit the Polkadot ecosystem if we become a parachain? 

Brexit on the blockchain? Would it even be feasible to re-bootstrap a set of validators and inflationary model if Edgeware doesn’t see positive effects from becoming a chain?
