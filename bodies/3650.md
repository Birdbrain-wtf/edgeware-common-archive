# Decent Partners: An introduction to Network Services

## **Background**
Edgeware was launched in 2019 by founding team Commonwealth Labs after[ the lockdrop](https://medium.com/commonwealth-labs/whats-in-a-lockdrop-194218a180ca) secured over 1.2m ETH and participation from Binance, Coinbase and established governance projects such as [Aragon](https://twitter.com/aragonproject/status/1122171605734395905).

With no ICO and a broad and fair public distribution that compared favourably to other projects, excitement was high for what Edgeware could achieve as the first smart contract chain and potentially parachain in the Polkadot ecosystem home.
![](https://commonwealth-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/1acd938e-58e7-407f-82f0-c39a082d146a.1643405185693)

From the start it was positioned as a platform to entice ETH holders to a project that would incubate governance experiments through the use of Substrate's low-fees, emerging interoperability and on-chain democracy and treasury.

## The challenge
The project had been positioned as a platform for incubating governance experiments, however the broad distribution ensured Edgeware would itself become the experiment.

With over 90% of the token distributed to the public in the lockdrop, the door was opened to a community of holders who could mediate the decision making powers of the founding team. The result was deadlock between large holders and the founding team who disagreed principally not on what to fund, but on _how to fund _which eventually led to the departure of Commonwealth Labs.

The team at [Decent Partners](https://decent.partners) joined the project around this time, building bridges between both groups and the broader community. The primary motivation was to unblock Edgeware's frozen governance and reinvigorate the project's potential to become a truly decentralised and grassroots incubator of Web3 initiatives.

The primary challenge has been how best to analyse the capabilities of the underlying Substrate technology, emerging organisational structures, grant funding, value capture and talent requirements in order to better coordinate networks such as Edgeware, incubate Web3 teams and indeed launch future Substrate networks that are decentralised and community run from the start.

Blockchain based operating systems that are dependent on sourcing creative contributions from a network of peers funded via community led governance, requires a new framework for understanding, defining and indeed separating the interests of key network participants, network services is one part of this evolving process.

## The journey so far

The_ Network Services_ model is the result of 12+ months spent researching:

- the project's technical, economic and cultural strengths and deficiencies
- existing governance structures and standards
- the varied talents, motivations and issues of the people involved


Before developing:

- Community participation, organisational processes and governance standards
- A network of grant funded talent, teams and projects across a diverse range of specialities
- A network development strategy for the incubation of a multi-chain future, beginning with [Kabocha](https://kabocha.network)


## Defining a Network Service Provider

The role of a Network Service Provider (NSP) is relatively simple to define but complex in its undertaking:

> _A network service provider onboards external talent, teams and projects to community networks - curating, communicating and formalising proposals that are then voted on by a decentralised network of token holders, before ensuring the careful deployment and management of approved funds, the successful delivery of agreed milestones alongside the timely reporting of issues, progress and lessons learned through the project. _

Through this evolving process the network service provider gains deep experience in the challenges of decentralised governance, grant funding, value capture and community development, which in turn then informs the recruitment of the next wave of talent, the establishment of new processes, the iteration of governance technologies and the discovery of new avenues of opportunity.

## A separation of concerns

When Edgeware is considered as a monolithic community, it becomes difficult to separate out the various roles, motivations and talents of the assembled members. Put simply there is currently organisational framework for categorising and delegating responsibilities in a way that can be repeatable.

As it stands there are three clear roles:

- Curators - source proposals
- Creators - deliver proposals
- Community - vote on proposals


We are not alone in this realisation - [recent discussions in Polkadot/Kusama](https://polkadot.network/blog/kusama-and-polkadot-now-reward-curators-helping-to-scale-councils-functions-join-the-force-moving-the-community-forward/) have led to the creation of a [Bounties pallet](https://github.com/paritytech/substrate/issues/5713) that is designed to delegate funds to curators, who then have the ability to execute funding at their discretion and indeed to benefit from successful curation.

With these emerging technical solutions, we are establishing the specific role of the network service provider - a curator specialised in the development of decentralised organisations.

## Incentives

The basic design of Substrate's treasury system requires the proposing team to bond 5% of the requested funds. If the proposal passes, the 5% is returned, if the proposal fails, the 5% is sent to the treasury.

This bond system is designed to encourage quality proposals. In practice it also creates a barrier to entry for teams, who may not have the required EDG, so in almost all cases, there is a need for an existing community member to advance this 5%.

The network services model introduces a 10% fee on top of the requested funds constituting both a success fee for proposals that successfully pass community approval and a basic cost for ensuring the completion of agreed milestones.

Currently the network service fee is implemented and executed manually through multisignature transactions, in the future we aim to automate this function into the treasury pallet and to split the 10% into two payments associated to the start and conclusion of a project.

## Where it goes from here

With Edgeware we have developed an upper bound of £25k for initial grants, therefore the 10% fee represents a small amount considering the work spent delivering network services.

However with the launch of our Kusama parachain candidate Kabocha, we are aiming to scale this funding amount for proven teams - you start your journey through project incubation in Edgeware, before advancing to Kabocha's interoperable accelerator, and then through the development of a new chain that will join our own relay network.

As a result of implementing the network services model into the technical foundations of all future incubated chains, and therefore retaining 10% of newly minted supplies, we believe there is a scalable and sustainable model to scale legions of community led Substrate networks that emerge through a shared set of values, structures and experience.

With this in place, we expect that the varied network services offered by Decent Partners can expand over time, creating added incentives, perks and opportunities that can attract and retain the best talent, teams and projects, which in turn can create positive feedback loops.

To encourage collective intelligence and to avoid centralisation, our intention is that Decent Partners will gradually decentralise by becoming a digital cooperative of members. You cannot purchase membership, the only way to join the collective is through the successful delivery of funded proposals.
