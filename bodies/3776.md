# Edgeware Treasury, Value Capture and Funding Terms

This is a post to try and unify a bunch of different discussions, debates and issues that have popped up at different points over the last few months. 

The aim is to open up focused discussion that can lead to establishing a few community standards or at least begin the process of getting people to think through some basic assumptions and principles. 

## Treasury 
 
Edgeware's treasury is/was created through inflation, it is effectively a tax on all holders of EDG established at the origin of the project, with funds assigned to a multisig wallet. 

The treasury also receives funds through network fees, slashing of validators and of proposal bonds if a referendum fails. 

The aim of the treasury is to fund the project's development which is about the broadest remit possible - in principle it can be used for anything the community agrees to fund... hence when someone asks _'What is Edgeware's roadmap'?_ the answer is simple... 

It is what we fund. 

To access funds, proposals need to be put forward to the community via a [simplemajority referendum](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/1563-simplemajority-a-proposed-edgeware-governance-standard). 

The community then votes to approve or reject the request, locking their tokens to indicate their view one way or the other. They can increase their voting weight by locking for longer which gives some signal of their commitment to a particular decision. 
Issues

The main issue is very simple but incredibly complex given the infinite directions the project could take given its current positioning:

## **Funding process and terms**

If we can agree that _Edgeware becomes what it funds_ then the next step is to debate the process of that funding and the terms attached. 

This is where things have historically become messy, since there are no agreed standards or even frameworks that unite the community and its funding.

## **The fuzzy stuff**
When the community approves funding what are the terms attached to that funding. You'll see from the following, that given them labels is easy, actually digging into the detail is much harder. 

- **Is it a grant? **Definition: A grant is generally understood to be funding that is not recoupable and is advanced because it extends the mission or utility of the core project. 


- I**s it an investment?**Definition: An investment is generally understood to be funding that is advanced with some expectation of direct financial return at a future date. 


- **Is it a loan?**Definition: A loan is generally understood to be be funding that is advanced with expectation of repayment often (but not always) with some rate of return charged as interest on the principal. 


- **Is it a hybrid? **There are many ways to combine two, or even three into different financial instruments that might offer different characteristics of each. 


Within existing finance it is relatively easy to see the difference between a grant, an investment and a loan - often because existing corporate structures indicate their preference, for example... 

- Non-profit foundations give grants
- For profit funds make investments
- Public treasuries extend loans as bonds


However with public networks such Edgeware and the things we are funding things rapidly become blurry, which is where issues arise. 

Let's take the case of [Kabocha](https://kabocha.network): 

**Grants**
Kabocha is being funded through a series of grants: 
- There is no expectation of return of those specific amounts since it is extending the mission of the project and expanding its utility, so good so far... 


**Investment**
Edgeware holders receive KAB for holding EDG at the snapshot: 

- If we consider Edgeware holders to be the network's owners, as they collectively control its resource allocation through voting, then this is effectively an investment return.
- The US 'Howey Test' is what roots existing and future regulations around the world and is the reason token projects carve out the US and other territories from token offerings.
- The qualification for an investment contract is rooted in the idea of an expectation of return delivered through the efforts of others both in the US and many other jurisdictions. 
- Irrespective as to whether we call it a lockdrop, crowdloan or paradrop Edgeware is the fund, KAB is a financial return. 


Despite all the fancy legal footwork this is why all other crowdloan campaigns, parachain projects and indeed the original Polkadot/Kusama relay chains carved out certain countries. 

It's clear all are securities, the only question is can they decentralise their operations fast enough to get to a position where the original team don't find themselves in legal jeopardy. 

Regulations are coming and the test of decentralisation will likely be rooted in the question of whether a project is viable if a core team were to be removed. This will ensure the rapid departure of founding teams, the wholesale de-listing of hundreds of projects and the collapse of many exchanges. 

A response may be that DEXes are unstoppable - the truth is, they're mostly not since they are led by core groups. In addition regulators will begin to focus on companies such as Discord who can essentially provide a regulatory pinch point, with terms and conditions expanded to include only projects that regulators allow. 

In the example of Edgeware, it was almost certainly an investment contract at launch despite legal recommendations to the contrary - you lock up ETH to get EDG, with the expectation of a return bsaed on the work of others. 
However the departure of Commonwealth Labs and the evolution of the project in their absence gives credibilty to the fact that Edgeware could soon claim to be **a public network. **

Which brings us back to why Kabocha moves the community forward... 

We are building the project in the open, putting every major decision and grant proposal through public referendums and relying on a distributed network of community contributors to build every component independently, with no single corporate entity in charge. 

It would be way easier and faster to have a single group dictating terms, but then it would definitively not be a public network, but rather a corporate entity with a token attached that someday hopes, somehow to become a public network... 

There is a very good reason we are doing this - because if Edgeware has some claim to be a public network due to its current community leadership, then Kabocha reinforces and extends this claim. 

**Loans**
The final question relates to whether Edgeware's funding constitutes a loan of some kind - aka, is this funding recoupable at some later date. 

Given the fact that Kabocha is being developed by core Edgeware contributors, it should be possible to negotiate this in good faith, though there are strong arguments that Kabocha extends and expands Edgeware's utility and that the EDG snapshot is effectively the return. 

Kabocha is **not a contentious fork** - since the community is actively voting this project through, it is by definition uncontroversial. This is in contrast to a project such as [Hedgeware](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/1153-announcing-hedgeware-a-kusama-parachain-for-edgeware) which was effectively just a snapshot of balances, with no democratic engagement or community approval. In practice an approach like Hedgeware would have effectively re-centralised Edgeware. 

## **Value capture **
Outside of regulatory compliance, value capture is pretty much the only important question a decentralised funding system has to ask itself - namely, if funding goes one way, then what flows the other way, else there is no chance of scaling the economy. 

The Ethereum / EVM thesis of value capture comes from the requirement to use ETH for transactions. Therefore the price of ETH is speculated on the fact that Ethereum will process more fees, and in turn encourage more speculators, which in turn brings in more developers who create more financial games with which to speculate... like a casino. 
The addition of the fee burning model is itself a new casino mechanic that utilises Bitcoin's deflationary narrative, introducing a scarcity model that further pumps the price. It's casinos all the way down... 
In truth its a very simple economic game that plays to base human instincts despite the marketing hype around the complex systems built on top. 

Edgeware's lockdrop was such a success because in 2018/19 it was not clear that Ethereum could scale and so it was seen as a hedge by many large ETH holders - a bet that Gavin could solve Ethereum's problems better than Vitalik. 
The early hope was that Edgeware could onboard tens or even hundreds of apps, which would in turn create new financial games, that would somehow, at some point drive value back to the chain in the form of fees - even though some of Substrate's primary design decisions are rooted in the reduction of transaction fees. 

This has not happened, and it is wishful thinking to think it will happen any time soon. There is no swarm of development teams eagerly testing on Edgeware's EVM testnet, if they are anywhere they are on Moonriver, Phala and other EVM focused Substrate chains. 

This is a good thing. Edgeware is not Ethereum, and Kabocha certainly isn't. 

What is most exciting about Substrate is it is not Ethereum, it enables new things, the chance to start from a blank piece of paper, to design simple systems that can do more than create an unregulated casino in the cloud, but new economic systems that build from the ground up - that can return dignity, community and purpose to people via the contribution of their varying talents. 

You know, like a real economy is supposed to... 

## Is there another way? 

We are rethinking [Kabocha's value capture](https://decentration.medium.com/whats-the-bigger-picture-roadmap-for-kabocha-4480529c8cfd) from the ground - creating a system that is built around a simple and replicable mechanism that simplifies the relationship between community, currency and contribution. 

> Rather than inflating a supply and directing it into a treasury – as has been done by all Substrate chains so far, we will only be minting new KAB when a proposal is approved by the community.
> Therefore, we can say that every KAB minted after launch is to fund a specific job.
> In addition, those requesting funds will assign NFTs to Kabocha’s ‘reserve’ that are representative of their ideas / concept or project.
> We call this ‘cultural collateral’.
> These NFTs will also confer access rights, permissions and future utility to those holding KAB and can be upgraded as the funded projects develop over time utilsing [https://rmrk.app.](https://rmrk.app.)
> The narrative idea is a rework of the well known ‘Store of Value’ concept which has been the major hook for Bitcoin.
> Except rather than being backed by machines / sunk electricity costs of miners, Kabocha is backed by the time, effort and insight of its contributors. 
> Those investing in Kabocha are essentially betting on the future collective cultural value and access afforded by the assigned assets.
> We call this ‘Store of Values’.
