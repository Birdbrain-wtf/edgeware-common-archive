## **Challenge**

Owing to a series of challenges, the team returned to the community for [a top up](https://gov.edgewa.re/discussion/3255-gmi-crypto-media-how-to-make-it-in-crypto-media-top-up-proposal) to cover: 

> _Reduction in EDG price, which has left us short with finishing the run: $8.1k_
> _New design work: $1.5k_
> _Podcast Studio time for narrative recording: $2k_
> _A 10% additional network service fee which was not included in the original proposal: $2.04k_

This is the first time a funded project has returned for this kind of top up funding and so this was a chance for an open and honest debate that split the community. 

Key issues that were raised by the community:

- The team are responsible for managing funds and should have taken the exchange rate volatilty into account. 
- The community should not 'bail-out' funds lost in this way since a proposal team would be unlikely to return funds if the exchange rate had moved in their favour. 
- The community had not heard anything of the podcast - it would be good to get a taste of the work to date.
- A feeling that Edgeware was not getting enough value from the podcast given a request for funds to finish the project - there was no interviewee featured from the project. 


In the end the voting reflected an interesting split in the community with [Referenda 54](https://gov.edgewa.re/proposal/referendum/54-referendum-54) not passing.

If you've read this far, the following introduces some lessons learned and how the role of a Network Service provider is evolving. 

## **Lessons learned **

Although we have approved many grants, there are no clear standards or terms to that funding.

Additionally it has not been clear where the responsibility should rest when it comes to negotiating top ups or indeed whether the community are open to them. This debate has helped clarify some things.

- It seems clear that unless originally agreed, the funding approved for set milestones is to cover the delivery of a project and if the price rises then the proposers benefit, but if it falls then they still have the responsibility to deliver or lose trust - this needs to be clear in the future.


- The team could have liquidated all of their grant, but this seems wrong given they had only just started work.


- Ideally we would have payments at both the start of the project and the finish which are each approved by the community, or indeed a delegated representative which means each would have a separate 7 day moving average price.


- This is much closer to how traditional project invoicing works for exactly the reasons we have faced here.


- Milestone 1: approval of proposal at 7 day moving average
- Milestone 2: delivery of proposal at 7 day moving average


There is currently no way to do this in Substrate without running two separate but unconnected on-chain referendums or over-funding a bounty which could mediate exchange risk but is an imperfect solution.

## The emerging responsibilities of a Network Service Provider (NSP)

Additionally it has become clearer now that the role of an NSP such as Decent Partners should be to take responsibility for making these terms clear and for mediating or remedying the situation.

- A NSP takes the risk of losing the initial 5% bond if a proposal fails (as in the case of the top up)
- A NSP should ensure clarity of communication between the community and the proposal team
- A NSP takes ultimate responsibility for successful delivery - this is how they build trust.
- If a team loses funds, does not deliver or does not manage their funds well, then the NSP should be responsible - e.g. for making good on missing funds.
- If a team requires top up funding to conclude a project then the NSP is responsible for negotiating the terms with the community in good faith as a representative of the Project team.


- **In return a NSP earns the right to the 10% Network Service Fee which is how the model sustains.**
