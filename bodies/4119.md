## **Background**
Edgeware did not have run an ICO or raise funds directly from VCs, instead launching via the lockdrop with a broad and fair public distribution. There was no foundation in this approach and the launch team Commonwealth Labs as issuers received a legal recommendation that Edgeware was likely not a security, but this is not a legal guarantee. 

With Commonwealth Labs no-longer involved directly in the day to day operations of Edgeware, it has been left to a distributed network of community contributors to self-organise and shape the future direction and strategy of the network through a series of entirely independent funded proposals and teams approved via the votes of EDG holders. 

## **An evolving regulatory environment**
The standard approach for launching crypto-projects has been to establish a non-profit foundation based in a friendly crypto jurisdiction such as Switzerland, or further off-shore in places like the Cayman Islands. Importantly this foundation acts as a steward of the project and is required to be independent of a founding developer and their investors, though in practice this can lead to challenges and conflicts of interest, either real or perceived. 

The division between foundations and core development is considered the safest and most compliant approach but still exists in a legal grey area, as there is still very little regulatory clarity around the status of crypto-currencies, NFTs and DAOs in general, with most projects likely to fall foul of a wide range of corporate, money issuance or tax legislation. 

In general, most crypto projects have adopted a [progressive decentralisation](https://a16z.com/2020/01/09/progressive-decentralization-crypto-product-management/) narrative. Rather than avoiding security regulations or money issuer status, the hope has been that a token may initially be a security, but can evolve into a utility token - this is certainly the hope for Ethereum and for the market more broadly. 

Over the next 12-18 months the US, Europe and the UK will set out more cohesive regulatory structures that seek to contain the emerging crypto-ecosystem. With certain countries blacklisted, there are profound changes ahead that will dictate just how open, accessible and global crypto-currencies can become and by definition, who will ultimately benefit. 

See: [Joe Biden's Crypto Executive Order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefing-room/statements-releases/2022/03/09/fact-sheet-president-biden-to-sign-executive-order-on-ensuring-responsible-innovation-in-digital-assets/)
See: [What happens if Ethereum is classed as a security?](https://www.reddit.com/r/ethereum/comments/s4yww6/what_actually_happens_if_ethereum_is_classified/)
See: [Defining real and fake DAOs](https://lexnode.substack.com/p/defining-real-and-fake-daos?s=r)

Given the unique promise of blockchain technologies as new systems for organising and funding global talent both supra-nationally and hyper-locally, it is of vital importance to consider how to launch, maintain and evolve public networks to ensure they remain sovereign and credibly neutral, whilst also ensuring those contributing can continue to interface with existing legal jurisdictions rather than be pushed into more shadowy or anonymous practices. 

## **Kabocha's approach**
Our approach with Kabocha has been to double down on the Edgeware's lockdrop launch and decentralised contribution model, where no single group, legal entity or individual controls or dictates the project's development. 

The creation of the [Network Services](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/discussion/3650-decent-partners-an-introduction-to-network-services) model, initiated by [Decent Partners](https://decent.partners) replaces the need for a non-profit foundation with a network native structure built into the emerging organisational system that can nurture talent, develop proposals, communicate between project teams and ultimately steward, but not dictate a roadmap. 

Every single component of Kabocha has been developed in the open, with established contributors drawn from Edgeware and newcomers making their first contributions in a network of peers, with funding approved through simple majority proposals voted on by a distributed network of EDG holders whose decision making remains wholely independent from the contributors. 

Within Substrate this is unique, outside of Substrate it may well also be the first of its kind. An on-chain governed blockchain birthing an entirely new network that it holds stake in, reliant on no central authority, leader or legal entity.

## **Work ahead**
Given the complex nature of the project and the many moving parts we believe it is wise to create a small budget that can be used to give ongoing legal and compliance advice both in the lead up to, during and post the crowdloan. 

This work covers, but it not limited to:

- Crowdloan offering
- Organisational structures
- Terms and conditions
- Regulations and compliance


The aim is for this work to represent a one off structure that can be templated and repeated with new chains as we begin to incubate new projects that will go on to join the relay network. 

Our focus is on UK, European and US law.
