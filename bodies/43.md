While Commonwealth Labs has quietly encouraged a volunteer-based ambassador team, which we lovingly describe as a thinktank, I believe the landscape has shifted for traditional ambassador programs. As a result of hype-cycles and development cycles being somewhat asynchronous- and perhaps some attention fatigue, I believe we should reconsider the current un-resourced program.

As there is no 'traditional' source of authority in Edgeware (or even several,) the community needs to own the future of the Ambassador program. The best way to do this I believe is to test small amounts of funding for a passionate community leader who can be renumerated for their time and perform nominal operations to recruit, coordinate and incentivize Ambassadors. 

I will personally lend my time to coaching any person who is selected for this role - but I think the best route is to consider an 'RFP' process - request for proposal. While we don't have a formal setup for this sort of thing for the network yet, it basically follows this: 

- We announce a certain amount of funding.
- 'Crypto-contractors' come forward with their plan on that amount of funding.
- The community selects a contractor to utilize that funding. (Ideally, this would be an easy ranked-choice style selection, however, only binary votes are enabled in the democracy module currently.)
- The contractor performs to a set of standards described by the RFP.
- Through some on-chain process or through a second signal or vote, the RFP is fulfilled by the treasury and the contractor is renumerated (paid!)

Regardless of the exact purpose, we should begin to consider RFP procedures for the on-chain treasury. I welcome opinions re: ambassador programs more widely, their usefulness, ROI, etc.
