In Edgeware, staking rewards are paid automatically at the end of eras.  However, for scaling and block-time stability, Polkadot and Kusama have opted to make this a triggered event - by a transaction. Initially, Lazy Payouts required a staker to claim rewards themselves within an expiry window.  The newer design, Simple Payouts.

> 
> Polkadot and Kusama **make stakers claim their rewards for past eras by submitting a transaction**. This naturally leads to spreading out reward distribution, as people make transactions at disparate times, **rather than updating the accounts of all stakers in a single block. **Even if everyone submitted a reward claim at the same time, the fact that they are individual transactions would allow the block construction algorithm to process only a limited number per block and ensure that the network maintains a constant block time.** If all rewards were sent out in one block, this could cause serious issues with the stability of the network.[ Polkadot Wiki](https://wiki.polkadot.network/docs/en/learn-simple-payouts)**

> Simple payouts requires one transaction per validator per era to claim rewards. The reason Polkadot requires this is to avoid an attack where someone has several thousand accounts nominating a single validator. The major cost in reward distribution is mutating the accounts in storage, and Polkadot cannot pay out several thousand accounts in a single transaction. **[Polkadot Wiki](https://wiki.polkadot.network/docs/en/learn-simple-payouts) **

Currently, Kusama/Polkadot _also _have implemented a 64-nominator limit per Validator. 

Under Simple Payouts, ANYONE can send the payout transaction - triggering ALL of the validators nominators to be paid out.  Lazy Payouts requires each user to submit their own.  A value-add that validators might bring is to conduct these mass-payout transactions out of their own pocket on behalf of their nominators. Service enhancement. :)

While tools will likely evolve to automate even the Lazy Payout Transactions, we should begin to discuss whether we can stomach avoiding this change, and if not, the route we should take.
