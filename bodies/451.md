With Substrate experiencing big changes, many chains are starting to wonder how best to upgrade when  on-chain data stores need to be migrated to new locations to 'preserve the chain' of the chain with newer versions of Substrate. 

I posted recently on the upgrade path as explained by Drew,  but [Emiel of Polkascan](https://matrix.to/#/!dQIXacXSBDQsPsWEYR:matrix.org/$eZ_YspsT-n0Ww_gK4YW3kZCPSkMLIRMIB4ckzkEmPq4?via=matrix.org&via=matrix.parity.io) recently brought to our attention an alternative he called 'Hard Spooning':

> You could perhaps consider a chain reset through a ‘hard spoon’. You could get a new chain with a genesis state that reflects the state of the current chain at a given future block. This is how the Kulupu mainnet migrated yesterday after consent of its users. The main argument was removing the complex migration path. This approach solves all your problems elegantly.

In addition, it is an elegant way to release testnet or canary-nets with historical blocks. Drew mentioned that Edgeware has some trading volume and halting the chain to perform this 'hard spoon' might result in some confusion on exchanges.

User. Remz raised the point that those exchanges 'rely on finalization, which is sometimes lagging for hours already.'  He also feels that the concern is unwarranted: "chains often start and stop deposits and handle these events internally."

@nasamura:matrix.org mentioned that the Kulupu run of this technique went smoothly, but that "If it takes too long to build a workable runtime upgrade or begins to grow in complexity I would favour the path that Kulupu took."

The technical challenges and potential options for multiple Substrate-layer migrations continue to unfold.  :-]
