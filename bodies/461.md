### Proposals and Drafts

**Treasuries and Funding**

- Dillon notes a need for guidelines for grant and norms before formalizing rules.
- Perhaps using the signaling module for establishing a budget for the year.
- Jam says that hardcapping the amount that the council might spend is difficult. Informal rules and community accountability is more feasible.


**Multiple Treasuries**
Users are largely against the idea of deploying separate pools of funds managed by a different collective. (The council currently manages the main treasury.) In essence, don't need to complicate governance structures when we haven't maximized the existing treasury and spending mechanisms.

**Budgets / Soft Caps for Treasury Planning**
Dillon proposed casually '2/2/2/2' — 2% of yearly inflation for:

- 2% Listings and Partnerships
- 2% - 4% for Developer Partnerships, Core development Developer Tooling, e.g., GraphQL layer.
- 2% for bonding for a Polkadot parachain slot
- 2% for Commonwealth to direct on a discretionary basis for community support.


Users acknowledge that core development is the highest ROI, but also the most likely to be blocked on runtime engineering expertise. Jam conveyed that there will be a Parity runtime engineer with near-full time dedication to the Edgeware project.

**Core Development Additions funded via Treasury**
Drew and several members proposed and supported the use of treasury funds to hasten core development, as Drew is largely the only engineer building the Edgeware runtime.

**Seedcode**
Drew has been working with a development team called Seedcode, who has proposed a treasury motion to fund the development of staking tools. They have posted a draft to Commonwealth and invite public input.

**Staking Facilities's Proprietary Staking Tool**
SF announced an Edgeware-supporting staking tool they are working on, and gathered opinions on whether the project is appropriate for community funding, given that it is a proprietary product and will be closed-source. Users largely supported their application for funds as long as it truly contributed to the community. Also, they raised the option of their releasing some technology as open-source but not the entire tool, or combining it with network development KPIs - for example, if their tool increased the staking rate significantly through UX improvements.

Staking Facilities could also separate out the backend infrastructure - nodes for public access.

**Validator Set Limits**
"On ****validator set limit changes, we had a recent democracy action from user lucky. And that failed it was to raise the set from 85 to 90. There was quite a bit of discussion on this topic. You know, largely that the increase wasn't massive, but also that we didn't want to make any changes to the existing validator set up currently because we are still struggling to understand the dynamics of their operational costs and the impacts to edges address security and developmental capacity at this time."
Users agreed that validators could benefit from further support but little consensus on the best way to this.

- Establishing a burn to improve economics for validators.
- Increasing the limit of the set. (But would likely drop the validator return but supports more validators.)
- Timing on network upgrades is an important factor, Dillon Chen said.
- Vote stats indicated that there was not a lot of demand for increasing the set.
- Unsure whether new validators increase the total amount staked but if so, would improve security, Dillon Chen also mused.
- Julius recommends using a signal proposal to look for a quorum between 5 and 13% (the highest on KSM) before proceeding with a validator limit increase.
- Dillon suggested that an interface for vote delegation, not merely staking, would enhance participation.

And a counterexample from Kusama, in which there is no block reward that goes to the Treasury. Dillon also raised the idea of a Stimulus payment from the treasury to validators to balance out rewards. (Dillon doesn't support this strongly however.)

**Council and Elections**
Thom's Introduction: _"Regarding council elections, we had a big shift this this month, several council members got booted off and an unknown person entered the set with several accounts. We recently learned that this individual is user Remz, and that he did this to illustrate a point that the council may have too much power and can be easily kind of co-opted and the treasury potentially drained."_ This conversation largely focused on specific mechanisms to improve participation and to restrict the council's power.

- Thom to include an event notification for election cycles to the Edgeware Google Event Calendar.
- Jam highlighted the need for a better awareness and participation as a major cause of this shift in members.
- Several members support the notion of a council as primarily a watchdog entity, relying on vetos to cancel unproductive activities or actions.
- A coded hard cap to council spending is not feasible at this time but would be a good thing to parameterize.
- Thom raises a solution for limiting the treasury abuse potential: Adjust the spending period to be longer than the democracy voting period, (Jam argues 2-3x longer would be sufficient) for democracy actions to wipe undesired spends off the queue. Shorten the democracy period, and lengthen the spending period. Jam is also interested in shortening the voting period to speed action on the network.


**Ideal Staking Rate**
In the May governance call, Jam shared that the ideal staking rate specified by Web3F Research for DOT/KSM, probably isn't appropriate for Edgeware because we do not have parachain bonds or on-chain lockup, and therefore EDG is more liquid. Increasing the staking rate over 50% (KSM is currently at 50%), would increase security.
In general, participants accepted a temporary decrease- for example, 50%, but preferred a return to higher levels as we reach that lower goal and see activity. Edgeware is currently at 25% and is targeting 80%. Altering this staking rate however is hard-coded in a runtime constant, and is not parameterized, and would require a more serious upgrade to change.

**Chain Hires**

"_On the governance call yesterday we talked about some of the challenges inherent to compensating them, including the risks of being kind of a chain worker with uncertainty about the fulfillment of future payments." — Thom_

- Bounties function in development for KSM may be appropriate, utilizing a council-approved curator- an independent third-party, to gauge bounty success and dispense payment. this function is still in development however.
- Jam let us know about a new tool, the Scheduler palette, which could be used to pre-schedule treasury spends and other actions and ensure payment programmatically.


**Treasury Norms and Spending Mechanisms**
Thom asks - Norm-wise, what is the max amount we are comfortable submitting in a single payment? Anything beyond that number should be separated into a second milestone.
Ray argues that payment once work is delivered is most in the interest of the chain.

- Multisig may be used to ensure funds are available and budgeted for approved grantees.
- KSM frequently pays out for work done previously, but generally uses milestones to prevent devaluation from currency price volatility.
- Treasury spend proposed and approved > spends to multisig > Curators assigned to the multisig decide when to release the escrowed payment. Signatories might include both grantees and curators/council.
- Currently the most straightforward route is to have milestones - it's technically simple, and oit reduces volatility costs on holding EDG for the grantee.
- Raymond argues that we should have a standard payment on the front-end for the project specification itself, which takes work and should be compensated. Thom agrees and says that this will also incentivize teams to bring projects to the table.
- Thom notes that the time between milestones, treasury stash may lower/change, or council members may change. Going the multisig route alleviates these, but is still subject to volatility.
