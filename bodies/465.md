Consolidating a few threads that have brought up validator economics. There’s been some recent discussion by community members around the economics of validating. The gist is that reward given to validators is too low, which seems important to address as we compete for a healthy validator ecosystem.

Overall, a large treasury, right now is a “feature”, rather than a bug. Its main use is to attract developers, a separate but equally important set of stakeholder. If there are no dapps or protocols to provide fees, the network atrophies over the long term. Even if the treasury is large now, I think we should weigh the potential effects on any changes that directly limit the treasury will have in the long run. Instead of trying to limit the treasury, the network can bring rewards in line with other networks without turning off the treasury reward.

TLDR. Easiest way to increase validator returns in line with other networks, while still maintaining the treasury is to lower **ideal staking rate from 80% to 50%, while maintaining the same over inflation to validators at 10%. This pushes up the overall interest rate to validators to 20% if the target 50% of the network is staked.**
Here, I’ll go over:

- Key parameters that influence validator rewards
- Preferred adjustment to incentivize validators (outlined above)
- Current thoughts on validator count / competition.


**What are the key parameters that influence validator returns (and where they are right now)?**

- Ideal Inflation Rate: The inflation rate that the network sets for validators (currently 10%). It feels unnecessary to increase this amount.
- Ideal Staking Rate: Currently 80%, which was derived by consulting [Web3 research docs on token economics](https://research.web3.foundation/en/latest/polkadot/Token%20Economics.html).
- Inflation Rate Function: If the network is below the ideal staking rate, then the interest paid to individual validators will be higher than the target 20%, which we can derive by taking the ideal inflation of 10% over the 50% ideal staking rate. The inflation rate will vary, see the Web3 research docs or Thom's Validator spreadsheet for more information.
- Parachain Bonding (not applicable to us): "Another large percentage of the DOT supply will be frozen as deposits by the commercial blockchains who get a parachain slot. We originally aim to have around 50% of DOTs staked in NPoS, and 30% in parachain deposits. As a reference, the percentage staked in other PoS-based projects is as follows. - Tezos is 65.73% staked - DASH is 58.69% staked - Lisk is 58.20% staked - EOS is only 35.49% staked, but that is because it is DPoS and the yield is low.” Given that Edgeware has no parachain bonding, the 30% was added to the ideal staking rate of 50%.
- Current Staking Rate: **27%.** Not something that is directly tunable, therefore not really up for discussion here
- Treasury Inflation: Currently 95 EDG per block + any leftovers from validator rewards. Together, these yield, ~15% on a yearly basis. My current position is that this should be high so that we can aggressively grow the Edgeware developer ecosystem, which currently is the limiting factor on network growth.
- RewardRemainder: Any reward that goes unissued to validators goes to the treasury.
- Treasury Burn: Currently 0%. I feel this should stay at 0%.


**Preferred Approach to Validator Inflation**

After a bit of modeling thanks to [Thom’s Spreadsheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1VlzTUDESbbfOggMRz3GyE9-VqR9MlOhNuoekBboKvLw/edit#gid=2011072230) (screenshot below), my TLDR is to lower **the ideal staking rate to 50%** as has been suggested (as well as changing ideal interest rate to 20%) to maintain the inflation rate of 10% to validators. Assuming the current percentage (27%) of the network staked, this increases nominal interest rate from a current 18.63% to 24.26%. . Additionally, this raises the real interest rate to 4%, which is in line with other staking protocols as sourced from [https://staked.us/yields/](https://staked.us/yields/).
![](https://commonwealth-uploads.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/f764103b-ee55-4f0c-bcb7-d75583b68688.1591039532013)

If validators still feel like this is not sufficient, there are additional remedies, that impact how much EDG is held in the treasury. Although I'm not currently in favor, I see two potentially viable additions:

- they can vote to turn the RewardRemainder from treasury to (). The reward remainder deposits any “unclaimed” validator rewards to the treasury. On a yearly basis, this currently amounts to something like an additional ~5% (250m EDG) to the treasury at the current staking rate. I would prefer not to turn this off at this point in time. However, I see this as the less bad choice than wholly turning of the treasury reward.
- A related parameter is the treasury burn. I am not in favor of tuning this parameter at this time. Although strictly speaking inflows and outflows to the treasury are equal. As remz has previously mentioned, it feels psychologically different to see the treasury slowly drop to zero over time.
- Last, most of the folks running validators can / have been involved in multiple roles within the ecosystem. For example, Staking Facilities and _remz_ have both serving as councilors. If validators still feel the reward is inadequate, then they could potentially create treasury proposals that can earn additional rewards. However, I think the most important concern to weigh is the potential short run interests of validators vs the potential long run interests of the chain as whole. As good utilization of the treasury will create more fees for validators as application developers are onboarded.

**Validator Competition / Barriers to Entry / Costs**

Some related issues.

On increasing the validator count. I am weakly in favor of expanding the validator count at this time and will be more firmly in favor of such a thing, once finality issues are solved in this next upgrade. It seems like Substrate is able to handle a few hundred validators quite well. Increasing the validator count has the effect of lowering the lowest and average stake for a validator. Rewards will decrease for those larger validators who do not create more nodes to join the set.

Remz bringing up a potential misconception on how stake is calculated "Since the reward is computed on the minimum stake, increasing the validator count _decreases_ the reward (exponentially by acting both on decreasing the total reward based on the minimal stake and by dividing it by a greater number of people).” I cannot find references to this as it seems to be based on the current and ideal staking rate, and number of slots ([reference](https://research.web3.foundation/en/latest/polkadot/Token%20Economics.html)). I’ve also done another look at the staking module as well. With that out of the way, there should be less concern to expanding the set.

It shouldn’t make a difference what the dollar amount required to run a single node. On a dollar basis, as long as the current value staked remains the same, it shouldn't matter what dollar amount required to run a single node. With that said, the difference between purchasing the marginal ~$500 of EDG vs ~$5000 makes a large difference to a smaller validator. Instead of the current ~$50-60k of EDG (or ~10m EDG) necessary to enter the set, it would be great to be in the same range as the ~$6-7k to enter the active set for an ETH 2.0 Validator (32 ETH * $200). Having a smaller barrier to entry may entice more community validators to find nominators among the long tail of Edgeware Lockdrop participants. Secondarily, this would also increase the effectiveness of the [Commonwealth Community Validator Program](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/355-commonwealths-community-validator-program).
I don’t have a good gauge on the costs for running various setups (secure or not), and that will definitely impact this as well. If someone wants to create a thread for that, please be my guest.

Overall, I’m curious to hear what everyone thinks. Whatever gets decided by the community would be included in the next network upgrade. I’ll spin up more signaling proposals to gauge sentiment on issues named above.
