# Decent Partners: Edgeware, Kabocha , Revenue and Growth Strategy

## **Introduction**
Edgeware is an experiment in grass-roots value creation, organisation and funding. 

It's holders are in effect a decentralised association of members, brought together by a successful token distribution event - the lockdrop. 

90% of the initial supply was set aside for the public which had the eventual and perhaps inevitable result that no one group could dictate a roadmap.

This has led to many challenges when attempting to communicate the project's primary purpose, since it doesn't easily fit within category norms. 

This is a good thing. 

Edgeware is different. It is not a pre-defined thing. It is a process - an emergent organism, self-evolving into itself. 

It is a digital community, with organisational and economic super-powers that can be tapped by anyone, anywhere with talent, commitment and vision. 

As a result its 'use case' is entirely open to interpretation - this is ours and is the framework we are using to communicate with as we build towards our particular north star. 

## Edgeware structure 

Edgeware is a _public innovation fund (hereon PIF)_ stewarded by a decentralised association of members.

The PIF offers an initial max cap of £25k for research and development grants (_hereon R&D_) to creators across any discipline or category that can make a good enough case to the members who approve all spends. 

The PIF has the opportunity to recoup the grant from Kabocha's Public Pilot Fund (_hereon PPF_), which is itself a product of R&D grants from The PIF. 

The PPF exists to advance the PIF’s R&D experiments into commercial pilots that can generate revenues through licensing and service agreements in a real economy. 

The PPF's funding is initially capped at £250k per pilot. 

In exchange for funding, teams assign the PPF a share of commercial rights in the pilots.

We call this _Cultural Collateral. _

These commercial rights have the potential to return revenue to the PPF in the form of recurring service agreements and licensing revenues from successful pilots. 

The PIF only recoups its initial grant if the PPF funds the pilot or implements the technology created. 

In this way the PIF builds a growing stake in the PPF in the form of KAB, but only if continues to fund projects that receive follow on funding. 

This ensures a simple mechanism to ensure long term value alignment. 

## Decent Partners - Network Services and Commercial Model

Decent Partners (_hereon DP_) mediates the relationship between the PIF and the PPF (_hereon The Funds_) and R&D grants and commercial pilots (hereon _Funding Proposals_), representing the best interests of both.

**Income**

DP puts forward proposals for ongoing operational expenditure to both Funds in the form of 12 month management contracts.

In addition DP receives: 

- 10% of each funding proposal (_hereon Network Service Fee_)
- 10% of any licensing and service agreements that come through commercial exploitation of the pilots (_Hereon the Network Service Share_).


**Incubation through loans not equity **

Decent Partners currently leverages its commercial income to offer teams it is incubating on behalf of The Funds development loans fixed at 0% interest. 

These loans are repayable first in line from revenues derived from successful pilots.

This improves upon the standard terms set by startup incubators who generally take between 6% and 20% of a team's initial shares. 

It also sets a path to creating ventures that deliver positive cashflows generating, rather than looking to exit, ensuring teams can stay aligned to their long term goals.  

**OTC**

Decent Partners will also act as an OTC buyer, enabling project teams to liquidate EDG or KAB into working capital in return for a 10% fee. 

## Example project lifecycle 

Decent Partners offers [Auto Fabrica](https://gov.edgewa.re/discussion/4747-project-auto-fabrica-a-decentralised-creator-collective) (_hereon AF_) support and funding in the form of a repayable 0% loan to develop an R&D grant for The Funds. 

The PIF approves a £25k R&D grant to AF for the creation of a decentralised creator collective. 

The R&D is focused on a feasibility study for an Electric Concept vehicle that enables their community to contribute their talents and earn a stake in the project.

The R&D is successfully delivered and advances to the development of a commercial pilot in the PPF. 

AF now present their vehicle pilot plan to the PPF. 

The PPF funds the pilot with £250k in KAB.

AF assign a % of their rights to the PPF. 

DP takes 10% of the negotiated rights. 

The PIF recoups the initial grant in the form of KAB, taking stake in the funded venture in the process. 

DP cash flows £250k of KAB from AF, offering the team a financial structure that works with their project schedule. The pilot is successfully delivered.

DP negotiates with outside parties who are interested in licensing AF pilot and also in creating service agreements with the creator collective who brought it to life.

Revenues flow back to the PPF and AF via DP who take 10%.

The income is shared with those who contributed to the project, with returns automated depending on the assigned credit to a contributors Seed. This requires legal development and the structure is not yet set. 

The PIF continues to grow its own underlying value by holding increasing stake in the PPF via KAB.

## Relay chain

Successful commercial pilots can be developed with both external partners and also as parachains that are onboarded to either Kusama or our own experimental relay - Pop Art, ensuring each offers a sustainable and scalable value accrual for each network, and in turn the Relay that offers them shared security and interoperability.
