The challenges we face and the opportunity

Sovereignty
The big question that has lingered in crypto now since it became recognised by the media is about regulation and jurisdiction. Yes Bitcoin and altcoins are outside of government control, but only because of the ingenious design of blockchains, giving it excellent and cryptographically elegant defenses. But from a legal standpoint, it has no explicit jurisdiction or structure. It’s basically standing in the middle of the road. And any centralized elements to blockchains, will be on the list to get mowed over by regulators.

One obvious problem is a lack of knowledge on the topic and key concepts and principles of law and jurisdiction. Crypto runs the risk of being absorbed by current states, acting as digital technology that get used by governments which can amplify the authoritarian prone elements of power within current systems. CBDCs are bound to increase oversight over citizens and increase the likelihood of the techno-authoritarian future that definitely doesn’t resonate with the philosophical roots of Bitcoin.

Many thought leaders are signaling the need for sovereignty and “network states”. Frank and his work with Ucadia, is as far as I have researched over the past 7 years, the only solution that can offer humanity an alternative to the current “system”.  

Plan
The plan is to take Web3 from just a digital realm to be sovereign in all realms, this is called “Web3S” (or Web3[s]).

Proposal to pilot SAOs, and make Kabocha and Edgeware, and its surrounding companies. This will then open the door to digital assets and physicals to be held by a blockchain community. 

 “Turn networks into sovereign states called True Trusts, make all members True Persons, and form True Companies. Create education resources so people understand the above terminology, the context, history and concepts, in order to gain greater competence as a community of crypto networks.”
