Occasionally we get inbound requests from would-be translators of EDG content. 
Given that we fund these sorts of network developments and supports using the public fund of the Treasury, we should ponder the best way to structure these engagements. 

For example:

Translator 1 wants to translate a document for some EDG into Mandarin. They apply for a grant and show their previous experience and other writing samples. Their grant is funded and they complete the task.

However, later on, a user or another translator reads the document and finds errors.

**How do we verify the work of would-be translators in a way befitting of the cryptoeconomic space?**

Overall, I would like this process to be
- Permissionless as possible, to reduce friction to expanding access to documentation and knowledge.
- Decentralized, so that no individual is burdened with coordinating these.
- Incentivized, so that translators step forward and perform
- Quality-controlled, so errors are avoided.
- Larger-scale, so we are not funding piecemeal translations of single or few blog articles, but rather large chunks of our written material.
- Sustainable, and designed so that translators are inclined to remain with the project into the future, that these relationships are a public good.



**Some proposed ideas:**

- Translation grants must have two translators who are paired, one to translate and one to verify or editorialize.  
- Translators must stake EDG or reputation to obtain a grant (this is already somewhat true for treasury proposals-- the proposer must bond a percentage of the amount looking to be received or a minimum EDG, whichever is larger) 
- Translators must provide verifiable letters of referral regarding the quality of their work.


I haven't yet had a moment to research how other teams do these- KSM may have some of the best guidance.
