Discussing and outlining a founding charter such as [Decred's Constitution](https://docs.decred.org/governance/decred-constitution/) is a good way to ascertain what kind of culture Edgeware is seeking to foster.

It's also a great opportunity to solicit input from a diverse set of stakeholders and community members and a way to drive use of the emerging Commonwealth governance architecture.

With the project in the very earliest stages, asynchronous conversations the norm, and decentralised governance a work in progress, it is important to utilise the tools available to drive increasing community participation to aid decision making, transparency and to better plot a path forward together.  

With the bridging of different communication channels, an update to Commonwealth and the development of both a new project website and its first major proposal [BuilDAO](https://www.notion.so/BuilDAO-Incubator-Paper-35d451f814014f9cbff96b6c8ab443a4), it seems like it’s a pretty good time to ask the fledgling community a few questions… 

(These are just prompts feel free to ignore)

- What was the motivation that excited the Commonwealth team at the start (individually and collectively)?* 
- The same for the existing council members? 
- What inspired the name and the logo? 
- What first attracted you about the project?
- What problems do you see Edgeware solving?
- What is Edgeware **not** for?
- What other projects do you follow or think Edgeware could improve upon? 
- What lessons do you think Edgeware can learn from other projects?
- What founding values or principles do you think Edgeware should have? (e.g. should the project pay for exchange listings / focus on public benefit etc etc)
- What areas in the wider crypto-sphere interest you?
- What areas or communities outside of crypto interest you?
- What does success look like to you? (what kind of a world do you see?)
- What does failure look like to you?


*Personal motivation is an important thing to understand and has a massive impact in the earliest stages - ultimately you become the culture you create.
