Summary: We have a few levers to pull to ensure our most contributing users remain involved and even more passionate. We need to begin designing those subsystems and tooling and I explore the options here.
--

Recently we discussed more appropriate names for Edgeware users - which is a very Web2, cold way to go about describing someone who participates in the system, which is also a DAO in the case of Edgeware.

I'm a fan of 'membership' in Edgeware, because it requires you to have some stake in the network. Now, this doesn't need to be purely token-holding, but it can also be reputation, and I expect we will integrate reputational tools ASAP since we already have an on-chain ID system that is primed to demonstrate more than just our attested IDs. 

I'm also interested in **micro-bounties**, where tasks are so divided into sub-tasks that we can offer an Amazon Turk-like experience, rewarding EDG from collective pools (like a Working Group budget,) or even the Treasury. 

In all communities, there needs to be a level of influential, highly contributing members. They model community values and norms through behavior, co-enforce these norms --and I want to say we should always use a call-in, rather than call-out culture to enforce these. 

But we cannot be transactional in our approach for many reasons- the first being that it's generally a perverse incentive in that it funds members who are potentially not aligned on value and mission, risks their termination of support when the incentive ends, and there are also legal liability concerns with volunteer programs that begin to look like tit-for-tat. (Something that chain-contractorships clearly butt up against but haven't been tested in any jurisdiction given the nature of blockchains.)

We should begin to consider upgrades to our ID module to leverage better platforming of power members as I mentioned above. Pride and accomplishments are massive drivers; social recognition. Specifically, these might involve avatar overlays, badges, presence on a power member showcase site. We can also make NFTs to ensure the portability of this recognition.

We can also consider powers that come with these recognitions- privileged access to core development teams, sub-forums, chat rooms, or other communities, maybe even technical functions of Edgeware, **perhaps a special collective that occupies a Council seat and acts like a multisig in it's actions.  **Included in this category also may be moderator powers in community chats or forums, the ability to draft and submit tweets under the official Edgeware twitter.

Now swag: we can offer some tangibles but they shouldn't be the core- these might include codes to shirts or hats, invitations to special events, etc. 

We should also consider making contribution decay- recent activity trumps ancient but good contribution, and reassesment should be possible.


As we approach further upgrades, thinking about how we want to situate those users who are not council, but have strong influence over the progression and norms of Edgeware will be key.
