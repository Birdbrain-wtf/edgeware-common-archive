Currently, Cryptovoxels, Decentraland and other major digital-land VR projects quantify and boundary their core resource, but my point is that VR doesn't need to be this way. Using scale (within technological limits) we can have a city on a tabletop, or a planet-sized party room between Earth and Mars.

Don't like where you are? Shrink down and see whats happening in the cellular scale.  

When VR projects come to Edgeware (This is an essential component of my vision for a replication of civic life in Edgeware-  the creation of realistic space to engage.)  ... they should consider more innovative routes of economic modeling for that space- specifically they shouldn't use space as the scarce resource at all. The creation of a cathedral the size of Hungary should not be more expensive than other uses- the value we gain from being able to generate, explore and share such a cathedral is far more important. 

**We have reiterated the capacity for  creativity based on wealth.**

**A potential solution instead of boundaries using the traditional dimensions, is to derive value from the ability to perceive entities. Overlapping projects and cities can occupy similar space, maximizing convenience (although teleportation is built in frequently,) but still be permissioned and financialized.  **

Overall, my repeat concern as we build the new technological landscape is why do we reiterate the limits of meat-space reality into the digital, over and over? 

These are the only economic models we know comfortably- we have not engaged artists or critical thinkers sufficiently to improve on them, we are financially incentivized not to innovate and instead devour low-hanging fruit until the competition arrives. Alas!
