## Narrative development

At the root of any narrative development there are typically four key stages.
Insight - Set the scene, introduce the big picture, what is the context to the story you are telling?

Problem - within the context you have framed, what is the primary issue?

Solution - what is the compelling answer to the problem and context you have set?

How it works - explain how the solution works, from high level to deep dive.
This approach gives structure to storytelling and applies pretty much universally - from startups to filmmaking, crypto-networks and even memes - the atomic units of storytelling which bundle context, problem and solution in one image...

This structured approach can waterfall across every aspect of a project, from a helicopter perspective to deep down into the weeds of a complex technological stack. 

Let's take the (simplified) original positioning of Apple.
Insight - Computers will change the world but...

Problem - Right now only software engineers understand them...

Solution - Introducing Apple. We make computers everyone can use.

How it works - An intuitive human machine interface etc
From this strategic thinking came the iconic:

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d0/Apple_logo_Think_Different_vectorized.svg)

And one of the [most famous ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5sMBhDv4sik)adverts of all time, all of it derived from a killer insight about a great product and the aspiration of potential users.

The same for Nike, the brand that Steve Job's derived his [inspiration:](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=keCwRdbwNQY)
Insight - If you have a body you are an athlete but...

Problem - Finding motivation to exercise is hard.  

Solution - Introducing Nike. We bring inspiration and innovation to every athlete in the world.

How it works - Training gear, apps, experience
![](https://miro.medium.com/max/3360/1*AEPYrlmqvZJxcK8ZpDQkWg.jpeg)

It's not about explaining shoe technology, it's about inspiring an emotional response and connecting that storytelling to those who are primed to engage by virtue of their existing interests, values, associations or aspirations.

## Crypto-Networks

For the most part, crypto-networks / blockchains / distributed ledgers / DAOs are at the same stage as computers were before Apple came along.

Whilst there is no shortage of excitement among those who believe fervently that the technology will transform every aspect of society, they are complex, hard to explain and also have an additional issue which Nike and Apple didn't face - there are now economic incentives in play that drive rampant speculation.

With crypto-networks the fundamentals of storytelling have not changed, but the mechanisms for distilling and perpetuating narratives through emergent social systems have.

The arrival of blockchain networks such as Edgeware with treasury systems, proposal systems and the potential to invest in projects over multi-decade time horizons also impacts the way a narrative is architected, executed and persisted.

From [Headless Brands](https://otherinter.net/web3/headless-brands/):

> While brands have traditionally been planned and designed directly by corporations, the rise of networked media has challenged the coherence of centrally-managed brand identities. New blockchain-based decentralized organizations take this a step further by giving users financial incentive to spread brand narratives of their own

## Cultural Evolution of Crypto Networks

Currently the crypto ecosystem puts huge emphasis on the _categorisation_ of a project — currency, store of value, smart contract platform, attempting to define competitors through monetary theory, economic design, product insight or potential use case.

Differentiating [cryptocurrencies](https://multicoin.capital/2018/05/09/on-the-network-effects-of-stores-of-value/) through analogies to software products is undervaluing the fundamentally social nature of crypto networks, with the ‘best’ technology, block size or economic model trumped by a story that connects.

> Bitcoin’s growth resembles that of a social movement.
> Social movements are purposeful, organized groups striving to work toward a common goal.
> These groups might be attempting to create change (Occupy Wall Street, Arab Spring), to resist change (anti-globalization movement), or to provide a political voice to those otherwise disenfranchised (civil rights movements).
> Social movements create social change.
> [The United States of Shitcoin](https://medium.com/@Melt_Dem/the-united-states-of-shitcoin-1362a6e0a40e), [Meltem Demirrors](https://medium.com/@Melt_Dem)
