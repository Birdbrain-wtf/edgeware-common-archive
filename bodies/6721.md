## **Summary**
_Pallet Version Control Docs enables the sequential hashing and timestamping of arbitrary data from a governance or publishing platform into the blocks of a Substrate based blockchain, creating foundational assurances that can form the basis for securing higher layer information structures._

## **Background**
In principle DAOs exist to enable a digital collective to coordinate, contribute and make decisions upon creative initiatives in a decentralised manner. The central thesis being that over time they will assemble a greater collective intelligence than is possible in today’s existing organisational structures.

If the promise is to be realised, then trust in the provenance of underlying data structures is paramount, since higher order implementations will only be as credible as the sources on which they depend. 

Existing organisational frameworks for these collectives focus primarily on reading on-chain data and providing a more intuitive interface for members to discuss, debate and move forward together. 

Version controlled documents are a base expectation in most group collaboration scenarios: high-school projects, software development, academia and newsrooms and are fundamental to establishing and iterating human consensus. 

## **Problem**
The rise of social media and the erosion of trust in media can be rooted to a foundational disconnect in the provenance of data. With no shared, tamper resistant and credibly neutral ledger to verify sources of information, or indeed their historical evolution, we are left adrift in a fog of stories with no way to ascertain the relative merit or indeed motivation behind an emerging narrative.   

If we look to the early _news-rooms_ of Web3, we see that they are the governance forums of crypto communities. These are forums in the very truest and oldest sense of the world - places where individuals self-assemble into proto-digital societies able to develop their own bureaucratic, financial and cultural systems. 

Despite it being easy to spin up a governance interface for your community using Snapshot, Commonwealth or any number of other Web3 forums, there is less focus given to the assurances the information shared by members will be preserved, especially when most governance interfaces rely on centralised servers for data storage or on pinning information to IPFS, which is effectively a 3rd party service on a chain outside of the Substrate ecosystem. 

Beyond the basic data provenance issues, we also find issues with how changes to first order information is recorded during the process of debate and dialogue as governance proposals iterate and evolve based on rounds of feedback. 

Currently no system exists within Substrate for a native version control system to provide long term and irrefutable assurances for public posts within governance interfaces.

This results in a severe limitation on the basic trust assumptions for those engaging in reasoned debate and indeed for the scalability of the emerging data structures and in turn the collective intelligence of the emerging network of contributors.

## **Existing work and inspiration**
Inspired by Peter Todd’s[ OpenTimestamps](https://petertodd.org/2016/opentimestamps-announcement), Decred developers implemented [dcrtime](https://docs.decred.org/advanced/dcrtime/) that now forms the source of truth for their off-chain proposal system [Politeia](https://proposals.decred.org). 

dcrtime is a timestamping application that allows users to store hashes of arbitrary data on the Decred blockchain. 

Parity developer Shawn Tabrizi hacked together a Proof of Existence blockchain on Substrate to show the capabilities of the technology.

Proof of Existence is an online service that verifies the existence of computer files as of a specific time via timestamped transactions in the bitcoin blockchain.

- [https://github.com/shawntabrizi/substrate-proof-of-existence](https://github.com/shawntabrizi/substrate-proof-of-existence)


We believe that Substrate’s native flexibility and interoperability can expand on the usefulness of these examples and open up interesting new avenues for experimentation. 

## **About the pallet**
_Pallet_Version_Control _addresses the issue of publishing provenance and version control with the initial use case focused on offering credible and durable assurances for communities wishing to govern their projects through open and meritocratic means. 

In the future, we imagine the pallet forming one of the core primitives for an entire decentralised publishing stack that can gradually enable open, sovereign and scalable media organisations alongside their associated identities, rights and attributions. 

Principally, the pallet focuses around storing “Documents” - data that groups an author, a hash representing the content, and a version number.

## **Where it can go**
With a basic blockchain based version control system verifying and tracking changes from contributors to a governance system, many new things become possible - for example the creation of new publishing operations that can better track fast moving stories across a decentralised network of journalists. 

By offering contributors and readers credible assurances as to the veracity of the data and objective crypto-graphic proofs that document the evolution of a particular narrative or understanding we can provide far stronger foundations, checks and balances when it comes to anything that requires collective sensemaking. 

We hope that with the basic technology in place, this system can inspire other projects to make use of the underlying information structures, offering novel new opportunities for something like Web3 journalism to emerge. 

## **Integration with other tools**
