## Diversity is Edgeware's super power and greatest weakness.

Edgeware is an blockchain based internet organisation made up of many strangers contributing across many platforms in many different ways.

We speak _different languages_, come from _different cultures_ and bring _different perspectives_.

We create in _different forms_, we understand ideas through _different lenses_ and we prefer _different communication mediums and styles_ to make our points.

Some like _writing_, others do their talking with _code_, some prefer to tell their story via _imagery_, others speak their truth in _logos and symbols, _others find _talking_ the best way to illuminate their creativity. 

Some of us contribute under _our own names_, some _anonymously_, some _a mix of both_, some maybe even under_ a few aliases_ and others under _team or collective names_.

We utilise governance forums that leave _no room for nuance, _with a user experience designed by those who don't _understand our specific challenges, _in a system where our _data and credentials are locked in. _

We make decisions using _binary on-chain goverance decisions_, that leave _no room for nuance_ or an ability to create binding _on chain agreements_. 

Given all of this, is it surprising that _communication_ is hard?

That commonalities get swamped by our perceived _differences_?

Or that people prefer to see _malice_, where perhaps there is only _incompetence_? 

**Diversity, when channeled well becomes a super-power, but when not understood correctly, becomes an existential weakness. **

## K.I.S.S. (Keep It Simple Stupid) 

Given the above, we have to approach things with a KISS mentality. 

Every layer of compexity we add to Edgeware, it's technology, tools, processes, organisational structures and culture compounds the aforementioned problems.

We therefore need to get into the habit - a real and dedicated pattern of asking really good questions, again and again, about what we focus on and why... 

**Less WenWenWen... and more WhyWhyWhy?**

## Why, WhyWhyWhy is hard

The challenge is that crypto _marketing _(and the advertising industrial complex in general_) _is rooted in the ability to over-sell a communities capabilities, a products features, or the ability to _change the world_.  

_Ask yourself_ -  can a deoderant address inquality? Does your favourite influencer really care about your financial wellbeing? Do data monopolies really understand whats best for you or good for them?

Web2 based marketing models rely on clicks, likes, subscribers, views and ATTENTION that are turbo-charged using paid media, cookies and all sorts of psychological profiling expressly designed to press the lizard brain inside us all. 

This is the story of NGU (number go up) and literally every part of our on-line and much of our off-line lives is dedicated to this pursuit... 

WenWenWen... is a Web2 story where everything is optimised for NGU (and conversely for NGD), since **VOLATILITY PAYS BABY**!!! Remember, all of Web2 and pretty much all of Web3 (exchanges, market makers, token issuers, VCs, hedge funds, traders etc) make money on both sides...  

WhyWhyWhy... is a Web3 story and is born from the spirit of the Five Whys - which was made famous by Sakichi Toyoda in the early 1900s - the creator of Toyota cars / engineering.

> The 3 Whys is precisely what you think it is - asking the question "why?" three times to get to the _real_ root of a question or problem.
> Let's look at an example of The 3 Whys in action:

> John Smith (JS) talking to his boss: _"I think I need to quit, I really don't like my job."_
> John's Boss: _"**Why** don't you like your job?"_
> JS: _"The atmosphere isn't what it used to be when I started. It feels toxic and I never used to hate showing up for work, but now I do."_
> Boss: _"**Why** do you hate showing up for work?"_
> JS: _"The culture that existed when I started working here has changed. It's just not the same."_
> Boss: _"**Why** do think the culture has changed?"_
> JS: _"Well, it's that new guy, Tom. He's so negative. He has a comment for everything. He's frustrating to be around and really hurts our team's dynamic."_

Read more [here](https://www.inc.com/jason-surfrapp/the-theory-of-the-3-whys.html).

So lets try and get into the habit of asking more WhyWhyWhys and less WenWenWens, it's the only thing we need to really focus on.
