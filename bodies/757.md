This is also an opportunity for newer members of the community to share their experience of joining the project - the good, the bad and the indifferent. 

Community standards, processes and values do not emerge fully formed - this is as good a time as any to set parameters for debate and to open up a dialogue, especially as we move towards the launch of the new Edgeware website and its focus on providing a [network for DAOs/Collectives](https://edgeware-website.vercel.app). 

Blockchain based digital organisations have the potential to set new standards in transparency, accountability and impact, to be better than the old systems we claim are no longer fit for purpose.

However as with any new and unregulated technology, the counter is also true - there is a temptation for short term decision making and profiteering. 

The key is to find a balance between defences of two competing philosophies - conservatism (build slowly, avoid mistakes) and progressivism (fail fast, break things). 

## **A quick reminder of the opportunity **

The project began with a lot of hope and excitement post lockdrop - with over 15m addresses taking part, putting the project in the top 5 most widely distributed coins and spawning an active community with a large treasury. 

Developments around [Commonwealth.im](https://commonwealth.im) further demonstrated the opportunity for the platform to move the dial when it comes to governing on-chain communities and expertise of the core team. 

On this, the team at CW and their backers have done a great job and it appears that there is a desire to be good stewards of Edgeware - certainly when reading the various responses to the[ founding culture](https://commonwealth.im/edgeware/proposal/discussion/536-why-are-you-here-edgeware-founding-values-culture-and-focus) question.

The recent emergence of working groups and collectives across a range of areas demonstrates that it is possible to rethink and restart community efforts. 

## **The role of the council **

There is a yawning chasm of discontentment and mistrust within the council - a situation that manifests as uncertainty and confusion outside of the main players (and even amongst them), a factor that degrades conditions for the creation of a resilient and productive community culture.

In many ways the existing dysfunction is a result of the differing perspectives on just what responsibility and accountability this position demands and the opportunities it should afford.

In the current protocol design, the council can both propose and approve treasury spends - this is therefore a position that grants enormous power to those elected. 

As such, it is intuitive that being a council member should be seen as a responsibility, not just an opportunity and should bring with it some expectations from the community. 

## **The public goods narrative**

The long term narrative of Edgeware has been to focus on[ public goods funding](https://blog.edgewa.re/edgeware-2020/).

> In the real world, one strategy for economic development is charter cities, which use clear rules for corporate governance, good infrastructure, and direct subsidies to create economic growth.
> We can think of Edgeware as a similar effort in the Polkadot ecosystem, that aims to use governance to build a thriving ecosystem around natural resources, infrastructure, and a prime location.
> With on-chain governance, a large treasury, and builDAO at its core, Edgeware should focus on serving as an incubator for valuable new projects.
> Unlike other blockchains that take the philosophy of a neutral, un-opinionated platform, we expect participants will actively direct the networks’ resources towards partners and developers, as well as public goods that benefit everyone in the ecosystem.
> While it's too early to say how this will play out, we think this is a narrative that the chain should focus around.

It should be obvious, but it is worth reiterating, that the long term viability of this narrative is entirely dependent on the levels of trust granted to those who control the levers of power and in turn their duties as stewards of public funding.

## **What can be done?**

This is the start of a conversation and the hope is that this opens the door for a wide range of perspectives to come forward and for this to then lead to actionable proposals and emerging standards.

Some thought starters (feel free to add):
 
- What are the norms around conflicts of interest - real and perceived for elected members?
- What should be expected when it comes to clear and timely updates and reporting - good and bad on the progress of funded initiatives? 
- What kind of ongoing dialogue should the community expect from those elected to council positions? 
- What motivations do council members have with regard to the direction of the project?
- What are the expectations with regard to engagement in the voting of proposals? 
- Should the council have the power to propose and approve proposals? 
- What are other options for the governance of the treasury? 
- What can the project learn from existing governance standards in PLCs, governments and other publicly accountable bodies?
