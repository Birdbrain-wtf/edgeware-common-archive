**Summary: **This article is primarily a speculative vision for how the Working Groups may evolve over time, and helps inform the skill-tree model I proposed in the Games Working Group recently:
## 
## Stage 1: Working Group
**An unsustainable task force**
 Designed to  discover, investigate and plan an initial solution to a challenge, many working groups focus on increase awareness of the birth-chain, convert users and partners and generated use-cases.  Very 1-1 efforts, ad hoc tactics, and non-scaling conversions towards goals. Limited by resources including funding, people, and talent. 

Should a process for solving a challenge be discovered, and smaller grants by the birth-chain or other 3rd party be reliable, they may receive their own discretionary funding, enabling them to become an agency. 

Key Events:
- Formation
- Reaching 60 Group Participants
- Reaching 9-12 Core Members
- **Transformational Event: **Forms a multisig and receives funding.
- **Transformational Event: **200 participants.


_Example: Games Working Group, Artist Working Group_

## Stage 2: Agency / Firm
**A funds-redeployer contracting other workers and agencies **
A KPI-centric strategic board deploying funds seeking to measure return on those funds and optimize their use.  Discretionary funding from the birth-chain allows this entity to contract other agencies, contractors, and specialists on their own terms, rather than relying on scope-limited grants or bounties. Whether they wisely deploy their funding  determines whether they devolve to a Working Group, grow into their next stage, or cease operations. 

They may also engage in capacity building in order to in-source work or provide talent related to their initial mission to other peers or groups in the birth-chain’s ecosystem, but may also provide outsource talent at a premium.
 
The capacity building may come at a variety of costs- including the taking of personal tokens of apprentices or revenue sharing agreements. In groups of this stage, there is a spectrum of non-profit and for-profit activity, where break-even capacity building may be the norm earlier on. 

Key Events:
- Applies for and receives discretionary funding
- Participants use a multisignature wallet
- Deploys funding to participants or outside agents. 
- **Transformational Event:** Becomes financially self-sustainable.



_Examples: Brand Working Group (post-funding)_

## Stage 3: DAOglomerate
**Mission-oriented, (but coincidentally so,) conglomerate with venture characteristics**
As the birth-chain matures, the agency has operational security independent of grants due to a warchest whose value has presumably increased by orders of magnitude under conditions of success.  

It may be composed of several substructures, have a dedicated financial wing  investing and shielding assets,  engage in venture-backing other organizations and take token ownership in these orgs.  Through it’s original Working Group niche, it likely has accumulated talent and information that allow it to excel within that niche, but this is no longer required given the immense resources and security it has.  

While it  is an immense asset to the chain, beyond the financial holdings, it is likely incentivized to operate cross-chain, and other chains would be benefited from the relationships. Due to having reached financial escape velocity, the birth-chain may consider ways to limit the options of the DAOglomerate, specifically in blocking it from benefitting other ecosystems- it may seek to do this through financial action, including purchasing/trading and holding DAO tokens (if they were issued) to retain a controlling stake in the DAOglomerates’ behavior. 

Key Events:
- A wealth event occurs, including the appreciation of their assets.
- They create their own token, or hold other tokens.


_Examples: None_
