## **2. Overview **
Decent is a digital cooperative developing a cultural collateral backed token, community and blockchain ecosystem bridging multiple chains, assets and Web2 and Web3 media

The project brings together an experienced network of creators, entrepreneurs and communities, from across traditional, digital and crypto media, technology and finance, united by public service values that prioritise trust, transparency and long term thinking. 

Decent approaches the challenge of launching, sustaining, governing and mainstreaming blockchain based community networks via **a five step strategy**.

1. **DecentDAO** - progressive decentralising governance of the ecosystem and handing over the reserve holdings and treasury to the community, creating a publicly owned and operated ecosystem born for Web3 media..

## **3. Ghost Towns**
Blockchain technologies offer alternative mechanisms for digital communities to organise, capitalise and co-own digital assets, however projects are plagued by volatile prices, poor governance, lack of transparency and low levels of awareness, engagement and adoption.  

Currently the crypto ecosystem puts huge emphasis on the _categorisation_ of a project — currency, store of value, smart contract platform, attempting to differentiate through monetary theory, economic design, transaction throughput, product insight or potential use case. 

Differentiating[ cryptocurrencies](https://multicoin.capital/2018/05/09/on-the-network-effects-of-stores-of-value/) through a product or engineering mindset undervalues the fundamentally social nature of crypto networks, with the ‘best’ technology, block size or economic model just one factor in a ecosystemic affair that relies on a community that can reach, engage and empower a diverse set of network participants who would ordinarily not work together in such a coordinated and open fashion.

From a brand awareness perspective, there is an assumption that marketing is analogous to building a community, when in fact the latter is far more about cooperation and collaboration, taking a role closer to the facilitator of a community’s intrinsic ambitions, rather than authors of a top down message.

> While brands have traditionally been planned and designed directly by corporations, the rise of networked media has challenged the coherence of centrally-managed brand identities. New blockchain-based decentralized organizations take this a step further by giving users financial incentive to spread brand narratives of their own.
> [Headless Brands](https://otherinter.net/web3/headless-brands/), Toby Shorin, Laura Lotti, Sam Hart

## **4. Way of the DAO**
Ethereum evolved Bitcoin’s design, enabling smart contracts that could form more complex agreements, simplifying business logic and enabling rapid coordination between actors at greater scale, speed and complexity.

One of the most seductive of the Ethereum communities early concepts was [‘The DAO’,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_DAO_(organization)) which promised to do away with the need for a company, instead handing over all of the financial, legal and organisational functions to code.

‘The DAO’ raised over $150m in Ethereum from over 11,000 investors, making it the largest crowdfunding event in history. However users exploited a vulnerability in the code enabling them to siphon off $50m of funds. It was one of Ethereum’s most newsworthy events and is still referenced today.

Since then a huge amount of progress has been made around DAOs. 

- [Aragon](https://aragon.org), [DAOstack](https://daostack.io) and [Colony](https://colony.io/) have built DAO platforms on Ethereum, promising to simplify the establishment and management of digital native organisations.
- [Decred](https://decred.org) has focused on building a DAO around its native asset DCR, pioneering thoughtfully designed off-chain and on-chain governance including a block derived treasury system governed via randomised ticket voting through its proposal system [Politeia](https://proposals.decred.org).  
- [Polkadot](https://polkadot.network) focuses on cross chain interoperability and its blockchain SDK [Substrate](http://substrate.dev/), enabling the simplified creation of PoS blockchains with on-chain governance, consensus upgradability and wealthy chain treasuries.
- [Commonwealth.im](https://commonwealth.im) now offers an interface for managing on-chain communities, making the coordination of proposals, the development of culture and network upgrades increasingly easy.
- [Boardroom](https://governance.substack.com/p/introducing-boardroom) speaks to this governance infrastructure trend and introduces an easier way to manage the politics of protocols and the [various politicians ](https://app.boardroom.info/politicians)representing key stakeholders.


As governance infrastructure matures, it is interesting to see the challenges that the various approaches are facing and the opportunities this presents. 

**Decred**
When it comes to governing protocols, Decred has the most established, transparent and well monitored system, alongside a treasury with over [$9m](https://explorer.dcrdata.org/address/Dcur2mcGjmENx4DhNqDctW5wJCVyT3Qeqkx). 

Stakeholder governance engagement is relatively high with an “average (mean) turnout of 27.9%, with a total of 431,496 ticket votes being cast”, however that engagement seems to be falling as the project struggles to attract developer talent and broad awareness of its capabilities something we noted in [this essay](https://github.com/monsieurbulb/forksintheroad/blob/master/Decred_forks_in_the_road.md) regarding the projects capability for scaling DAOs. 

A recent[ review of the data](https://richardred.medium.com/year-two-of-decreds-politeia-in-numbers-and-graphs-1a7f36c61b99) demonstrated that building reputation has a direct correlation with proposal approvals, demonstrating the value of showing up and building trust with stakeholders before requesting funding.

**Polkadot / Kusama **
The successful rollout of the first substrate based chains has raised excitement for the potential of new cross-chain interoperability and asset transfer and a more nuanced approach to governance, including the introduction of a chain council made up of nominated members who can bias voting and control submission and approval of funding.

Both projects are early in their life cycles and so governance engagement remains relatively low - incentivisation mechanisms are being developed such as [ticket lotteries](https://hackmd.io/68rduBydTEy4X-ULevd90g) with the aim of boosting council and user participation. 

**Edgeware**
Following the launch of Polkadot and Kusama, Commonwealth Labs launched Substrate chain Edgeware using an innovative lockdrop, achieving a distribution strategy that created one of the market’s top 5 best distributed coins.

The token design set a high initial inflation of 20% and a treasury reward that has pooled around 600m EDG ($4-5m), some of which has been spent on a Grants fund for Commonwealth, alongside a 50m EDG grant for incubator project BuilDAO. 

The projects stated ambition is to become an [‘incubator’ ](https://blog.edgewa.re/edgeware-2020/)for the Polkadot ecosystem.

> With on-chain governance, a large treasury, and builDAO at its core, Edgeware should focus on serving as an incubator for valuable new projects. Unlike other blockchains that take the philosophy of a neutral, un-opinionated platform, we expect participants will actively direct the networks’ resources towards partners and developers, as well as public goods that benefit everyone in the ecosystem.

The project has great potential but is facing early issues around the governance of its council, the transparency and accountability of its treasury funding and a series of technical issues that have slowed momentum in its nascent community. 

**Defi**
Decentralized Finance (defi) if the fastest growing segment of the crypto space. It's a category of projects and initiatives to build a new financial system on-chain that is permissionless, decentralized, and much more cost efficient to interact with than the traditional or centralised financial system. 

The speed at which product development is taking off is increasing the critical need for governance structures that would ensure these networks run smoothly as they grow and scale. As a result there is increased experimentation with “fair launches” that ensure equitable distribution of tokens which give governance rights to holders.  

Barnbridge as an example is in the early days of its token distribution using a “proof of value” approach. Interested parties in the protocol stake financial value which must be locked through weekly epochs to earn the $BOND tokens that assign governance rights to holders, and eventually determine how the fees generated by the protocol will be apportioned across stakeholder groups. 

This ‘[DAO first’](https://medium.com/barnbridge/dao-first-a-new-governance-model-863e8434bf00) approach has sought to learn from governance failures and issues that have marked the launch of other prominent protocols like YFI, Compound, Uniswap, while taking cues from more successful launches like Synthetix, and Aave.

## **5. Enter Moloch**
[MolochDAO](https://www.molochdao.com/) set out to solve issues around digital coordination issues using Ethereum’s smart contracts to enable member managed capital that could be deployed rapidly as Ethereum ecosystem grants.

The core insight was that the collective intelligence of those building the bleeding edge technology of Ethereum knew best where money, talent and resources should be directed, with the technology enabling grants to be developed, proposed, approved and tracked far faster than a traditional foundation structure.  

Beyond Moloch DAO itself, the framework has evolved and now enables ETH based multi-token collateral, stablecoins such as DAI and a simplified proposal funnel leading to over 100 Moloch forks through recently launched [DAOHaus](https://daohaus.club/stats). 

Moloch’s thinking then evolved into a for-profit venture, with [Metacartel Ventures](https://github.com/metacartel/MCV/blob/master/Whitepaper.pdf) and [The LAO](https://medium.com/openlawofficial/the-lao-a-for-profit-limited-liability-autonomous-organization-9eae89c9669c) enabling DAOs to be bridged to US corporations in Delaware and Wyoming using [OpenLaw’s](https://www.openlaw.io/) set of legally linked smart contracts. 

This achieved several important things: 

- Members of the DAO benefitted from the legal protections, assurances and limited liability of traditional corporate structures, lowering the risk of assigning rights, contracting suppliers and protecting members from the actions of the entity. 
- The DAO could now invest both in tokens **and/or** equity or other real world assets such as patents or profit sharing agreements held on a company's balance sheet, with a [SAFE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_agreement_for_future_equity) or [SAFT](https://saftproject.com/) agreement representing future rights on issuance of tokens.  
- The simplification of legal arrangements, investment processes and capital allocation and saving time and money compared to a traditional structure.


The LAO currently holds over $5m from over 100 members (recently upgraded to 200) and has successfully deployed over $3m to projects across the Ethereum ecosystem.

Both The LAO and Metacartel Ventures also introduced the idea of DAO [Scouts](https://scout.thelao.io/) and DAO [Mentors](https://www.thelao.io/mentors)**, **positions that confer rights in the project through subsidiary tokens, thus expanding the collective intelligence of the organisation.

The high engagement in both projects is a direct result of members having real skin in the game, a relatively small number of participants and a focused mission. By starting as a ‘squad’ there is more intimacy and trust has a shorter distance to travel, making it easier to maintain than with an amorphous ‘community’ who have to figure out each other’s motives and capabilities in a far more complex economic and social environment.

## **6. Token Tribalism**

> Tokens naturally inspire tribalism - since greed / price discussion dominates the conversation and actually distracts from much of the progress made in the space.
> However, the broader shared goal for credible project teams is in building a fairer, more decentralised future that offers users better control and coordination of their information, money and communities.
> Put simply, crypto teams by and large share more in common than the crypto-twitter flame wars and trolling would have you believe.

This tribalism doesn’t just compound community filter bubbles, it also depletes resources and dismisses learnings as projects continually rehash mistakes made by others, whilst failing to appreciate the shared values teams share. Collective intelligence is replaced with binary perspectives, shill armies and maximalist tendencies that work well in an ad-supported crypto media environment, where shock tactics, singular voices and division thrive.  

> It is also obvious that there is already a lot of duplication of effort as each team attempts to solve the same awareness and adoption issues, especially around community building, governance engagement, proposal templates and visions.

With inter-chain interoperability and asset transfer, the overarching concept of a MetaDAO that exists as a ‘supra-national’ entity is inevitable as cooperation between projects and communities takes precedence since no one team or community has all the answers. 

> ...in principle the idea would be to research a range of core issues facing fully on-chain economies that are setting out to escape centralisation vectors and to create a 'Meta DAO' to address them that benefits from ongoing investment from a range of projects.

## **7. Digital Cooperatives**
The failure to govern public goods on a planet with finite resources is at the heart of the 21st century’s greatest challenges and with existing system faltering, there has been a renewed interest in older ways to steward common pool resources - both man made and natural assets.

In the UK an alternative approach to the legally linked structure has emerged, led by [Nexus Mutual](https://nexusmutual.io/) - ‘a people powered alternative to insurance’ that is established as [a companu limited by guarantee](https://nexusmutual.gitbook.io/docs/docs#legal-framework) with its [articles of association ](https://nexusmutual.io/pages/NexusMutualArticlesV1.0.pdf)capturing its status as a mutual - with members liability limited to £1. 

The project currently insures smart contracts, but its founding mission is to offer insurance where the market is not incentivised to provide cover. Examples include earthquakes and natural disasters. 

Key aspects of the mutual include:

- **Limited Liability **- each Member's liability is limited to £1. This means if the mutual was to ever run out of funds each member would be liable to provide an additional £1 only
- **Register of Members **- as each member becomes a guarantor of the mutual it is required that they be listed on the register of members. This register is held by the mutual (off-chain) and contains the Names and Contact Address (or substitute) for each member.
- **Wind-Up** - if the mutual is to be wound up, for example, on agreement by the members, then any funds in excess of contributions made (for example from investment earnings) are to be donated to charity. This ensures the mutual as a whole can never make any profit.
- **Withdrawal** - the mutual may request that any member withdraw from the mutual. This would only be enacted via a membership proposal in a relatively extreme situation where the member's participation endangers the ongoing operation of the mutual.


The mutual is a form of cooperative - a structure that predates the origins of the joint stock company, upgrading it for a digital world.

A cooperative seeks to align incentives between members who pool, govern and assign shared resources and also receive something in return. The contribution could be providing services to the collective, investing capital or assigning other useful assets.

There is often a misconception that co-ops are non-profits. Like traditional companies, co-ops are profit-seeking private enterprises that act in the interest of their shareholders.

A[ recent bill](https://www.uk.coop/new-co-ops-bill-parliament-great-people-and-planet) introduced to the UK Parliament aims to make co-operatives a more powerful tool for people, local initiatives and the planet, ensuring their status is protected long into the future. 

- give co-operative societies the option of legally preventing their capital surplus (what is left after share capital is repaid) being distributed among members upon dissolution 
- give co-operative societies the option of legally preventing their future conversion to a company 
- provide a new equity instrument that is repayable at the option of the society rather than withdrawable at the option of the shareholder

## **15. Legal Structure **
Decent is being established as a digital co-operative utilising the aforementioned company limited by guarantee structure, alongside a bespoke set of constitutional documents linked to smart contracts and bespoke DAO interfaces that control on-chain activity.  

Membership is assigned based on non-tradeable voting blocks and there will be no profits, though the legal entity is able to hold and manage traditional and digital assets on its balance sheet and disburse dividends.

The final structure is in development but we are studying both Nexus Mutual and the [artist owned cooperative](https://docs.ampled.com/coop/) governance model established by [Ampled](https://www.ampled.com/) alongside consulting with established London law firm [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/) and cooperative experts including [Cooperatives UK](https://www.uk.coop/). 

All value flowing into Decent will be reinvested into the cooperative, its expanding network of partners and in funding cultural collateral that continues to be assigned by its members and provides the fundamental value capture of the network. 

Members will receive $DECENT which is minted in return for valuable contributions to the network that can be capital, crypto-assets or valuable services. 

Members will govern the ecosystem through its various stages before handing over control of the network to the community as the emerges DecentDAO.
