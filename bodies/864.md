Entrepreneur in residence is a common role one might see in the VC world. It is a role that signifies a lot of trust and ability to execute on a given task, be it starting a new company, building a team, and scaling to success. In the blockchain world however, we can get more creative. In fact, I think there's ample room for many "in residence" roles for individuals who want to leverage their skills and have ownership over their time and work.

In this spirit, I think the one of the most impactful roles we can aim to fill would be an _**Edgeware Developer In Residence (DIR)**_: a developer who instills trust through their track record in the industry of developing great tools & products, potentially answering questions and helping other developers, and more. This role would be more open-ended than most, where the _**DIR**_ has the ability to chart their own path in the tools and products they work on, but solely to further the success of Edgeware.
This role could be broken down along a number of different tracks and ideally entirely funded by the Edgeware Treasury. Some tracks come to mind:
- **Tooling -** a tooling focused _**DIR**_ is entirely trusted to build out whatever developer tooling they may use for their own pursuits as well as see fit for other developers in the community.
- **Product -** a product focused _**DIR**_ is entirely trusted to build user-facing products for the users of Edgeware. This could be in the form of faucets or ways of viewing chain data in novel ways.
- **Protocol -** a protocol focused _**DIR**_ is entirely focused on building protocols (if they can manage launching more than one). These protocols would be community run, since they are launched by a paid community developer.


So far, I've explored this idea with a few developers from the Ethereum community and have received decent feedback around its goals and freedom for capable developers. The costs involved directly reflect the quality of the output and so are not entirely cheap. For starters, it represents a good signal as to what kind of developer the Edgeware Treasury would be ok paying for.

If the appetite is there, I believe we can find a capable _**DIR**_ for market rate or less. In filling this role it is paramount that we identify the KPIs that we can measure success against. Some ideas come to mind:
- _**Tooling DIR**_ - Publishing at least 1 usable tools for active developers each month
- _**Product DIR**_ - Publishing at least 1 usable product for Edgeware users each month
- _**Protocol DIR**_ - Developing at least 1 usable protocol for Edgeware every 3 months.


Each of these roles depends on other community roles for broadcasting and handling community management, opening up avenues for working groups to help these developers in residence in their pursuit to build awesome technology. To that end, our current collectives could grow to improve the success of roles like this as well, by enabling the _**DIR**_ to focus on the work they are interested in and benefit from strong working groups to help deploy and handle community engagements. All in all, it would drive value back to the protocol and make Edgeware a scalable community platform.

Key questions for the community are:
- What kind of _**DIR**_ do we think is most useful? For developers themselves? For the chain to sustain itself with new sources of value?
- Should this be wrapped in to a proposal for 2021 core development team personnel?
- What are the KPIs to measure these _**DIR**_s against?
