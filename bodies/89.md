What is Social Key Recovery?

Social Key Recovery is a tactic for getting back a lost password that is based on Shamir’s Secret Sharing Scheme (SSSS). You might have already heard of HTC’s cryptocurrency phone, Exodus, putting it into practice, or perhaps their app, Zion. The idea is basically that you can share your password among friends, and if you lose your key, your friends can help you out. Social Key Recovery is an easy-to-use system that could become an integral part of Edgeware for ensuring account security and access in extraordinary circumstances (which could range from unavailability of the main key, to death of the main password holder).


How does Shamir's Secret Sharing Scheme work?

SSSS allows for a secret to be split and defines a threshold number of individuals required to put it back together. With this mechanism, there is no single point-of-failure. If you shared your secret with three people, then you would need two of them to confirm your identity and deliver your secret back to you. For the key recovery use case, your secret is your secondary key, which you can split into “shards” and distribute to other trusted persons using a linear key split.

You have the option to choose how many shards you want and who to give them to. It would make sense to give your shards to different types of friends and family. You wouldn’t want to give three of your four shards to your business partners, for instance, because then they may be more likely to collude against you. The more shards you make and the more trusted contacts you introduce, the more secure your recovery key is.

Christopher Allen and Mark Friedenbach, authors of a commonly cited article on the topic that was written in January of this year, suggest calling these different types of trusted contacts “circles.” They further suggest allowing the user to specify different thresholds for each circle (so you might need just one business partner, but two family members to put together your recovery key), and “then also specify which circle thresholds are required in disjunctive normal form, e.g. ‘Friends AND Family’ OR ‘Family AND Coworkers.’” [1]

Another level of security could be added with a further encryption of the recovery key, so that it is encrypted before it is split up. While this does introduce a single point-of-failure (should the key-owner forget her password, the key would be lost), it may be useful to introduce a secondary, shorter password for the recovery of a much longer and/or more valuable password.


Should Edgeware use Social Key Recovery?

Introducing a Social Key Recovery tool to Edgeware would ensure access to accounts, as well as security. Given the nature of the network, it may be to the benefit of the community to set the threshold of trusted contacts fairly high, perhaps splitting the key into five pieces and requiring four friends to put it together again. It would also be beneficial to implement the secondary encryption of the recovery key (despite the single point-of-failure, the security benefit for safeguarding accounts against collusion is worth the relatively small risk). There are currently multiple on-going projects to code an implementation of this tool, including in Dart, Javascript, and Rust. Most projects are targeted towards a phone app UI, but should still be fairly easy and inexpensive to implement.
