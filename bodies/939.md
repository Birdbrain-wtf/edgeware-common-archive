So this has had about 5 minutes thought but I was inspired by a scene I saw in 'Les Miserables' - recent film, not the musical. 

In it, women are collecting  and managing money in a 'Tontine' - they throw money in  together and it goes towards big events - funerals, weddings, a big moment where one cannot afford alone.

From Wikipedia: 

> A **tontine** ([/ˈtɒntaɪn, -iːn, ˌtɒnˈtiːn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English)) is an investment plan for raising capital, devised in the 17th century and relatively widespread in the 18th and 19th centuries.
> It enables subscribers to share the risk of living a long life by combining features of a group [annuity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annuity_(financial_contracts)) with a kind of mortality [lottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lottery). 
> Each subscriber pays an agreed sum into the fund and thereafter receives a periodical payout. 
> As members die, their payout entitlements devolve to the other participants, and so the value of each continuing payout increases. 
> On the death of the last member, the scheme is wound up.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tontine#cite_note-1)

Seemed to contain some interesting ideas that could have a crypto spin - namely collective funding (aka a DAO), a lottery (albeit a mortality one) which might touch on the long term value of crypto...
