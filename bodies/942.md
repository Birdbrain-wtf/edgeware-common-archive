**Some thoughts on why - and how to improve things**

As noted before, pretty much all of the issues on the governance side of the chain - around upgrades, strategy, proposals - both in the development and funding parts which seem to come down to a perceived lack of community input or discussion in most cases. 

There are a few reasons for this:

- The community is in its early stages so you can forgive certain things as comms channels are being worked out and people are finding their voice - as a result there are a lack of responses to proposals. 
- The existing social structure of the chain - its channels, working groups and discussion forums need to be simplified and people need to know when / where to post and to have their attention focused.
- There are still occasions when ideas are proposed - as if for community feedback - but then started without due time for consideration or to discuss the pros and cons.


The idea of being 'community directed' is nice to say, but harder (and slower) in practice, since you actually have to engage in debates and discussions that would otherwise be decided by a small core team / company. 

There is no silver bullet for any of this stuff but I would suggest we agree on how to best solve the comms channels and to simplify the threads and discussions to ensure people can find and discuss important posts where relevant.

**Edgeware discussion threads / groups / forum.**

The Edgeware discussion channels are a bit of a mess and this is contributing factor in the struggle to concentrate people's attention where it is needed.

Some chats are bridged, whilst others don't seem to be.

We have an increasing amount of Working Groups which again means we end up with siloed threads and it's hard to know when and where to post updates / calls for action and who is doing what work and when and how best to direct feedback.

The [nearly completed website](https://www.figma.com/file/gnjge0yVnFMlrs196SE6kf/Edgeware-Site-Dark?node-id=0:1) makes a big play to how Edgeware's social structure is one of it's key differentiators - and key to its ability to achieve any social scalability of its work-force and its ability to incubate community directed projects and DAOs.

This claim is redundant if we can't organise even the simplest stuff.
