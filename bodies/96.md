Currently, it is still possible to connect an individual’s actions within a DAO, even if all the members are anonymous. For those concerned with total privacy, this is obviously an issue. Semaphore is a solution to this problem: a language that was built to create smart contracts that allow for anonymous signaling and solve the problem of DAO privacy.

**How does Semaphore work?**

The Semaphore smart contract allows anyone to make a proposal, so long as they verify their identity as a member of the group. The smart contract requires a unique nullifier (for each proposal to have a unique identification), and an epoch to be specified (this helps avoid spam proposals). Members can use external_nullifier = hash(proposal_id) to make a Semaphore signal and vote once on each proposal. A snark is used to prove membership of a merkle tree and calculate the nullifier.

The circuit uses a burn relay to refund users the gas money it takes to participate. To create a proposal, you pay a proposal fee, which is then used to pay the gas for everyone who votes on it. They then propose using [Burn relay registry: Decentralized transaction abstraction on layer 2](https://ethresear.ch/t/burn-relay-registry-decentralized-transaction-abstraction-on-layer-2/5820) (you can see the code for this at [https://github.com/lsankar4033/micromix_relayer](https://github.com/lsankar4033/micromix_relayer)).

Although Semaphore solves the problem of action-anonymity, it still runs the risk of votes being subject to collusion. While private DAO created with Semaphore is indeed private, the author of the introduction to the language barryWhiteHat is quick to point out that the individual can still prove the content of their vote. This creates a very real potential for vote-buying or other forms of collusion.

[See the code for the Semaphore circuit here.](https://github.com/kobigurk/semaphore.)

**Why should a DAO be private?**

The Semaphore circuit has already been built, it’s just waiting for a DAO to use it. But there are definite hold-backs to creating this sort of network.

Not only does Semaphore allow for anonymous signalling for votes, but according to barryWhiteHat, it also has the potential to let a subset of a Semaphore group anonymously join a DAO. The privacy language could be used to facilitate the actions of “Dark DAOs,” which are essentially anonymous vote-buying cartels. 

While in any PoS chain, no user should theoretically have motivation to vote against their network, there are certainly cases where it might happen. What if you’re involved in multiple networks and you had an opportunity to leverage your position in one to benefit the other? If, for instance, you had a group of people with more stake in the other network that you were beholden to, you might not want anyone to link your actions and see how your behavior in one network positively affected yours (and others) status in another. Some might then see the choice of true anonymity in a DAO as a choice between the freedom of privacy, and being held to standards of acting for network utility.
